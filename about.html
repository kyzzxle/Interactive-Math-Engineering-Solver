About Solver Hub

Interactive Math & Engineering Solver Hub is a free collection of calculators that explain their answers. It exists to help students check their work, find their mistakes and understand the methods behind common problems in mathematics and engineering.

Our mission

Many online calculators return a single number. That is useful when you only need the number, but frustrating when you are learning: if your answer differs, you cannot tell whether you used the wrong formula, substituted a value incorrectly or slipped in the arithmetic. Solver Hub shows the whole route — formula, substitution, intermediate values and final answer — so that a mismatch points directly to the step where it happened.

Each calculator page also explains the method: the formula and what each variable means, the assumptions and domain restrictions, how results are rounded, and a worked example you can load into the calculator. The frustum, point-to-line distance and permutations pages go further, with derivations, common mistakes, applications and frequently asked questions.

Who builds it

Solver Hub is an independent project created and maintained by a college student who wanted the kind of study tool they wished they had: one that teaches the method and is honest about precision. It is not affiliated with any school, publisher or examination board.

Advertising on the site, if and when it is shown, helps cover running costs and supports the creator's education. Advertising never influences the mathematics or the explanations.

Principles we follow

  • Accuracy first. Every formula is checked against independent calculations by automated tests before it is published, including boundary cases and invalid inputs.
  • Honest precision. Exact results are labelled as exact. Rounded decimals are marked as approximations. Results too large to compute exactly are clearly flagged.
  • Clear error messages. Impossible inputs — such as a slant height that is too short to form a frustum — are explained instead of producing a misleading number.
  • Original explanations. Lessons are written for this site in plain language, aimed at students meeting a topic for the first time.
  • Privacy. Calculations run entirely in your browser. Your inputs are not sent to or stored on a server. See the privacy policy for details.
  • Accessibility. Pages are designed to work with keyboards and screen readers, on small and large screens, in light and dark mode.

What is covered

The hub is organised by subject — solid mensuration, analytic geometry, algebra, trigonometry, calculus, statistics and probability, combinatorics, engineering mathematics and physics. There are currently 58 calculators, at least six in every subject, and many include an interactive explorer and practice problems with hints. Each one is published only after its worked examples and edge cases have been tested, and each page states what the calculator does not handle. You can see everything that is available in the calculator directory.

Corrections and suggestions

If you find an error, an unclear explanation or a problem type you would like to see covered, please use the contact page. Reports of mathematical errors are treated as the highest priority and are corrected as quickly as possible.

Responsible use

Solver Hub is a learning aid. It is not a substitute for your own reasoning, your instructor's guidance or professional engineering judgement. Please read the disclaimer before relying on any result for design, safety or financial decisions.
