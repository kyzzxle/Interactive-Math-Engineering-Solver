What are function operations?

Functions can be combined like numbers: added, subtracted, multiplied and divided. They can also be composed, feeding the output of one into the other: \((f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))\). An inverse function \(f^{-1}\) undoes \(f\): if \(f(2) = 7\), then \(f^{-1}(7) = 2\).

Try changing the input

Swap f and g. For \(f(x) = 2x + 3\) and \(g(x) = x^2 - 1\), \(f(g(x)) = 2x^2 + 1\) but \(g(f(x)) = 4x^2 + 12x + 8\): composition is not commutative.

What this calculator does

  • Combine: for any two functions, gives \(f \circ g\), \(g \circ f\), \(f + g\), \(f - g\), \(f \cdot g\) and \(f/g\), simplified. With a value \(x_0\) it also evaluates them, inside out.
  • Inverse: for \(\tfrac{ax + b}{cx + d}\), \(a(x - h)^n + k\) and \(a \cdot b^x + k\), finds \(f^{-1}\), the domain and range of both, and checks that \(f(f^{-1}(x)) = x\).

The ideas behind it

Composition is a pipeline. In \(f(g(x))\), \(g\) acts first and \(f\) acts on its output. The domain of \(f \circ g\) contains only the inputs that \(g\) accepts and whose outputs \(f\) accepts.

An inverse swaps inputs and outputs. Every point \((a, b)\) on the graph of \(f\) becomes \((b, a)\) on the graph of \(f^{-1}\), so the two graphs are mirror images in the line \(y = x\). The domain of \(f^{-1}\) is the range of \(f\), and the other way round.

Only one-to-one functions have inverses

If two inputs give the same output, the inverse would not know which one to return. \(x^2\) is not one-to-one, but it is on the restricted domain \(x \ge 0\), whose inverse is \(\sqrt{x}\).

How to find an inverse

  1. Write \(y = f(x)\).
  2. Swap \(x\) and \(y\).
  3. Solve for \(y\). The result is \(f^{-1}(x)\).

For \(y = \tfrac{ax + b}{cx + d}\): swapping gives \(x(cy + d) = ay + b\). Collecting the \(y\)-terms, \(y(cx - a) = b - dx\), so

\[ f^{-1}(x) = \frac{-dx + b}{cx - a}. \]

This works only when \(ad - bc \ne 0\); otherwise \(f\) is a constant.

Complete worked example

Problem

For \(f(x) = 2x + 3\) and \(g(x) = x^2 - 1\), find \(f(g(x))\), \(g(f(x))\) and both at \(x = 2\).

Step 1: f(g(x))

\[ f\big(g(x)\big) = 2(x^2 - 1) + 3 = 2x^2 + 1 \]

Step 2: g(f(x))

\[ g\big(f(x)\big) = (2x + 3)^2 - 1 = 4x^2 + 12x + 8 \]

Step 3: At x = 2, inside out

\[ g(2) = 3 \Rightarrow f(3) = 9, \qquad f(2) = 7 \Rightarrow g(7) = 48 \]

Check

\(2(2)^2 + 1 = 9\) and \(4(4) + 24 + 8 = 48\). ✓

Second worked example: an inverse

Problem

Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \tfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}\), with its domain and range.

Step 1: Swap and solve

\[ x = \frac{2y + 1}{y - 3} \;\Rightarrow\; xy - 3x = 2y + 1 \;\Rightarrow\; y(x - 2) = 3x + 1 \] \[ f^{-1}(x) = \frac{3x + 1}{x - 2} \]

Step 2: Domain and range

\(f\) is undefined at \(x = 3\) and never reaches \(y = 2\). So \(f\) has domain \(x \ne 3\) and range \(y \ne 2\), and \(f^{-1}\) has domain \(x \ne 2\) and range \(y \ne 3\).

Check

\(f(4) = \tfrac{9}{1} = 9\), and \(f^{-1}(9) = \tfrac{28}{7} = 4\). ✓

Practical applications

  • Unit conversions: converting °C to °F and back are inverse functions; chaining conversions is composition.
  • Engineering models: sensor output as a function of temperature, and the inverse to read temperature from the output.
  • Finance: compound growth \(A = P(1 + r)^t\) and its inverse, the logarithm, give the time to reach a target.
  • Signal processing: a chain of filters is a composition of functions.

Common mistakes

  1. Composing in the wrong order. \(f(g(x))\) applies \(g\) first.
  2. Treating \(f^{-1}\) as a reciprocal. \(f^{-1}(x) \ne \tfrac{1}{f(x)}\).
  3. Missing brackets when substituting. \(f(g(x))\) for \(f(x) = x^2\) is \((x + 1)^2\), not \(x^2 + 1\).
  4. Forgetting to restrict the domain. \(x^2\) needs \(x \ge 0\) before it has an inverse.

Assumptions and limitations

  • In combine mode, f and g may use powers, fractions, roots and functions such as exp, ln and sin. Polynomial and rational results are expanded and reduced.
  • Inverse mode covers three families of functions; the square power uses the domain \(x \ge h\).
  • The domain of a composition is described in words; it is not computed for every expression.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find f(g(x))?

Replace every x in f with the whole expression g(x), in brackets, and simplify.

How do I find an inverse function?

Write y = f(x), swap x and y, and solve for y.

How are the graphs of f and f⁻¹ related?

They are reflections of each other in the line y = x.

Is f(g(x)) the same as g(f(x))?

Usually not. They are equal only for special pairs, such as a function and its inverse.
