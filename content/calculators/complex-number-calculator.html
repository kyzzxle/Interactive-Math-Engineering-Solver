Complex numbers

A complex number \(z = a + bi\) combines a real part \(a\) and an imaginary part \(b\), where \(i^2 = -1\). Engineers use complex numbers to represent AC voltages and currents (phasors), impedances and signals. This calculator performs exact arithmetic with complex numbers, converts between rectangular and polar forms, raises numbers to integer powers with de Moivre's theorem and plots everything on an Argand diagram.

Try changing the input

Multiply any number by \(i\): its point on the Argand diagram rotates 90° counter-clockwise, and its modulus stays the same.

Formulas

OperationFormula
Product\((a + bi)(c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i\)
Quotient\(\dfrac{a + bi}{c + di} = \dfrac{(a + bi)(c - di)}{c^2 + d^2}\)
Modulus\(|z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\)
Argument\(\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(b, a)\), in \((-\pi, \pi]\)
Polar / exponential form\(z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) = re^{i\theta}\)
De Moivre\(z^n = r^n(\cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta)\)

The conjugate \(\bar{z} = a - bi\) satisfies \(z\bar{z} = |z|^2\), which is why multiplying by the conjugate makes a denominator real.

Worked example

\(\dfrac{3 + 4i}{1 - 2i}\)

Step 1 — Multiply by the conjugate \(1 + 2i\)

\[ \frac{(3 + 4i)(1 + 2i)}{(1 - 2i)(1 + 2i)} = \frac{3 + 6i + 4i + 8i^2}{1 + 4} \]

Step 2 — Simplify with \(i^2 = -1\)

\[ = \frac{-5 + 10i}{5} = -1 + 2i \]

Input format and rounding

  • Write numbers as \(a + bi\): 3+4i, -2i, i, 5 or 1/2 - 3/4i; j may be used instead of \(i\).
  • Rectangular results are exact fractions; the modulus is exact in simplest radical form.
  • Arguments are in radians (with degrees shown); a simple multiple of \(\pi\) is written exactly. The argument of 0 is undefined.
  • Powers are limited to \(|n| \le 100\).
