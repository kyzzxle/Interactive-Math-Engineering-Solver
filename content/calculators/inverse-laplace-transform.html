What the inverse Laplace transform calculator does

The inverse Laplace transform recovers f(t) from F(s). For rational functions the method is: split F(s) into partial fractions, then recognise each piece in the transform table.

Real poles give exponentials; repeated poles add powers of t; complex poles give exponentially growing or decaying oscillations. The location of the poles therefore tells you how a system behaves.

Try it

Poles with negative real part give terms that die away — the hallmark of a stable system.

Formulas and variables

\[ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s - r)^k}\right\} = \frac{t^{k-1}e^{rt}}{(k-1)!} \]\[ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{B(s + \alpha) + C\omega}{(s + \alpha)^2 + \omega^2}\right\} = e^{-\alpha t}\left(B\cos\omega t + C\sin\omega t\right) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Real pole
\(-\alpha \pm i\omega\)Complex poles
\(k\)Multiplicity of a pole

Worked example

Invert (s + 3)/(s² + 2s + 5)

Step 1 — Complete the square

\[ s^2 + 2s + 5 = (s + 1)^2 + 4 \]

Step 2 — Split the numerator

\[ \frac{s + 3}{(s + 1)^2 + 4} = \frac{s + 1}{(s + 1)^2 + 2^2} + \frac{2}{(s + 1)^2 + 2^2} \]

Step 3 — Table

\[ f(t) = e^{-t}(\cos 2t + \sin 2t) \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Proper rational functions (numerator degree lower than the denominator).
  • Denominators with rational roots and at most one quadratic factor, degree up to 6.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to complete the square before using the sine and cosine pairs.
  • Dropping the 1/(k − 1)! for repeated poles.
