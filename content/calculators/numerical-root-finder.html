Finding roots numerically

Most equations met in engineering — \(\cos x = x\), \(x^5 - 3x + 1 = 0\), friction-factor and heat-transfer equations — have no formula for their solutions. Numerical root finders produce a sequence of approximations that closes in on a solution of \(f(x) = 0\). This calculator implements the two classic methods and shows every iteration, so you can see how — and whether — they converge.

Try changing the input

Solve \(x^2 - 2 = 0\) with both methods to the same tolerance. Bisection needs about 20 steps; Newton's method needs 5, because its number of correct digits roughly doubles each step.

Methods and convergence

Bisection
Start with \([a, b]\) where \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\) have opposite signs. Evaluate \(f\) at the midpoint \(m = \frac{a + b}{2}\) and keep the half that still contains a sign change. The error after \(k\) steps is at most \(\frac{b - a}{2^k}\): slow but guaranteed for continuous \(f\).
Newton–Raphson
\(x_{k+1} = x_k - \dfrac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}\). Very fast (quadratic convergence) near a simple root, but it needs a good starting guess and can fail: if \(f'(x_k) = 0\) the tangent is horizontal; the iterates may cycle (as for \(x^3 - 2x + 2\) from 0) or diverge.

Stopping rule: stop when successive approximations differ by less than the tolerance (and \(|f(x)|\) is small), or when the iteration limit is reached — in which case the calculator reports non-convergence rather than an unreliable number.

Worked example

Solve \(\cos x = x\) with Newton's method from \(x_0 = 1\)

Step 1 — Set up

\[ f(x) = \cos x - x, \qquad f'(x) = -\sin x - 1 \]

Step 2 — First iteration

\[ x_1 = 1 - \frac{\cos 1 - 1}{-\sin 1 - 1} \approx 1 - \frac{-0.4597}{-1.8415} \approx 0.7504 \]

Step 3 — Continue

\(x_2 \approx 0.739113\), \(x_3 \approx 0.739085\); after 5 iterations \(x \approx 0.7390851332\).

Assumptions and limits

  • \(f\) must be continuous on the bisection interval; Newton's method also needs \(f\) to be differentiable (the derivative is computed symbolically).
  • Tolerance between \(10^{-4}\) and \(10^{-10}\); at most 1000 iterations.
  • Each method finds one root. To find others, change the interval or the starting guess — the graph shows where the curve crosses the axis.
