What the inequality solver does

An inequality asks where an expression is greater or less than another. Move everything to one side, find where it equals zero (the boundary points), then check the sign in between.

For a linear inequality there is one boundary. For a quadratic there can be two, one or none; the direction the parabola opens tells you where it is positive or negative.

Try it

Dividing or multiplying both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

Formulas and variables

\[ ax + b < 0 \qquad ax^2 + bx + c < 0 \]\[ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a,\ b,\ c\)Coefficients after moving everything to one side
\(\lt,\ \le,\ \gt,\ \ge\)Strict (open dot) or inclusive (filled dot) inequalities

An upward parabola (a > 0) is negative between its roots and positive outside them.

Worked example

Solve x² − 5x < −6

Step 1 — One side

\[ x^2 - 5x + 6 < 0 \]

Step 2 — Boundary points

\[ (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2,\ 3 \]

Step 3 — Sign

The parabola opens upward, so it is negative between the roots:

\[ 2 < x < 3,\quad \text{i.e. } (2,\ 3) \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Linear and quadratic expressions in x only (degree ≤ 2 after moving terms).
  • Coefficients may be fractions; boundaries are exact (fractions or simplest radicals).

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to reverse the sign when dividing by a negative number.
  • Writing x < 2 and x > 3 for “outside the roots” — it must be “or”.
