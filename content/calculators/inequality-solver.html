What is an inequality?

An inequality compares two expressions with \(\lt\), \(\le\), \(\gt\) or \(\ge\). Its solution is usually a whole interval of numbers, not a single value. A linear inequality like \(-3x + 4 \ge 10\) is solved almost like an equation; a quadratic one like \(x^2 - 5x \lt -6\) is solved by finding where a parabola is above or below the x-axis.

What this calculator does

  • Solves linear and quadratic inequalities in \(x\), with both sides typed as expressions.
  • Finds exact boundary points (in simplest radical form when needed).
  • Gives the solution in inequality notation, interval notation and on a number line.
  • Checks the answer with a test point in each interval.

The idea behind it

Move everything to one side so the question becomes "where is this expression positive (or negative)?" A polynomial can only change sign where it equals 0, so its roots split the number line into intervals with a constant sign in each.

For a quadratic \(ax^2 + bx + c\) with two roots:

  • if \(a \gt 0\) (opens upward), it is negative between the roots and positive outside;
  • if \(a \lt 0\) (opens downward), the opposite.

Reversing the sign

Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number flips the inequality: \(2 \lt 3\), but \(-2 \gt -3\).

Formula breakdown

Inequality (\(a \gt 0\), roots \(r_1 \lt r_2\))Solution
\(a(x - r_1)(x - r_2) \lt 0\)\(r_1 \lt x \lt r_2\)
\(a(x - r_1)(x - r_2) \gt 0\)\(x \lt r_1\) or \(x \gt r_2\)
No real roots, \(\gt 0\)All real numbers
No real roots, \(\lt 0\)No solution

Use square brackets for \(\le\) and \(\ge\) (boundary included) and round brackets for \(\lt\) and \(\gt\).

The sign-chart method

  1. Move every term to one side: \(f(x) \lt 0\) (or another sign).
  2. Find the roots of \(f(x) = 0\); they are the boundary points.
  3. Pick a test point in each interval and find the sign of \(f\) there.
  4. Keep the intervals with the required sign, and include the boundaries for \(\le\) or \(\ge\).

This works because a polynomial is continuous: it cannot change sign without passing through 0.

Complete worked example

Problem

Solve \(x^2 - 5x \lt -6\).

Step 1: One side

\[ x^2 - 5x + 6 \lt 0 \]

Step 2: Boundary points

\[ (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2,\ x = 3 \]

Step 3: Sign

The parabola opens upward, so it is negative between the roots.

\[ 2 \lt x \lt 3, \qquad (2, 3) \]

Check

\(x = 2.5\): \(6.25 - 12.5 + 6 = -0.25 \lt 0\) ✓. \(x = 4\): \(16 - 20 + 6 = 2\), not negative ✓.

Second worked example: a linear inequality

Problem

Solve \(-3x + 4 \ge 10\).

Step 1: Subtract 4

\[ -3x \ge 6 \]

Step 2: Divide by −3 and reverse the sign

\[ x \le -2, \qquad (-\infty, -2] \]

Check

\(x = -3\): \(9 + 4 = 13 \ge 10\) ✓. \(x = 0\): \(4 \ge 10\) is false ✓.

Practical applications

  • Design limits: stresses, deflections or temperatures that must stay below an allowable value.
  • Budgets: how many units can be bought for at most a given amount.
  • Projectiles: the time interval during which an object is above a certain height.
  • Profit: the production range where revenue exceeds cost.

Common mistakes

  1. Not reversing the sign after multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
  2. Taking square roots of both sides. \(x^2 \lt 9\) means \(-3 \lt x \lt 3\), not just \(x \lt 3\).
  3. Wrong brackets. \(\le\) includes the endpoint: use \([\ ]\).
  4. Choosing the wrong region. Always test a point.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Both sides are polynomials in \(x\) of degree at most 2.
  • Boundary points are exact; decimals are shown for irrational ones.
  • For absolute values, use the absolute value calculator.

Frequently asked questions

When do I flip the inequality sign?

When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number.

How do I solve a quadratic inequality?

Move everything to one side, find the roots, then use the direction the parabola opens (or test points) to decide which intervals work.

What is interval notation?

A way to write a set of numbers: (2, 3) means between 2 and 3, excluding both; [2, 3] includes both; ∞ always gets a round bracket.

What if the quadratic has no real roots?

It never changes sign, so the inequality is true for every x or for none, depending on which way the parabola opens.
