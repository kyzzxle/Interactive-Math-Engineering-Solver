What the effective interest rate calculator does

A bank may quote "12% compounded monthly". That 12% is the nominal rate: the rate per month (1%) multiplied by 12. Because interest is added every month and then earns interest itself, the balance actually grows by more than 12% in a year. The true yearly growth is the effective rate, here about 12.68%.

The calculator converts a nominal rate to an effective rate, an effective rate back to a nominal rate, or one compounding frequency to another with the same effective rate. A table shows how the effective rate of the same nominal rate changes with the compounding frequency.

Try it

Enter 12% and look at the table. Monthly compounding gives 12.68%, daily 12.747%, and continuous 12.750%: the effective rate approaches \(e^{r} - 1\) but never exceeds it.

Formulas and variables

\[ ER = \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{m} - 1 \qquad r = m\left[(1 + ER)^{1/m} - 1\right] \]
\[ ER_{\text{continuous}} = e^{r} - 1 \qquad r = \ln(1 + ER) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Nominal annual rate, as a decimal
\(m\)Compounding periods per year
\(ER\)Effective annual rate: the actual growth in one year

Two rates are equivalent when they have the same effective rate. That is how you compare a loan quoted quarterly with one quoted monthly.

Worked example

Which is better for a depositor: 6% compounded semi-annually or 5.95% compounded monthly?

Step 1: Effective rate of the first offer

\[ ER_1 = \left(1 + \frac{0.06}{2}\right)^{2} - 1 = 1.0609 - 1 = 6.09\% \]

Step 2: Effective rate of the second offer

\[ ER_2 = \left(1 + \frac{0.0595}{12}\right)^{12} - 1 \approx 6.1150\% \]

Step 3: Compare

The second offer has the lower nominal rate but the higher effective rate, so it earns more.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The rate is constant for the whole year and there are no fees. Real loans may add charges that raise the true cost.
  • Daily compounding uses 365 periods a year.
  • Use the effective rate, or the rate per period, in the compound interest and annuity formulas.
