What is a truncated prism?

A truncated prism is what remains when a prism is cut by a plane that is not parallel to its base. The lateral edges then have different lengths, the side faces become trapezoids, and the top is a tilted polygon. A wedge of cake cut at an angle, a slanted chimney top and a sloped roof over a rectangular room are truncated prisms.

What this calculator does

  • For a right truncated triangular prism, from the three base sides and three lateral edges.
  • For a right truncated rectangular prism, from the base and four lateral edges, checking that the top is a single plane.
  • For any truncated prism, from the area of the right section and the lateral edges.

It finds the volume, every trapezoidal face, the area of the slanted top and the total surface area, and draws the solid in 3D.

The geometry behind it

A truncated triangular prism splits into three pyramids on the same base triangle, with heights equal to the three lateral edges. Each has volume \(\tfrac{1}{3}Bh_i\), so the total is \(B\) times the average edge.

Same as a straight prism

A truncated prism has the same volume as an ordinary prism on the same base whose height is the average of the lateral edges.

Deriving the formulas

Call the base triangle \(PQR\) and the top triangle \(P'Q'R'\), with \(PP' = h_1\), \(QQ' = h_2\), \(RR' = h_3\). Cut the solid into three tetrahedra: \(P'PQR\), \(Q'P'QR\) and \(R'P'Q'R\). Each can be shown to have the same volume as a pyramid on \(PQR\) with height \(h_1\), \(h_2\) and \(h_3\) respectively, because their apexes slide parallel to the lateral edges without changing their distance from the base plane. Adding:

\[ V = \tfrac{1}{3}B h_1 + \tfrac{1}{3}B h_2 + \tfrac{1}{3}B h_3 = B\,\frac{h_1 + h_2 + h_3}{3}. \]

A rectangular base splits into two triangles; with a planar top the result is the average of the four edges.

Complete worked example

Problem

A right truncated prism stands on a triangle with sides 5, 6 and 7 cm. Its edges are 4, 6 and 9 cm. Find its volume and lateral area.

Step 1: Base area (Heron)

\[ s = 9, \qquad B = \sqrt{9(4)(3)(2)} = \sqrt{216} = 6\sqrt{6} \approx 14.697\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 2: Average edge and volume

\[ \bar{h} = \frac{4 + 6 + 9}{3} = \frac{19}{3}, \qquad V = 14.697 \times 6.3333 \approx 93.08\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 3: Lateral faces

\[ 5\cdot\frac{4 + 6}{2} = 25,\quad 6\cdot\frac{6 + 9}{2} = 45,\quad 7\cdot\frac{9 + 4}{2} = 45.5,\quad L = 115.5\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Second worked example: a sloped rectangular roof space

Problem

A room is 8 m × 5 m. Its walls are 3, 5, 9 and 7 m high at the four corners (in order) under a single sloping ceiling. Find the volume of the room.

Step 1: Check the ceiling is flat

\[ h_1 + h_3 = 3 + 9 = 12, \qquad h_2 + h_4 = 5 + 7 = 12\ \checkmark \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = (8 \times 5)\,\frac{3 + 5 + 9 + 7}{4} = 40 \times 6 = 240\ \text{m}^3 \]

Practical applications

  • Buildings. Rooms and sheds with sloping roofs.
  • Earthworks. Volumes of fill on a sloping site, using corner depths.
  • Manufacturing. Bars and posts cut at an angle.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the longest or shortest edge as the height. Use the average.
  2. Corner heights in the wrong order. Opposite corners are \(h_1, h_3\) and \(h_2, h_4\).
  3. Inconsistent corners. Four heights that do not satisfy \(h_1 + h_3 = h_2 + h_4\) do not form one flat top.

Assumptions and limitations

  • For the triangular and rectangular options the prism is right: its lateral edges are perpendicular to the base.
  • For an oblique truncated prism, or one with both ends slanted, use the right-section option with the full lateral edge lengths. A solid whose two ends are parallel polygons of different shapes is a prismatoid.
  • An uncut prism is the special case with all edges equal; see the prism calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a truncated triangular prism?

The base area times the average of the three lateral edges: V = B(h₁ + h₂ + h₃)/3.

What is the right section of a prism?

The cross-section perpendicular to the lateral edges. For a right prism it is the same as the base.

Why must h₁ + h₃ = h₂ + h₄ for a rectangular base?

A plane over a rectangle rises by the same amount along opposite sides, so the diagonally opposite corner heights must have equal sums.
