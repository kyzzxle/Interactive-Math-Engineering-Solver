What is a prism?

A prism is a solid with two identical, parallel faces called bases, joined by flat side faces. In a right prism the side faces are rectangles that stand perpendicular to the bases. The prism is named after the shape of its base: a box is a rectangular prism, a cube is a square prism with equal edges, a Toblerone box is a triangular prism, and a pencil is close to a hexagonal prism.

The distance between the two bases is the height \(h\) (sometimes called the length, when the prism lies on its side).

The geometry behind it

Every slice of a prism parallel to its base is an exact copy of the base. That is why one rule gives the volume of every prism, whatever the base shape: volume = base area × height.

For the surface, unfold the prism into a net: two bases and a row of rectangles. The rectangles all have height \(h\), and their widths are the edges of the base, so together they form one long rectangle of width equal to the base perimeter \(P\). Hence the lateral area is \(Ph\).

Find the base first

A prism problem is really a plane-geometry problem about its base. Once you know the base area \(B\) and perimeter \(P\), everything else is one multiplication.

Deriving the formulas

Volume

Every slice parallel to the base has area \(B\), so \(V = \int_0^h B\,dy = Bh\). For a box, \(B = lw\), giving \(V = lwh\).

Area of a regular polygon base

Join the centre to every vertex. The polygon splits into \(n\) identical triangles, each with base \(a\) and height equal to the apothem \(m\):

\[ B = n \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} a m = \tfrac{1}{2}(na)\,m = \tfrac{1}{2} P m, \qquad m = \frac{a}{2\tan(180^\circ / n)}. \]

The apothem comes from the right triangle with half a side \(a/2\) and the central half-angle \(180^\circ/n\).

Heron's formula

When only the three sides of a triangle are known, Heron's formula gives the area without needing a height. With the semi-perimeter \(s = \tfrac{a + b + c}{2}\), \(B = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A closed box measures 8 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm. Find its volume and the card needed to make it.

Step 1: Base area and perimeter

\[ B = 8 \times 5 = 40\ \text{cm}^2, \qquad P = 2(8 + 5) = 26\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2: Lateral area

\[ L = P h = 26 \times 3 = 78\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Total surface area

\[ T = 2(40) + 78 = 158\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Check with the box formula: \(2(8\cdot5 + 8\cdot3 + 5\cdot3) = 2(40 + 24 + 15) = 158\) ✓.

Step 4: Volume

\[ V = B h = 40 \times 3 = 120\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.12\ \text{L} \]

Second worked example: a hexagonal prism

Problem

A concrete column is a regular hexagonal prism with sides of 0.3 m and a height of 3 m. How much concrete is needed?

Step 1: Apothem

\[ m = \frac{0.3}{2\tan 30^\circ} = \frac{0.3}{2(0.57735)} \approx 0.25981\ \text{m} \]

Step 2: Base area

\[ B = \tfrac{1}{2}(6 \times 0.3)(0.25981) \approx 0.23383\ \text{m}^2 \]

Step 3: Volume

\[ V = 0.23383 \times 3 \approx 0.7015\ \text{m}^3 \]

Answer

About 0.70 m³ of concrete. (A regular hexagon's area is also \(\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2\), which gives the same result.)

Practical applications

  • Packaging and shipping. Box volume and the card or film to wrap it.
  • Construction. Concrete for beams, footings, slabs and columns; earth to fill a trench.
  • Rooms and tanks. Air volume for ventilation, water in a rectangular tank, paint for the walls (lateral area).
  • Roofs and tents. A gable roof space is a triangular prism.

Common mistakes

  1. Confusing the prism's height with a triangle's height. A triangular prism has two heights: the triangle's (for the base area) and the prism's (between the bases).
  2. Counting the bases once. A closed prism has two bases.
  3. Impossible triangles. Three sides form a triangle only if each is shorter than the other two together.
  4. Using the side length instead of the apothem for a regular polygon's area.
  5. Unit conversions. 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 1000 L.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The prism is right: its side faces are rectangles. For a prism cut by a slanted plane, use the truncated prism calculator.
  • The volume \(Bh\) also holds for an oblique prism if \(h\) is the perpendicular height, but the lateral area formula does not.
  • Regular bases have 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 sides.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the volume of any prism?

Volume = area of the base × height, V = Bh. It works for every base shape.

Is a cube a prism?

Yes: a square prism whose height equals its base edge. V = a³ and the surface is 6a².

How do I find the area of a triangular base from its sides?

Use Heron's formula: s = (a + b + c)/2 and area = √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)).

What is the lateral area of a prism?

The total area of the side faces, not counting the two bases. For a right prism it is the base perimeter times the height.

How do I find the space diagonal of a box?

Use the 3-D Pythagorean theorem: √(l² + w² + h²).
