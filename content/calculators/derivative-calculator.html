What the derivative calculator does

The derivative \(f'(x)\) measures the instantaneous rate of change of \(f\): the slope of its graph at each point. This calculator differentiates symbolically — it applies the same rules you use by hand and shows which rule handles each term — then double-checks the answer numerically. It can also find the second derivative and evaluate either derivative at a point.

Try changing the input

Compare the graphs of \(f\) and \(f'\). Wherever \(f\) has a peak or valley, the graph of \(f'\) crosses the x-axis; where \(f\) is steepest, \(f'\) reaches its largest size.

Worked example

Differentiate \(f(x) = x^3 \sin x\)

Step 1 — Identify the rule

A product of \(u = x^3\) and \(v = \sin x\), so use the product rule.

Step 2 — Differentiate each factor

\[ u' = 3x^2, \qquad v' = \cos x \]

Step 3 — Combine

\[ f'(x) = u'v + uv' = 3x^2\sin x + x^3\cos x \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • The variable is \(x\); constants \(\pi\) and \(e\) are recognised. Implicit multiplication such as 3x or x sin(x) is allowed.
  • The derivative is valid where the function is differentiable; for example \(\frac{d}{dx}|x|\) is undefined at \(x = 0\).
  • Results are exact but may be written differently from your textbook (for example, factored or not). Every answer is checked against a numerical derivative before it is shown.
  • Values at a point are rounded to 6 decimal places.
