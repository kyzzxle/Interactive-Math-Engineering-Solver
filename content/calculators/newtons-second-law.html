What the Newton's second law calculator does

Newton's second law says that the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. A bigger net force gives a bigger acceleration; a bigger mass needs a bigger force for the same acceleration. This calculator solves \(F = ma\) for any one of the three quantities and draws a free-body diagram.

The force in the law is the net (resultant) force — all the forces added together with their directions. If you push a box with 100 N and friction pushes back with 30 N, the net force is 70 N.

Try it

In the explorer, make the two pulls equal. The net force is zero, so the acceleration is zero: the cart stays still or keeps moving at a steady velocity (Newton's first law).

Formula and variables

\[ F_{\text{net}} = m\,a \qquad a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} \qquad m = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{a} \]
SymbolMeaningSI unit
\(F_{\text{net}}\)Net force (sum of all forces, with signs)newtons, N
\(m\)Masskilograms, kg
\(a\)Accelerationm/s²

One newton is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s²: \(1\ \text{N} = 1\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\). The weight of an object is the special case \(W = mg\).

Worked example

What force accelerates a 1200 kg car at 2.5 m/s²?

Step 1 — Known values

\(m = 1200\) kg, \(a = 2.5\) m/s².

Step 2 — Formula

\[ F_{\text{net}} = m\,a \]

Step 3 — Substitute and simplify

\[ F_{\text{net}} = 1200 \times 2.5 = 3000\ \text{N} \]

This is the net force. The engine must also overcome air resistance and rolling friction, so it supplies more than 3000 N.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Forces and motion act along one straight line; positive and negative signs show direction. For forces at angles, resolve them into components first.
  • The mass is constant (not a rocket burning fuel) and speeds are far below the speed of light.
  • Force and acceleration always point the same way, so a mass can only be found when they have the same sign.
  • The weight shown uses \(g = 9.81\) m/s² (Earth's surface).
