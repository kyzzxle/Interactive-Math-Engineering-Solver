Factoring polynomials

Factoring rewrites a polynomial as a product of simpler polynomials, which reveals its roots and makes expressions easier to simplify. This tool factors polynomials in \(x\) of degree 2 to 6 completely over the rational numbers, showing the greatest common factor, the rational root theorem, the division that removes each root, and a final check by multiplying back.

Try changing the input

Compare \(x^4 - 4\) and \(x^4 + 4\). The first splits as \((x^2 - 2)(x^2 + 2)\); the second has no rational roots but still factors into two quadratics, \((x^2 + 2x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 2)\).

Supported methods

  1. Greatest common factor, including powers of \(x\): \(2x^4 - 32 = 2(x^4 - 16)\).
  2. Rational root theorem: if \(\frac{p}{q}\) (in lowest terms) is a root of a polynomial with integer coefficients, then \(p\) divides the constant term and \(q\) divides the leading coefficient, and \((qx - p)\) is a factor.
  3. Synthetic division removes each factor found, leaving a polynomial of lower degree.
  4. Higher-degree factors: a remainder of degree 4 to 6 without rational roots is tested for quadratic and cubic factors with integer coefficients.

These steps cover the standard patterns automatically: difference of squares \(a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)\), perfect square trinomials \(a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2 = (a \pm b)^2\), sum and difference of cubes \(a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2)\), and trinomials \(ax^2 + bx + c\) with rational roots.

Worked example

Factor \(x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6\)

Step 1 — Candidates

Possible rational roots are the divisors of 6: \(\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6\).

Step 2 — Test

\[ f(1) = 1 - 6 + 11 - 6 = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (x - 1) \text{ is a factor} \]

Step 3 — Divide and repeat

\[ x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = (x - 1)(x^2 - 5x + 6) = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3) \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Polynomials in \(x\) only, degree 2 to 6, with rational coefficients (integers, decimals or fractions).
  • Factoring is over the rational numbers: a factor such as \(x^2 - 2\) is left as it is, even though it equals \((x - \sqrt{2})(x + \sqrt{2})\) over the reals. The tool says so when this happens.
  • Every answer is verified by multiplying the factors back out exactly; if the check ever failed, no result would be shown.
