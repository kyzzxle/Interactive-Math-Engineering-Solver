What the circular motion calculator does

An object moving in a circle at constant speed is still accelerating, because its direction keeps changing. That acceleration always points towards the centre: it is the centripetal acceleration v²/r.

Some real force must provide it — string tension for a ball on a string, friction for a car on a roundabout, gravity for a satellite. That inward net force is called the centripetal force.

Try it

In the explorer, double the speed: the acceleration arrow becomes four times as long.

Formulas and variables

\[ v = \frac{2\pi r}{T} = \omega r \qquad f = \frac{1}{T} \]\[ a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r \qquad F_c = m a_c = \frac{m v^2}{r} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Radius
\(v\)Speed
\(T,\ f\)Period and frequency
\(\omega\)Angular speed (rad/s)
\(a_c,\ F_c\)Centripetal acceleration and force

Worked example

A 0.3 kg ball whirled on a 1.2 m string at 5 m/s

Step 1 — Acceleration

\[ a_c = \frac{5^2}{1.2} \approx 20.83\ \text{m/s}^2 \]

Step 2 — Force (string tension)

\[ F_c = 0.3 \times 20.83 = 6.25\ \text{N} \]

Step 3 — Period

\[ T = \frac{2\pi(1.2)}{5} \approx 1.51\ \text{s} \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Uniform circular motion (constant speed). The speed may be given as v, as a period or in rpm.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking there is no acceleration because the speed is constant.
  • Treating centripetal force as an extra force on a free-body diagram.
