What the trig graph explorer does

Every sine or cosine wave can be written as \(y = A\sin(B(x - C)) + D\). Each letter does one job: \(A\) stretches the wave vertically, \(B\) squeezes it horizontally, \(C\) slides it left or right, and \(D\) moves it up or down.

Reading these off the equation tells you the amplitude, period, phase shift and midline without plotting a single point.

Try it

Set C = −π/2 on a sine graph: it lands exactly on the cosine graph, because cos x = sin(x + π/2).

Formulas and variables

\[ y = A\sin\big(B(x - C)\big) + D \]\[ \text{amplitude } |A| \qquad \text{period } \frac{2\pi}{|B|}\ \left(\frac{\pi}{|B|}\text{ for tan}\right) \qquad \text{range } [D - |A|,\ D + |A|] \]
SymbolMeaning
\(A\)Amplitude (a negative A also reflects the graph)
\(B\)Frequency factor
\(C\)Phase (horizontal) shift
\(D\)Vertical shift; y = D is the midline

Worked example

y = 3 sin(2(x − π/4)) + 1

Step 1 — Amplitude and midline

\[ |A| = 3,\quad y = 1 \]

Step 2 — Period

\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi \]

Step 3 — Shift and range

Shifted \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) to the right; the range is \([1 - 3,\ 1 + 3] = [-2,\ 4]\).

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • x is measured in radians. B cannot be 0.
  • tan has no amplitude; its vertical asymptotes are shown instead.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the phase shift of sin(2x − π/2) as π/2: factor B out first, giving 2(x − π/4).
  • Computing the period as 2πB instead of 2π/B.
