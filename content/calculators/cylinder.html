What is a cylinder?

A right circular cylinder is the solid formed by two identical, parallel circles (the bases) joined by a curved surface that meets them at right angles. It is the shape of a can, a pipe, a water tank, a column or a roll of coins.

Its size is fixed by two numbers: the radius \(r\) of the bases and the height \(h\) between them. A cylinder is a prism with a circular base, so the same rule applies: volume = base area × height.

What this calculator does

Enter the radius (or the diameter) and the height. The calculator finds:

  • the area of each circular base,
  • the lateral (curved) surface area,
  • the total surface area, and the area of an open-topped cylinder,
  • the volume, and the capacity in litres when you work in centimetres.

For whole-number or short-decimal inputs it also gives exact answers in terms of π.

The geometry behind it

Think of a cylinder as a stack of identical coins. Each coin is a circle of area \(\pi r^2\); stacking them to height \(h\) gives a volume of \(\pi r^2 h\).

For the surface, cut the curved side straight down and unroll it. It flattens into a rectangle: one side is the height \(h\), the other is the length of the circle it wrapped around, the circumference \(2\pi r\). So the curved area is \(2\pi r h\).

Closed, open or a pipe?

A closed can has two bases, an open tank has one, and a pipe has none. Decide which surfaces really exist before adding areas.

Deriving the formulas

Volume

Every horizontal slice is the same circle, so integrating the slice area over the height gives

\[ V = \int_0^h \pi r^2\,dy = \pi r^2 h. \]

Lateral area

Unrolled, the side is a rectangle \(2\pi r\) by \(h\):

\[ LSA = (2\pi r)(h) = 2\pi r h. \]

Total area

\[ TSA = 2(\pi r^2) + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r(r + h). \]

Factoring out \(2\pi r\) makes the calculation quicker by hand.

Complete worked example

Problem

A closed can has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm. Find the metal needed and the volume.

Step 1: Base area

\[ B = \pi(4)^2 = 16\pi \approx 50.27\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 2: Lateral area

\[ LSA = 2\pi(4)(10) = 80\pi \approx 251.33\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Total surface area

\[ TSA = 2(16\pi) + 80\pi = 112\pi \approx 351.86\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 4: Volume

\[ V = \pi(4)^2(10) = 160\pi \approx 502.65\ \text{cm}^3 \approx 0.503\ \text{L} \]

Answer

The can uses \(112\pi \approx 351.86\) cm² of metal and holds \(160\pi \approx 502.65\) cm³, about half a litre.

Second worked example: from the diameter

Problem

A water tank has a diameter of 1.8 m and a height of 2.5 m, with an open top. How many litres does it hold, and how much sheet metal does it use?

Step 1: Radius

\[ r = \frac{1.8}{2} = 0.9\ \text{m} \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \pi(0.9)^2(2.5) = \pi(0.81)(2.5) = 2.025\pi \approx 6.362\ \text{m}^3 \approx 6362\ \text{L} \]

Step 3: Sheet metal (one base plus side)

\[ \pi(0.9)^2 + 2\pi(0.9)(2.5) = 0.81\pi + 4.5\pi = 5.31\pi \approx 16.68\ \text{m}^2 \]

Answer

About 6360 litres and 16.7 m² of sheet metal.

Practical applications

  • Tanks and pipes. Water, fuel and grain storage; the volume of water in a length of pipe.
  • Concrete columns. The volume of concrete for a round column or a bored pile.
  • Packaging. The metal for a can (surface area) and its capacity (volume).
  • Engines. The displacement of a cylinder is \(\pi r^2\) times the piston stroke.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the diameter as the radius. Halve it first, or the volume comes out four times too large.
  2. Counting the wrong number of bases. Closed: two; open: one; pipe: none.
  3. Writing the lateral area as πr²h. That is the volume. The side is \(2\pi r h\).
  4. Mixing units. A diameter in millimetres with a height in metres gives nonsense. Convert first.
  5. Litres. 1 m³ = 1000 L, and 1000 cm³ = 1 L.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The cylinder is right: its side is perpendicular to its bases. For a leaning cylinder, see the oblique solids calculator (the volume is the same for the same perpendicular height).
  • Wall thickness is ignored. For a pipe with a hole, use the pipe option of the composite solid calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the volume of a cylinder?

V = πr²h: the area of the circular base times the height.

What is the difference between lateral and total surface area?

The lateral area is the curved side only, 2πrh. The total surface area adds the two circular ends: 2πr² + 2πrh.

How do I find the height if I know the volume?

Rearrange V = πr²h to h = V ÷ (πr²).

How many litres are in a cylinder?

Compute the volume in cm³ and divide by 1000, or in m³ and multiply by 1000.

Does doubling the radius double the volume?

No. The volume depends on r², so doubling the radius multiplies the volume by 4. Doubling the height only doubles it.
