What the cylinder calculator does

A right circular cylinder has two equal, parallel circular bases joined by a curved side that meets them at right angles — the shape of a can, pipe or tank. Enter the radius (or diameter) and the height to get the volume, the curved (lateral) area, the area of the bases and the total surface area, with exact multiples of \(\pi\) wherever possible.

Try changing the input

Doubling the height doubles both the volume and the curved area. Doubling the radius multiplies the volume by 4 because the radius is squared.

Formulas and variables

\[ LSA = 2\pi r h \qquad TSA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r(r + h) \qquad V = \pi r^2 h \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Radius of each circular base (half the diameter).
\(h\)Height: perpendicular distance between the bases.
\(LSA\)Lateral (curved) surface area.
\(TSA\)Total surface area: curved side plus both bases.
\(V\)Volume: base area \(\pi r^2\) times height.

Why \(2\pi r h\)? Cut the curved side along a vertical line and flatten it: you get a rectangle whose width is the circumference \(2\pi r\) and whose height is \(h\).

Worked example

A tank with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm

Step 1 — Known values

\(r = 4\) cm, \(h = 10\) cm.

Step 2 — Curved area

\[ LSA = 2\pi(4)(10) = 80\pi \approx 251.33 \text{ cm}^2 \]

Step 3 — Total surface area

\[ TSA = 2\pi(4)^2 + 80\pi = 32\pi + 80\pi = 112\pi \approx 351.86 \text{ cm}^2 \]

Step 4 — Volume

\[ V = \pi(4)^2(10) = 160\pi \approx 502.65 \text{ cm}^3 \approx 0.503 \text{ L} \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • The cylinder is a right circular cylinder. An oblique cylinder has the same volume \(\pi r^2 h\) (with \(h\) measured perpendicularly) but a different lateral area.
  • Radius and height must be positive numbers.
  • An open container (such as a cup) uses \(LSA + \pi r^2\) rather than the total surface area.
  • Exact answers are shown as multiples of \(\pi\); decimals are rounded to 6 places.
