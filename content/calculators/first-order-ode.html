What the first-order differential equation solver does

A first-order differential equation relates a function y(x) to its derivative. With a starting value y(x₀) = y₀ it usually has exactly one solution.

Four common forms are solved here: direct integration, exponential growth and decay, linear equations with constant coefficients (like Newton’s law of cooling), and separable equations dy/dx = f(x)·y. A slope field shows the direction the solution must follow at every point.

Try it

Solutions never cross in a slope field: each starting point gives its own curve.

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = ky \Rightarrow y = y_0 e^{k(x - x_0)} \]\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = ay + b \Rightarrow y = \left(y_0 + \frac{b}{a}\right)e^{a(x - x_0)} - \frac{b}{a} \]\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)\,y \Rightarrow y = y_0\,e^{F(x) - F(x_0)},\ F' = f \]
SymbolMeaning
\(y(x_0) = y_0\)Initial condition
\(k,\ a,\ b\)Constants
\(F\)An antiderivative of f

Each answer is checked against a Runge–Kutta (RK4) numerical solution.

Worked example

Cooling: dy/dt = −0.2y + 4 with y(0) = 90

Step 1 — Equilibrium

\[ y^* = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{4}{-0.2} = 20 \]

Step 2 — Solution

\[ y = 20 + (90 - 20)e^{-0.2t} = 20 + 70e^{-0.2t} \]

Step 3 — At t = 10

\[ y(10) = 20 + 70e^{-2} \approx 29.47 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Four forms only. f(x) must have an antiderivative in the supported table.
  • The separable form assumes y stays non-zero.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the constant of integration before applying the initial condition.
  • Treating dy/dx = ay + b as pure exponential growth (the equilibrium shift matters).
