Circular permutations

Arranging objects around a circle differs from arranging them in a row: rotating everyone one place gives the same circular arrangement, because what matters is who sits next to whom. Sometimes even turning the circle over (taking its mirror image) gives the same arrangement — a necklace or key ring can be flipped, but people at a table cannot. This calculator handles both situations and the case where only some of the objects are placed.

Try changing the input

Switch "mirror images the same" on and off for 8 objects: the count halves, from 5040 to 2520.

Formulas

SituationNumber of arrangements
\(n\) distinct objects in a circle\((n - 1)!\)
Mirror images identical (\(n \ge 3\))\(\frac{(n - 1)!}{2}\)
\(r\) of \(n\) objects in a circle\(\frac{P(n, r)}{r} = \frac{n!}{r\,(n - r)!}\)

The \((n - 1)!\) comes from fixing one object to remove the rotations; the others then form an ordinary row of \(n - 1\) objects.

Worked example

Seating 6 people at a round table

Step 1 — Fix one person

Seat one person anywhere; every seating can be rotated so that this person is in that seat.

Step 2 — Arrange the rest

\[ (6 - 1)! = 5! = 120 \]

Compare with 720 ways to line the same 6 people up in a row: each circular seating corresponds to 6 rotations.

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • All objects are distinct. Seats are not labelled (only relative positions matter); if the seats are numbered, use ordinary permutations instead.
  • \(n\) can be up to 20,000; \(r\) defaults to \(n\) when left blank.
  • Results are exact whole numbers.
