What the Laplace transform calculator does

The Laplace transform turns a function of time \(f(t)\) into a function of a new variable \(s\). It changes differential equations into algebra, which is why engineers use it for circuits, control systems and vibrations.

Most transforms are built from a short table plus two rules: linearity (transform term by term) and the first shifting theorem (an extra factor e^{at} replaces s by s − a).

Try it

Compare L{cos 2t} with L{e^{−t} cos 2t}: the second is the first with s replaced by s + 1.

Formulas and variables

\[ F(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st} f(t)\,dt \]\[ \mathcal{L}\{t^n\} = \frac{n!}{s^{n+1}} \quad \mathcal{L}\{\sin bt\} = \frac{b}{s^2 + b^2} \quad \mathcal{L}\{\cos bt\} = \frac{s}{s^2 + b^2} \quad \mathcal{L}\{e^{at}g(t)\} = G(s - a) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(t\)Time (t ≥ 0)
\(s\)Transform variable
\(a,\ b\)Exponential rate and angular frequency

Worked example

Transform f(t) = 3e^{−2t} + t² − 4 sin 5t

Step 1 — Term by term

\[ \mathcal{L}\{3e^{-2t}\} = \frac{3}{s + 2},\quad \mathcal{L}\{t^2\} = \frac{2}{s^3},\quad \mathcal{L}\{4\sin 5t\} = \frac{20}{s^2 + 25} \]

Step 2 — Combine

\[ F(s) = \frac{3}{s + 2} + \frac{2}{s^3} - \frac{20}{s^2 + 25} \]

Step 3 — Convergence

Valid for s > 0 (the largest exponential rate is 0 here, from t² and sin 5t).

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Sums of c·tⁿ·e^(at), e^(at)·sin(bt), e^(at)·cos(bt), t·e^(at)·sin/cos(bt) and e^(at)·sinh/cosh(bt).
  • Step functions, delays and products of different trig functions are not supported.

Common mistakes

  • Transforming a product as the product of transforms (it is not).
  • Forgetting to shift every s, including in s² + b², when e^{at} is present.
