Matrix operations

Matrices organise numbers in rows and columns and are the basic language of linear algebra, structural analysis, circuits, computer graphics and data science. This calculator adds, subtracts and multiplies matrices, multiplies by a scalar, transposes, and finds determinants and inverses — all with exact fractions, so results such as \(\tfrac{1}{3}\) are never rounded.

Try changing the input

Compute AB and then BA for two 2×2 matrices: the results are usually different. Matrix multiplication is not commutative.

Rules and formulas

OperationRuleSize requirement
\(A \pm B\)\((A \pm B)_{ij} = a_{ij} \pm b_{ij}\)Same size
\(AB\)\((AB)_{ij} = \sum_k a_{ik}b_{kj}\)Columns of A = rows of B
\(A^T\)\((A^T)_{ij} = a_{ji}\)Any
\(\det A\)\(\det\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc\); cofactor expansion for 3×3Square

Worked example

Determinant of \(\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}\)

Step 1 — Expand along the first row

\[ 2\begin{vmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 4 & 5 \end{vmatrix} - (-3)\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 5 \end{vmatrix} + 1\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 \end{vmatrix} \]

Step 2 — 2×2 determinants

\[ 2(0 + 4) + 3(10 + 1) + 1(8 - 0) = 8 + 33 + 8 = 49 \]

Input format and limits

  • Type one row per line, with entries separated by spaces or commas. Integers, decimals and fractions (such as -3/4) are allowed.
  • Matrices up to 6×6. Size mismatches and singular matrices are explained rather than producing an error value.
  • All arithmetic is exact.
