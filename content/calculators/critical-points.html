Critical points and where a function increases

A critical number of \(f\) is a value \(c\) where \(f'(c) = 0\) (or \(f'\) does not exist). Local maxima and minima can only occur there. Between critical numbers the derivative keeps one sign, so \(f\) is either increasing or decreasing on each interval. This calculator finds the critical numbers of a polynomial, builds the sign chart of \(f'\) and classifies every critical point.

Try changing the input

Compare \(x^3 - 3x\) with \(x^3 + 3x\). The first has a local maximum and minimum; the second's derivative \(3x^2 + 3\) is never zero, so it increases everywhere.

Method

  1. Differentiate: find \(f'(x)\).
  2. Solve \(f'(x) = 0\) for the critical numbers.
  3. Choose a test point in each interval between them and find the sign of \(f'\).
  4. Apply the first-derivative test:
\[ f' : + \to - \;\Rightarrow\; \text{local maximum} \qquad f' : - \to + \;\Rightarrow\; \text{local minimum} \qquad \text{no sign change} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{neither} \]

Worked example

\(f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1\)

Step 1 — Derivative

\[ f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3) \]

Step 2 — Critical numbers

\(x = 1\) and \(x = 3\).

Step 3 — Sign chart

\(f'(0) = 9 > 0\), \(f'(2) = -3 < 0\), \(f'(4) = 9 > 0\).

Step 4 — Conclusion

Local maximum at \((1, 5)\), local minimum at \((3, 1)\). Increasing on \((-\infty, 1) \cup (3, \infty)\), decreasing on \((1, 3)\).

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Supported functions: polynomials of degree 2 to 6 with rational coefficients. Their derivatives exist everywhere, so the only critical numbers are the solutions of \(f'(x) = 0\).
  • Critical numbers are exact when rational or when they come from a quadratic factor; others (from a cubic or higher factor of \(f'\)) are found numerically to about 15 significant digits.
  • Values are rounded to 6 decimal places (4 in the sign chart).
