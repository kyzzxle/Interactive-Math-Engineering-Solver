What the kinematics calculator does

When an object speeds up or slows down at a constant rate, its motion along a straight line is described by five quantities: initial velocity \(u\), final velocity \(v\), acceleration \(a\), time \(t\) and displacement \(s\) — often remembered as SUVAT. Any three of them fix the other two.

Enter exactly three values and leave two blank. The calculator chooses an equation that contains only one unknown, explains why it chose it, and solves step by step. When a problem has two valid answers — a ball thrown upwards passes the same height on the way up and on the way down — both are shown.

Sign convention

Pick one direction as positive and stick to it. If up is positive, a ball's acceleration under gravity is \(a = -9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\), and a displacement below the starting point is negative.

The five SUVAT equations

\[ \begin{aligned} (1)\quad & v = u + at && \text{no } s \\ (2)\quad & s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 && \text{no } v \\ (3)\quad & v^2 = u^2 + 2as && \text{no } t \\ (4)\quad & s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)\,t && \text{no } a \\ (5)\quad & s = vt - \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 && \text{no } u \end{aligned} \]
SymbolMeaningSI unit
\(u\)Initial velocitym/s
\(v\)Final velocitym/s
\(a\)Acceleration (constant)m/s²
\(t\)Times
\(s\)Displacement (change in position)m

Each equation leaves out one quantity, so the strategy is: find the quantity you neither know nor want, and use the equation without it. On a velocity–time graph the slope is \(a\) and the area under the line is \(s\).

Worked example

A car brakes from 20 m/s at 4 m/s² until it stops

Step 1 — Known values

\(u = 20\) m/s, \(v = 0\), \(a = -4\) m/s² (negative because it slows down). Unknown: \(t\) and \(s\).

Step 2 — Time, from equation (1)

\[ t = \frac{v - u}{a} = \frac{0 - 20}{-4} = 5\ \text{s} \]

Step 3 — Stopping distance, from equation (3)

\[ s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = \frac{0 - 400}{-8} = 50\ \text{m} \]

Check with equation (4): \(s = \tfrac{1}{2}(20 + 0)(5) = 50\) m.

Assumptions, domain and limitations

  • The acceleration is constant for the whole interval. Real motion with changing acceleration needs calculus or numerical methods.
  • Motion is along one straight line. For two-dimensional motion such as a thrown ball, treat horizontal and vertical motion separately, as the projectile motion simulator does.
  • Time must be positive. If the values imply a negative time or a square root of a negative number, the calculator explains why no solution exists.
  • Displacement is not the same as distance travelled: if the velocity changes sign during the motion, the distance is larger.
