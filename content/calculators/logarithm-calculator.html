What is a logarithm?

A logarithm answers the question "to what power must the base be raised to give this number?" Because \(2^5 = 32\), \(\log_2 32 = 5\). Logarithms are the inverse of exponential functions, which makes them the tool for solving equations where the unknown is in an exponent.

Try changing the input

Try \(\log_{10} 1000\) and then \(\log_{10} 50\). The first is exactly 3; the second lies between 1 and 2, because 50 is between \(10^1\) and \(10^2\).

What this calculator does

  • Evaluate \(\log_b x\) for any base, exactly when \(x\) is a power of the base, otherwise with the change-of-base formula.
  • Solve \(\log_b(ax + c) = d\), with the domain check.
  • Solve exponential equations \(b^{ax + c} = k\).

Each answer is checked by raising the base to the result.

The idea behind it

The definition is a two-way translation:

\[ \log_b x = y \iff b^y = x \qquad (b \gt 0,\ b \ne 1,\ x \gt 0). \]

Since \(b^y\) is always positive, the logarithm of 0 or a negative number is not defined. The graph of \(y = \log_b x\) is the graph of \(y = b^x\) reflected in the line \(y = x\); every logarithm graph passes through \((1, 0)\) and \((b, 1)\).

Common and natural logarithms

\(\log x\) usually means base 10 and \(\ln x\) means base \(e \approx 2.71828\). Any other base can be computed from either one.

Formula breakdown

RuleFormula
Product\(\log_b(MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N\)
Quotient\(\log_b \tfrac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N\)
Power\(\log_b M^p = p\log_b M\)
Change of base\(\log_b x = \tfrac{\ln x}{\ln b} = \tfrac{\log x}{\log b}\)
Special values\(\log_b 1 = 0,\ \log_b b = 1,\ \log_b b^k = k\)

Variables explained

SymbolMeaning
\(b\)The base: positive and not 1.

\(d, k\)The right side of the log or exponential equation.

Deriving the change-of-base formula

Let \(y = \log_b x\), so \(b^y = x\). Take the natural logarithm of both sides and use the power rule:

\[ y \ln b = \ln x \;\Rightarrow\; y = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b}. \]

Solving exponential equations

For \(b^{ax + c} = k\), take \(\log_b\) of both sides: \(ax + c = \log_b k\), then solve the linear equation for \(x\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Solve \(\log_3(2x + 1) = 4\).

Step 1: Domain

The argument must be positive: \(2x + 1 \gt 0\), so \(x \gt -\tfrac{1}{2}\).

Step 2: Exponential form

\[ 2x + 1 = 3^4 = 81 \]

Step 3: Solve

\[ 2x = 80 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 40 \]

Check

\(2(40) + 1 = 81 \gt 0\) and \(\log_3 81 = 4\) because \(3^4 = 81\). ✓

Second worked example: an exponential equation

Problem

Solve \(5^{x - 1} = 200\).

Step 1: Take log base 5 of both sides

\[ x - 1 = \log_5 200 \]

Step 2: Change of base

\[ \log_5 200 = \frac{\ln 200}{\ln 5} \approx \frac{5.298317}{1.609438} \approx 3.292030 \]

Step 3: Solve

\[ x \approx 4.292030 \]

Check

\(5^{3.292030} \approx 200\). ✓

Practical applications

  • Decibels: sound level \(10\log_{10}\tfrac{I}{I_0}\); a tenfold increase in intensity adds 10 dB.
  • pH: \(\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]\).
  • Earthquakes: magnitude scales are logarithmic.
  • Finance: the time to double money at interest rate \(r\) is \(\tfrac{\ln 2}{\ln(1 + r)}\).
  • Radioactive decay and cooling: solving for time in exponential models.

Common mistakes

  1. Splitting a log of a sum. \(\log(M + N) \ne \log M + \log N\).
  2. Ignoring the domain. A solution that makes the argument 0 or negative must be rejected.
  3. Dividing the arguments. \(\tfrac{\ln 200}{\ln 5}\) is not \(\ln 40\).
  4. Using the wrong base on a calculator. "log" is base 10, "ln" is base \(e\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The base is positive and not 1; type e for the natural logarithm.
  • The argument of a logarithm must be positive; solutions are checked against this.
  • Decimal results are rounded to 6 places; exact answers are given when the value is a rational power.

Frequently asked questions

What is a logarithm in simple terms?

The exponent you need: log_b x is the power you raise b to in order to get x.

How do I calculate a log with a different base?

Use the change-of-base formula: log_b x = ln x / ln b.

Why can't I take the log of a negative number?

A positive base raised to any real power is positive, so no real exponent gives a negative number or 0.

How do I solve an equation with x in the exponent?

Isolate the power, take a logarithm of both sides, and solve the resulting linear equation.
