Logarithms

A logarithm answers the question "what power?": \(\log_b x\) is the exponent you put on the base \(b\) to get \(x\). This calculator evaluates logarithms in any valid base — exactly when the answer is a fraction such as \(\log_4 8 = \tfrac{3}{2}\) — and solves two common equation types: logarithmic equations \(\log_b(ax + c) = d\) and exponential equations \(b^{ax + c} = k\).

Try changing the input

Evaluate \(\log_2 x\) for \(x = 2, 4, 8, 16\): each doubling of \(x\) adds exactly 1 to the logarithm. Logarithms turn multiplication into addition.

Formulas and rules

\[ \log_b x = y \iff b^y = x \qquad \log_b x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b} \]
\[ \log_b(MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N \qquad \log_b\frac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N \qquad \log_b(M^k) = k\log_b M \]
SymbolMeaning
\(b\)Base: positive and not equal to 1. Base 10 is the common log; base \(e \approx 2.71828\) is the natural log \(\ln\).
\(x\)Argument: must be positive.

Worked example

Solve \(5^{x - 1} = 200\)

Step 1 — Take \(\log_5\) of both sides

\[ x - 1 = \log_5 200 \]

Step 2 — Change of base

\[ \log_5 200 = \frac{\ln 200}{\ln 5} \approx \frac{5.298317}{1.609438} \approx 3.292030 \]

Step 3 — Solve

\[ x \approx 3.292030 + 1 = 4.292030 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Only real logarithms are used: the base must be positive and not 1, and every logarithm's argument must be positive.
  • An exponential equation \(b^{ax + c} = k\) has a solution only when \(k > 0\).
  • Supported equation types are exactly the two listed above; other forms (such as sums of logarithms) are not solved here.
  • Type e as the base for natural logarithms. Results are rounded to 6 decimal places unless exact.
