The law of sines

In any triangle, each side divided by the sine of its opposite angle gives the same number. The law of sines uses that fact to solve triangles that are not right-angled, whenever you know a side together with the angle opposite it. Enter any three values (at least one side) and leave the rest blank.

Try changing the input

In the two-triangle example, shorten side a. At some point the second triangle disappears, and then the first one too — the three outcomes of the ambiguous case.

Formula and cases

\[ \frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \qquad A + B + C = 180^\circ \]
GivenResult
AAS or ASA (two angles, one side)Exactly one triangle: find the third angle, then each side.
SSA (two sides, angle opposite one of them)The ambiguous case: 0, 1 or 2 triangles.
SAS or SSSUse the law of cosines instead.

The ambiguity comes from \(\sin\theta = \sin(180^\circ - \theta)\): an acute angle and an obtuse angle can have the same sine, so both may fit.

Worked example

\(A = 40^\circ\), \(B = 60^\circ\), \(a = 8\)

Step 1 — Third angle

\[ C = 180^\circ - 40^\circ - 60^\circ = 80^\circ \]

Step 2 — Common ratio

\[ \frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{8}{\sin 40^\circ} \approx 12.4458 \]

Step 3 — Remaining sides

\[ b \approx 12.4458 \sin 60^\circ \approx 10.778, \qquad c \approx 12.4458\sin 80^\circ \approx 12.257 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Enter exactly three values with at least one side; angles are in degrees and must be between 0° and 180°.
  • Two given angles must add up to less than 180°.
  • For SSA inputs every possible triangle is reported, or a clear statement that none exists.
  • Results are rounded to 6 decimal places; diagrams are drawn to scale.
