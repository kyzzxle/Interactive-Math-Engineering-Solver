What the pyramid calculator does

A pyramid has a polygon base and triangular faces that meet at a single point, the apex. In a right pyramid the apex sits directly above the centre of the base. Enter the base dimensions and the perpendicular height; the calculator finds the slant height of the faces, the lateral and total surface areas, the length of a lateral edge and the volume.

Try changing the input

Make the pyramid taller with the same base: the volume rises in proportion to the height, while the slant height — and so the lateral area — grows more gradually.

Formulas and variables

\[ V = \frac{1}{3} B h \qquad s = \sqrt{h^2 + m^2} \qquad L = \frac{1}{2} P s \qquad T = B + L \]
SymbolMeaning
\(B, P\)Area and perimeter of the base.
\(h\)Perpendicular height from the apex to the base.
\(m\)Apothem of a regular base: distance from its centre to the middle of an edge.
\(s\)Slant height: height of each triangular face.

For a rectangular base \(l \times w\) the faces have two different slant heights, \(s_l = \sqrt{h^2 + (w/2)^2}\) and \(s_w = \sqrt{h^2 + (l/2)^2}\), and the lateral area is \(L = l s_l + w s_w\).

Worked example

A square pyramid with base edge 6 m and height 4 m

Step 1 — Base area and apothem

\[ B = 6^2 = 36 \text{ m}^2, \qquad m = \frac{6}{2} = 3 \text{ m} \]

Step 2 — Slant height

\[ s = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5 \text{ m} \]

Step 3 — Surface areas

\[ L = \frac{1}{2}(24)(5) = 60 \text{ m}^2, \qquad T = 36 + 60 = 96 \text{ m}^2 \]

Step 4 — Volume

\[ V = \frac{1}{3}(36)(4) = 48 \text{ m}^3 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Right pyramids only: regular polygon bases (3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 sides) or rectangular bases, with the apex over the centre.
  • The volume formula \(\tfrac{1}{3}Bh\) also holds for oblique pyramids, but their lateral areas are different and are not calculated here.
  • All lengths must be positive. Results are rounded to 6 decimal places.
