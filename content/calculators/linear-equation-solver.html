Solving linear equations

A linear equation in one variable can be rearranged into the form \(ax + b = 0\) with \(a \ne 0\); its graph on each side is a straight line. Type the equation exactly as it appears in your problem — with brackets, fractions and x on both sides — and the solver expands each side, collects like terms, isolates \(x\) and checks the answer by substitution. It also recognises the two special cases: equations with no solution and identities that are true for every \(x\).

Try changing the input

Change \(2x + 5\) on the right of \(2(x + 1) = 2x + 5\) to \(2x + 2\): the contradiction becomes an identity, and every real number becomes a solution.

Method and rules

\[ ax + b = cx + d \;\Rightarrow\; (a - c)x = d - b \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{d - b}{a - c} \quad (a \ne c) \]
  1. Expand brackets using the distributive law \(k(p + q) = kp + kq\).
  2. Collect like terms on each side.
  3. Move x-terms to one side and constants to the other by adding or subtracting the same amount on both sides.
  4. Divide by the coefficient of \(x\).
  5. Check by substituting the answer into the original equation.

If the x-terms cancel (\(a = c\)), the equation is either always true (\(d = b\)) or never true (\(d \ne b\)).

Worked example

Solve \(2(x - 3) + 4 = 5x - 11\)

Step 1 — Expand and simplify

\[ 2x - 6 + 4 = 5x - 11 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x - 2 = 5x - 11 \]

Step 2 — Collect terms

\[ 2x - 5x = -11 + 2 \;\Rightarrow\; -3x = -9 \]

Step 3 — Divide

\[ x = \frac{-9}{-3} = 3 \]

Step 4 — Check

Left: \(2(3 - 3) + 4 = 4\). Right: \(5(3) - 11 = 4\). ✓

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • The variable must be \(x\). Both sides must be polynomials; equations with \(x\) in a denominator, under a root or inside a function are not linear and are rejected.
  • If \(x^2\) or higher powers remain after simplifying, use the quadratic equation solver.
  • All arithmetic uses exact fractions, so answers such as \(\tfrac{5}{6}\) are never rounded in the solution.
