What are partial fractions?

Partial fraction decomposition splits one complicated fraction into a sum of simpler ones: it is adding fractions in reverse. For example \(\tfrac{3x + 5}{x^2 + x - 2} = \tfrac{8/3}{x - 1} + \tfrac{1/3}{x + 2}\). Each simple piece is easy to integrate or to invert in a Laplace transform, which is why engineers use it so often.

What this calculator does

  • Divides first when the fraction is improper.
  • Factors the denominator into linear factors (repeated factors allowed) and at most one irreducible quadratic.
  • Writes the form of the decomposition and solves exactly for every coefficient.
  • Checks the decomposition at sample points and graphs the function with its asymptotes.

The idea behind it

The denominator's factors decide the shape of the answer:

  • each distinct linear factor \((x - r)\) gives a term \(\tfrac{A}{x - r}\);
  • a repeated factor \((x - r)^m\) gives \(\tfrac{A_1}{x - r} + \tfrac{A_2}{(x - r)^2} + \dots + \tfrac{A_m}{(x - r)^m}\);
  • an irreducible quadratic factor gives \(\tfrac{Bx + C}{x^2 + px + q}\).

Multiplying through by the denominator turns the problem into a polynomial identity, which holds for every \(x\). That gives equations for the unknown numerators.

The cover-up method

For a distinct linear factor \((x - r)\), substitute \(x = r\) after multiplying through: every other term vanishes and the coefficient appears at once.

How the coefficients are found

Take \(\tfrac{3x + 5}{(x - 1)(x + 2)} = \tfrac{A}{x - 1} + \tfrac{B}{x + 2}\). Multiply both sides by \((x - 1)(x + 2)\):

\[ 3x + 5 = A(x + 2) + B(x - 1). \]

This holds for every \(x\). Choosing \(x = 1\) removes \(B\): \(8 = 3A\). Choosing \(x = -2\) removes \(A\): \(-1 = -3B\). Alternatively, compare coefficients: \(A + B = 3\) and \(2A - B = 5\). Both routes give \(A = \tfrac{8}{3}\), \(B = \tfrac{1}{3}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Decompose \(\dfrac{3x + 5}{x^2 + x - 2}\).

Step 1: Factor the denominator

\[ x^2 + x - 2 = (x - 1)(x + 2) \]

Step 2: Cover-up

\[ x = 1:\ A = \frac{3 + 5}{1 + 2} = \frac{8}{3}, \qquad x = -2:\ B = \frac{-6 + 5}{-2 - 1} = \frac{1}{3} \]

Result

\[ \frac{3x + 5}{x^2 + x - 2} = \frac{8}{3(x - 1)} + \frac{1}{3(x + 2)} \]

Check

Put the two terms back over the common denominator:

\[ \frac{8(x + 2) + (x - 1)}{3(x - 1)(x + 2)} = \frac{9x + 15}{3(x^2 + x - 2)} = \frac{3x + 5}{x^2 + x - 2}\ \checkmark \]

Second worked example: a repeated factor

Problem

Decompose \(\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{(x - 1)^2(x + 2)}\).

Step 1: Form

\[ \frac{A}{x - 1} + \frac{B}{(x - 1)^2} + \frac{C}{x + 2} \]

Step 2: Multiply through

\[ x^2 + 1 = A(x - 1)(x + 2) + B(x + 2) + C(x - 1)^2 \]

Step 3: Find the coefficients

\(x = 1\): \(2 = 3B\), so \(B = \tfrac{2}{3}\). \(x = -2\): \(5 = 9C\), so \(C = \tfrac{5}{9}\). Comparing \(x^2\) terms: \(1 = A + C\), so \(A = \tfrac{4}{9}\).

Check

At \(x = 0\): the fraction is \(\tfrac{1}{2}\), and \(-\tfrac{4}{9} + \tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{5}{18} = \tfrac{9}{18} = \tfrac{1}{2}\). ✓

Practical applications

  • Integration: each term integrates to a logarithm, a power or an arctangent.
  • Laplace transforms: splitting a transfer function before using the inverse Laplace transform.
  • Circuit and control analysis: each term is one mode of the system's response.
  • Series: telescoping sums such as \(\sum \tfrac{1}{n(n + 1)}\).

Common mistakes

  1. Skipping the division. An improper fraction must be divided first.
  2. Missing powers of a repeated factor. \((x - 1)^2\) needs terms for both \((x - 1)\) and \((x - 1)^2\).
  3. A constant over a quadratic factor. Use \(Bx + C\), not just \(B\).
  4. Not factoring the denominator completely. The form depends on every factor.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The denominator has degree up to 6 and factors into linear factors with rational roots, plus at most one irreducible quadratic.
  • All coefficients are exact fractions.
  • The calculator names the coefficients A, B, C, … in order of the roots of the denominator, from smallest to largest, so its letters can differ from the ones in a textbook. The terms themselves are the same.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cover-up method?

For a distinct linear factor (x − r), cover it up in the original fraction and substitute x = r into what is left. The result is that factor's coefficient.

What if the numerator's degree is too high?

Divide first with polynomial long division, then decompose the proper fraction that remains.

How do I handle a repeated factor?

Include one term for each power of the factor, from 1 up to its multiplicity.

Why are partial fractions useful?

The simple terms are easy to integrate and to invert in Laplace transforms.
