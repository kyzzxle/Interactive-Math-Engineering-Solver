What the sequence calculator does

An arithmetic sequence adds the same difference d each time (3, 7, 11, …). A geometric sequence multiplies by the same ratio r each time (5, 10, 20, …).

Their sums have neat formulas. A geometric series with |r| < 1 even has a finite sum when infinitely many terms are added, because the terms shrink fast enough.

Try it

In the explorer, try r = 0.5: the running total levels off at 2a₁. Try r = −0.8: it zig-zags towards its limit.

Formulas and variables

\[ a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d \qquad S_n = \frac{n(a_1 + a_n)}{2} \]\[ a_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1} \qquad S_n = \frac{a_1(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} \qquad S_\infty = \frac{a_1}{1 - r}\ (|r| < 1) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a_1\)First term
\(d\)Common difference
\(r\)Common ratio
\(n\)Number of terms

Worked example

Sum of the first 20 terms of 3, 7, 11, …

Step 1 — Difference

\[ d = 7 - 3 = 4 \]

Step 2 — 20th term

\[ a_{20} = 3 + 19(4) = 79 \]

Step 3 — Sum

\[ S_{20} = \frac{20(3 + 79)}{2} = 820 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • n is a whole number from 1 to 100,000. Fractions are kept exact while the numbers stay manageable.
  • For r = 1 the sum is n·a₁; the infinite sum exists only when |r| < 1.

Common mistakes

  • Using n instead of n − 1 in the nth-term formula.
  • Applying S∞ when |r| ≥ 1, where the series diverges.
