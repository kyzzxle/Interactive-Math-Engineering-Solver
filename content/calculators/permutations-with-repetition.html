Arrangements with repeated items

How many different "words" can be made from the letters of MISSISSIPPI? If all 11 letters were different the answer would be \(11!\), but swapping two S's (or two I's, or the two P's) changes nothing, so most of those arrangements are duplicates. Dividing out the repeats gives the number of distinguishable permutations, also called a multinomial coefficient. Enter a word or the sizes of groups of identical items.

Formula and variables

\[ \frac{n!}{n_1!\; n_2! \cdots n_k!}, \qquad n = n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_k \]
SymbolMeaning
\(n\)Total number of items.
\(n_i\)Number of identical copies of the \(i\)-th kind of item.

Why divide? Each distinguishable arrangement appears \(n_1!\,n_2!\cdots n_k!\) times among the \(n!\) arrangements of labelled items — once for every way of reordering identical items among themselves.

Worked example

Arrangements of MISSISSIPPI

Step 1 — Count the letters

11 letters: M × 1, I × 4, S × 4, P × 2.

Step 2 — Apply the formula

\[ \frac{11!}{1!\,4!\,4!\,2!} = \frac{39\,916\,800}{1 \times 24 \times 24 \times 2} = \frac{39\,916\,800}{1152} = 34\,650 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • All \(n\) items are used in every arrangement, in a row. (For arrangements in a circle see the circular permutations calculator.)
  • Words may use letters A–Z (case is ignored); group sizes must be positive whole numbers. The total is limited to 10,000 items.
  • Results are exact; when there are at most 120 arrangements of a word, every one is listed.
