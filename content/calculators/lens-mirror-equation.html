What the lens and mirror calculator does

Lenses and curved mirrors bend or reflect light to form images. The same equation links the focal length f, the object distance dₒ and the image distance dᵢ for both, and the magnification tells you the image’s size and orientation.

A ray diagram shows why: a ray parallel to the axis passes through the focal point, and a ray through the centre of a lens goes straight on. Where the rays meet (or appear to come from) is the image.

Try it

Move the object inside the focal length of a converging lens: the image becomes virtual, upright and enlarged — a magnifying glass.

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} \]\[ m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o} = \frac{h_i}{h_o} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(f\)Focal length (+ converging, − diverging)
\(d_o,\ d_i\)Object and image distances (dᵢ > 0 real, dᵢ < 0 virtual)
\(m\)Magnification (+ upright, − inverted)

This uses the “real is positive” sign convention common in introductory physics.

Worked example

Object 30 cm from a converging lens with f = 10 cm

Step 1 — Image distance

\[ \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{30} = \frac{2}{30} \Rightarrow d_i = 15\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2 — Magnification

\[ m = -\frac{15}{30} = -0.5 \]

Step 3 — Describe

Real (on the far side of the lens), inverted and half the size of the object.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Thin lenses and spherical mirrors; rays close to the axis.
  • An object exactly at the focal point gives no image (the rays leave parallel).

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong sign for diverging lenses or convex mirrors.
  • Forgetting that a negative dᵢ means a virtual image, not an error.
