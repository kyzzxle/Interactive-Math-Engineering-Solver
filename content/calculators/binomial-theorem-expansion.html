The binomial theorem

Expanding \((a + b)^n\) by repeated multiplication quickly becomes tedious. The binomial theorem gives every term directly: the coefficients are the binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{k}\) — the numbers in row \(n\) of Pascal's triangle — while the power of \(a\) falls from \(n\) to 0 as the power of \(b\) rises from 0 to \(n\). Enter two terms such as \(2x\) and \(-3\) to get the full expansion, or ask for a single term of a large power.

Try changing the input

Change the second term from 3 to −3: the coefficients keep their sizes but alternate in sign, because odd powers of a negative number are negative.

Formula and variables

\[ (a + b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{\,n-k} b^{\,k}, \qquad T_{k+1} = \binom{n}{k} a^{\,n-k} b^{\,k} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a, b\)The two terms, each a single monomial (numbers and variables with whole-number powers).
\(n\)Whole-number power.
\(\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\)Binomial coefficient; the expansion has \(n + 1\) terms.
\(T_{k+1}\)The general term: term number \(k + 1\) uses \(b^k\).

Worked example

Expand \((2x - 3)^3\)

Step 1 — Coefficients (row 3 of Pascal's triangle)

1, 3, 3, 1.

Step 2 — Terms

\[ 1(2x)^3 + 3(2x)^2(-3) + 3(2x)(-3)^2 + 1(-3)^3 \]

Step 3 — Simplify

\[ 8x^3 - 36x^2 + 54x - 27 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Each of \(a\) and \(b\) must be a single term (no + or − inside); variables allowed are x, y, z, a, b and t.
  • The full expansion is available for \(n \le 30\); a single term can be found for \(n \le 1000\).
  • All coefficients are exact (BigInt integers or fractions).
