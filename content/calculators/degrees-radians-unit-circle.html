Degrees, radians and the unit circle

Angles can be measured in degrees (360 in a full turn) or radians (\(2\pi\) in a full turn). Radians are the natural unit in calculus and physics. This converter works in both directions — you can type radians with \(\pi\), such as 5pi/4 — and also places the angle on the unit circle, giving its quadrant, reference angle and exact sine, cosine and tangent for every multiple of 15°.

Try changing the input

Enter 30°, 150°, 210° and 330°. The reference angle stays 30°, so the values have the same size; only the signs change with the quadrant.

Formulas and key values

\[ \text{radians} = \text{degrees}\times\frac{\pi}{180} \qquad \text{degrees} = \text{radians}\times\frac{180}{\pi} \]
\(\theta\)\(0^\circ\)\(30^\circ = \frac{\pi}{6}\)\(45^\circ = \frac{\pi}{4}\)\(60^\circ = \frac{\pi}{3}\)\(90^\circ = \frac{\pi}{2}\)
\(\sin\theta\)\(0\)\(\frac{1}{2}\)\(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)\(1\)
\(\cos\theta\)\(1\)\(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)\(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(\frac{1}{2}\)\(0\)
\(\tan\theta\)\(0\)\(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\)\(1\)\(\sqrt{3}\)undefined

On the unit circle (radius 1) the point at angle \(\theta\) is \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\). The reference angle is the acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis; the quadrant decides the signs (sine is positive in quadrants I and II, cosine in I and IV, tangent in I and III).

Worked example

Convert 150° and find its trigonometric values

Step 1 — Convert

\[ 150^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{6} \approx 2.618 \text{ rad} \]

Step 2 — Quadrant and reference angle

150° lies in quadrant II; its reference angle is \(180^\circ - 150^\circ = 30^\circ\).

Step 3 — Values with signs

\[ \sin 150^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \cos 150^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad \tan 150^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Any real angle is accepted, including negative angles and angles larger than a full turn.
  • Exact values are given for multiples of 15°; other angles give rounded decimals.
  • A radian answer is shown as a multiple of \(\pi\) whenever it is a simple fraction of \(\pi\).
