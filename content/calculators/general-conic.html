What is the general equation of a conic?

Every circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola can be written as one second-degree equation

\[ Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0. \]

When \(B \ne 0\) the conic is tilted: its axes are not parallel to the coordinate axes. A rotation of axes turns it straight, and a translation (completing the square) moves its centre or vertex to the origin, leaving the familiar standard form.

What this calculator does

  • Classifies the conic with the discriminant \(B^2 - 4AC\), including degenerate cases (a point, two lines, or no graph).
  • Finds the rotation angle \(\theta\) and the rotated coefficients \(A', C', D', E'\).
  • Completes the square to reach standard form.
  • Reports the centre or vertex, \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), the eccentricity, foci, vertices, asymptotes or directrix, converted back to \(xy\)-coordinates.
  • Draws the curve with its rotated axes.

The geometry behind it

Rotating the axes does not change the curve, only the way we describe it. Some quantities stay the same in every rotated system: the invariants \(A + C\) and \(B^2 - 4AC\). That is why the discriminant can identify the conic before any rotation:

  • \(B^2 - 4AC \lt 0\): ellipse (a circle when \(B = 0\) and \(A = C\));
  • \(B^2 - 4AC = 0\): parabola;
  • \(B^2 - 4AC \gt 0\): hyperbola.

Degenerate conics

A plane can also cut a cone in a single point, one line or two lines. These degenerate cases have the same discriminant as the curve they resemble; the calculator detects them when completing the square leaves 0 on the right-hand side.

Deriving the formulas

Substitute the rotation into the equation. The coefficient of \(x'y'\) is

\[ B' = B(\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta) - 2(A - C)\sin\theta\cos\theta = B\cos 2\theta - (A - C)\sin 2\theta. \]

Setting \(B' = 0\) gives \(\tan 2\theta = \tfrac{B}{A - C}\). Collecting the other terms gives \(A'\), \(C'\), \(D'\) and \(E'\). One can check that \(A' + C' = A + C\) and \(B'^2 - 4A'C' = B^2 - 4AC\), which is why the discriminant is unchanged.

With no \(x'y'\) term, completing the square in \(x'\) and \(y'\) gives the standard form of an ellipse, hyperbola or parabola.

Complete worked example

Problem

Identify and reduce \(5x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 - 8 = 0\).

Step 1: Discriminant

\[ (-6)^2 - 4(5)(5) = 36 - 100 = -64 \lt 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{ellipse} \]

Step 2: Rotation angle

\(A = C\), so \(2\theta = 90^\circ\) and \(\theta = 45^\circ\).

Step 3: New coefficients

\[ A' = 5\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} - 6\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} + 5\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} = 2, \qquad C' = 5\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} + 6\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} + 5\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} = 8 \] \[ 2x'^2 + 8y'^2 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{x'^2}{4} + \frac{y'^2}{1} = 1 \]

Step 4: Features

\(a = 2\), \(b = 1\), \(c = \sqrt{3}\), \(e = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866\). The major axis lies along the line \(y = x\), so the vertices are \((\pm\sqrt{2}, \pm\sqrt{2})\).

Second worked example: the rectangular hyperbola xy = 4

Problem

Show that \(xy = 4\) is a hyperbola and find its vertices and foci.

Step 1: Discriminant

\[ A = C = 0,\ B = 1:\ \ 1 - 0 = 1 \gt 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{hyperbola} \]

Step 2: Rotate by 45°

\[ A' = \tfrac{1}{2},\ C' = -\tfrac{1}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{x'^2}{8} - \frac{y'^2}{8} = 1 \]

Step 3: Features

\(a = b = 2\sqrt{2}\), \(c = 4\), \(e = \sqrt{2}\). Vertices \((2, 2)\) and \((-2, -2)\); foci \((2\sqrt{2}, 2\sqrt{2})\) and \((-2\sqrt{2}, -2\sqrt{2})\); asymptotes are the coordinate axes.

Practical applications

  • Orbits: planetary and satellite orbits are conics, often written in tilted coordinates.
  • Optics and antennas: parabolic and elliptical reflectors.
  • Engineering: stress and strain ellipses, where rotating to principal axes is the same mathematics.
  • Statistics: confidence ellipses of correlated data.

Common mistakes

  1. Dividing by A − C when A = C. Then \(2\theta = 90^\circ\) directly.
  2. Forgetting to convert back. Centres and foci found in \(x'y'\)-coordinates must be rotated back to \(xy\).
  3. Using the sign of B alone. The type depends on \(B^2 - 4AC\), not on \(B\).
  4. Missing degenerate cases. \(x^2 - y^2 = 0\) has a positive discriminant but is two lines.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Coefficients are real numbers; results are given to about 6 significant figures.
  • The rotation angle is chosen between 0° and 90°.
  • For conics already in standard position, see the ellipse, hyperbola, parabola and circle calculators.

Frequently asked questions

How do I identify a conic from its general equation?

Compute B² − 4AC: negative means an ellipse (or circle), zero a parabola, positive a hyperbola, unless the conic is degenerate.

How do I find the angle of rotation?

Use tan 2θ = B/(A − C), or θ = 45° when A = C. Rotating by θ removes the xy term.

What is a degenerate conic?

A single point, a single line or a pair of lines, produced when the cutting plane passes through the cone's vertex.

Why does B² − 4AC not change when the axes rotate?

It is an invariant of the quadratic part of the equation, like the determinant of a matrix, so rotation cannot change it.
