What is a frustum of a pyramid?

A frustum of a pyramid is what remains when a pyramid is cut by a plane parallel to its base and the top piece, including the apex, is removed. It has two parallel bases that are similar polygons of different sizes, joined by trapezoidal side faces. Concrete footings, hoppers, lampshades with square sections and the lower part of a square chimney are frustums.

What this calculator does

Enter the bottom and top edges of a regular base (3 to 12 sides), or the dimensions of two similar rectangles, and the height. The calculator finds:

  • the areas of both bases,
  • the slant height of each trapezoidal face (two for rectangular bases),
  • the lateral area, the total surface area and the volume,
  • a check of the volume as the difference of two pyramids.

The geometry behind it

Extend the sloping faces upward until they meet: the frustum is a large pyramid minus a small pyramid on top. Because the two pyramids are similar, every length in the small one is the same fraction of the large one, which is what produces the frustum formula.

For the faces, cut through the middle of a side face. The right triangle there has legs \(h\) and the horizontal step between the two bases (for regular bases, the difference of the apothems \(m_1 - m_2\)), and its hypotenuse is the slant height \(s\).

The middle term matters

The volume uses \(A_1 + A_2 + \sqrt{A_1 A_2}\). The term \(\sqrt{A_1 A_2}\), the geometric mean of the bases, is what students most often forget.

Deriving the formulas

Volume as the difference of two pyramids

Let the full pyramid have height \(H\). The small pyramid on top has height \(H - h\). Similar figures have areas in the ratio of the squares of their lengths, so

\[ \frac{A_2}{A_1} = \left(\frac{H - h}{H}\right)^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H = \frac{h\sqrt{A_1}}{\sqrt{A_1} - \sqrt{A_2}}. \]

Subtracting the volumes, \(V = \tfrac{1}{3}A_1 H - \tfrac{1}{3}A_2(H - h)\). Substituting and using \(A_1\sqrt{A_1} - A_2\sqrt{A_2} = (\sqrt{A_1} - \sqrt{A_2})(A_1 + \sqrt{A_1 A_2} + A_2)\) gives

\[ V = \frac{h}{3}\left(A_1 + A_2 + \sqrt{A_1 A_2}\right). \]

Lateral area

Each side face is a trapezoid with parallel sides \(a\) and \(b\) and height \(s\), so its area is \(\tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)s\). With \(n\) faces, \(L = \tfrac{1}{2}(na + nb)s = \tfrac{1}{2}(P_1 + P_2)s\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A square frustum has a bottom edge of 10 m, a top edge of 6 m and a height of 3 m. Find its volume and surface area.

Step 1: Base areas

\[ A_1 = 10^2 = 100, \qquad A_2 = 6^2 = 36, \qquad \sqrt{A_1 A_2} = \sqrt{3600} = 60 \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \frac{3}{3}(100 + 36 + 60) = 196\ \text{m}^3 \]

Step 3: Slant height

\[ m_1 - m_2 = 5 - 3 = 2, \qquad s = \sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13} \approx 3.6056\ \text{m} \]

Step 4: Lateral and total area

\[ L = \tfrac{1}{2}(40 + 24)(3.6056) \approx 115.38\ \text{m}^2, \qquad T \approx 100 + 36 + 115.38 = 251.38\ \text{m}^2 \]

Check

Full pyramid height \(H = \tfrac{3(10)}{10 - 6} = 7.5\); \(\tfrac{1}{3}(100)(7.5) - \tfrac{1}{3}(36)(4.5) = 250 - 54 = 196\) ✓.

Second worked example: a concrete footing

Problem

A footing has a 2.4 m × 1.6 m bottom and a similar 1.2 m × 0.8 m top, 0.9 m high. How much concrete is needed?

Step 1: Base areas

\[ A_1 = 2.4 \times 1.6 = 3.84, \qquad A_2 = 1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96, \qquad \sqrt{A_1 A_2} = \sqrt{3.6864} = 1.92 \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \frac{0.9}{3}(3.84 + 0.96 + 1.92) = 0.3(6.72) = 2.016\ \text{m}^3 \]

Answer

About 2.02 m³ of concrete. The bases are similar (each top side is half the bottom), so the frustum formula applies.

Practical applications

  • Footings and pedestals. Concrete volumes for tapered foundations.
  • Hoppers and bins. Capacity of square or rectangular hoppers, and the sheet metal for their sides.
  • Earthworks and dams. Excavations with sloping sides.
  • Architecture. Tapered towers and the top of an obelisk.

Common mistakes

  1. Leaving out √(A₁A₂). This underestimates the volume.
  2. Averaging the two areas. \(\tfrac{A_1 + A_2}{2}h\) is only an approximation, and always too large.
  3. Using the slant height for the volume.
  4. Non-similar bases. If the bases have different proportions, the solid is not a frustum; use the prismatoid calculator.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The frustum is cut from a right pyramid by a plane parallel to the base, so the bases are similar, parallel and centred.
  • For rectangular bases, the calculator checks that \(L_2/L_1 = W_2/W_1\).
  • For circular bases, use the frustum of a cone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a frustum of a pyramid?

V = (h/3)(A₁ + A₂ + √(A₁A₂)), where A₁ and A₂ are the areas of the two parallel bases and h is the height.

What if the top edge is zero?

Then the solid is a full pyramid and the formula reduces to V = (1/3)A₁h.

What if both bases are the same size?

Then it is a prism: √(A₁A₂) = A₁ and V = (h/3)(3A₁) = A₁h.

How do I find the slant height of a square frustum?

s = √(h² + ((a − b)/2)²), where a and b are the bottom and top edges.

Can I use this for an earth dam or trench with different slopes?

Only if the two ends are similar. Otherwise use the prismoidal formula in the prismatoid calculator.
