What the counting probability calculator does

When every selection is equally likely, probability is counting: favourable selections ÷ all selections. Choosing n items from N without replacement, where K of the N are “successes”, the number of successes follows the hypergeometric distribution.

Combinations are used because the order of the chosen items does not matter. Card hands, lotteries, quality control and committee problems all fit this pattern.

Try it

“At least one” is usually easiest as 1 − P(none).

Formulas and variables

\[ P(X = k) = \frac{\binom{K}{k}\binom{N - K}{n - k}}{\binom{N}{n}} \]\[ E[X] = n\,\frac{K}{N} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(N\)Total number of items
\(K\)Number of successes among them
\(n\)Number chosen
\(k\)Number of successes wanted

Worked example

Probability of exactly 2 hearts in a 5-card hand

Step 1 — All hands

\[ \binom{52}{5} = 2\,598\,960 \]

Step 2 — Hands with exactly 2 hearts

\[ \binom{13}{2}\binom{39}{3} = 78 \times 9139 = 712\,842 \]

Step 3 — Divide

\[ P = \frac{712\,842}{2\,598\,960} = \frac{9139}{33\,320} \approx 0.2743 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Whole numbers with K ≤ N, n ≤ N and k ≤ n. Exact fractions while the numbers stay manageable.
  • Sampling is without replacement; with replacement use the binomial distribution.

Common mistakes

  • Using permutations when order does not matter.
  • Multiplying probabilities as if the draws were independent (they are not without replacement).
