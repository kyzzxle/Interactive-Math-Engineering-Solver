What is analytic geometry in space?

In three dimensions a point has three coordinates \((x, y, z)\). A line is fixed by a point and a direction; a plane is fixed by a point and a normal vector perpendicular to it, giving the equation \(Ax + By + Cz + D = 0\). Distances, angles and intersections all come from vectors.

What this calculator does

  • Two points: distance, midpoint, direction numbers, direction cosines and angles, and the equations of the line.
  • Three points: the plane through them, its normal, intercepts, distance from the origin, and the area of the triangle.
  • Line and plane: where the line meets the plane (or that it is parallel or lies in it) and the angle between them.
  • Two planes: the angle between them and their line of intersection, or the distance between parallel planes.

The geometry behind it

Two vector operations do almost all the work:

  • the dot product \(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = |\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta\) measures angles and tests for perpendicularity;
  • the cross product \(\vec{u}\times\vec{v}\) is perpendicular to both vectors, which gives the normal of a plane and the direction of the line where two planes meet.

Direction cosines

The cosines of the angles a line makes with the three axes always satisfy \(\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1\), because they are the components of a unit vector.

Deriving the formulas

Line meets plane

Write the line as \((x, y, z) = P + t\vec{d}\) and substitute into \(\vec{n}\cdot(x, y, z) + D = 0\):

\[ \vec{n}\cdot P + t\,(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{d}) + D = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; t = -\frac{\vec{n}\cdot P + D}{\vec{n}\cdot\vec{d}}. \]

If \(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{d} = 0\) the line is parallel to the plane: it misses it, or lies in it if \(P\) satisfies the plane.

Angle between a line and a plane

The angle between the line and the normal is \(\psi\) with \(\cos\psi = \tfrac{|\vec{n}\cdot\vec{d}|}{|\vec{n}||\vec{d}|}\). The angle with the plane itself is \(90^\circ - \psi\), whose sine is that same expression.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the distance and direction cosines from \(A(1, 2, 3)\) to \(B(4, 6, 15)\).

Step 1: Components

\[ \vec{AB} = (3, 4, 12) \]

Step 2: Distance

\[ d = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13 \]

Step 3: Direction cosines

\[ \left(\tfrac{3}{13},\ \tfrac{4}{13},\ \tfrac{12}{13}\right),\qquad \tfrac{9 + 16 + 144}{169} = 1\ \checkmark \]

Second worked example: a plane and a line

Plane through three points

Through \((1, 0, 0)\), \((0, 2, 0)\), \((0, 0, 3)\):

\[ \vec{AB} = (-1, 2, 0),\ \vec{AC} = (-1, 0, 3),\ \vec{n} = (6, 3, 2) \;\Rightarrow\; 6x + 3y + 2z - 6 = 0 \]

Line meets plane

The line through \((1, 2, 3)\) with direction \((2, -1, 1)\) meets \(x + y + z = 12\) where \((1 + 2t) + (2 - t) + (3 + t) = 12\), so \(t = 3\) and the point is \((7, -1, 6)\). The angle with the plane is \(\sin^{-1}\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}\sqrt{6}} \approx 28.13^\circ\).

Practical applications

  • Structural engineering: member lengths and directions in space frames and trusses.
  • Surveying and mining: the dip and strike of rock layers are planes.
  • Computer graphics: where a ray of light hits a surface.
  • Robotics: positions and orientations in 3D.

Common mistakes

  1. Using cosine for the line–plane angle. The formula with the normal gives the sine.
  2. Cross product order and signs. Swapping the vectors reverses the normal; the plane is the same, but sign errors in the components are common.
  3. Distance between parallel planes without first scaling both equations to the same normal.
  4. Collinear points. Three points on one line do not fix a plane.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Planes are entered as A, B, C, D for \(Ax + By + Cz + D = 0\).
  • Angles between planes are given as the acute angle.
  • For the distance from a point to a plane, see the point-to-plane distance calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the equation of a plane through three points?

Form two vectors from the points, take their cross product to get the normal (A, B, C), and use one point to find D in Ax + By + Cz + D = 0.

What are direction cosines?

The cosines of the angles a line makes with the x-, y- and z-axes. Their squares add up to 1.

How do I find the angle between two planes?

Find the angle between their normal vectors with the dot product.

How do I find where a line meets a plane?

Write the line in parametric form, substitute it into the plane, solve for the parameter t, and substitute t back.
