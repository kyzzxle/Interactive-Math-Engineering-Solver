The normal distribution

The normal distribution is the familiar bell-shaped curve that describes many measurements — heights, test scores, manufacturing errors. It is fixed by two numbers: the mean \(\mu\) (the centre) and the standard deviation \(\sigma\) (the width). This calculator finds the probability that a normal variable falls below, above or between values, and works backwards to find the value at a given percentile.

Try changing the input

Compute \(P(\mu - \sigma < X < \mu + \sigma)\) for any \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\): it is always about 0.6827. Normal probabilities depend only on how many standard deviations you are from the mean.

Formulas

\[ f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \qquad P(X < a) = \Phi\left(\frac{a - \mu}{\sigma}\right) \]
\[ P(X > a) = 1 - \Phi(z_a) \qquad P(a < X < b) = \Phi(z_b) - \Phi(z_a) \qquad x_p = \mu + \Phi^{-1}(p)\,\sigma \]

\(\Phi\) is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution — the values printed in a z-table. Here it is computed directly to about 15 significant digits.

Worked example

IQ scores: \(\mu = 100\), \(\sigma = 15\). Find \(P(X < 120)\).

Step 1 — Standardise

\[ z = \frac{120 - 100}{15} \approx 1.3333 \]

Step 2 — Look up \(\Phi(z)\)

\[ P(X < 120) = \Phi(1.3333) \approx 0.9088 \]

About 90.9% of people score below 120.

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • The variable is assumed to follow a normal distribution with the given \(\mu\) and \(\sigma > 0\).
  • For percentiles, \(p\) must be strictly between 0 and 1 (enter 0.95 for the 95th percentile).
  • Because the distribution is continuous, "<" and "≤" give the same probability.
  • Probabilities are rounded to 6 decimal places; very small tail probabilities are shown in scientific notation.
