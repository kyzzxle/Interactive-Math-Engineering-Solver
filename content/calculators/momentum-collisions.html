What the collision calculator does

In any collision with no outside forces, the total momentum p = mv is conserved. Kinetic energy may or may not survive: an elastic collision keeps it all, a perfectly inelastic one (the bodies stick together) loses the most.

The coefficient of restitution e measures bounciness: the speed at which the bodies separate is e times the speed at which they approached.

Try it

Equal masses, elastic collision, one cart at rest: the moving cart stops dead and the other leaves with its velocity (Newton’s cradle).

Formulas and variables

\[ m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = m_1 v_1' + m_2 v_2' \]\[ v_2' - v_1' = e\,(v_1 - v_2) \]\[ v_1' = \frac{p + m_2 e (v_2 - v_1)}{m_1 + m_2},\quad v_2' = \frac{p + m_1 e (v_1 - v_2)}{m_1 + m_2} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(m_1,\ m_2\)Masses
\(v,\ v'\)Velocities before and after (signed: right is positive)
\(e\)Coefficient of restitution, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1

Worked example

A 1200 kg car at 15 m/s hits an 800 kg parked car and they lock together

Step 1 — Momentum before

\[ p = 1200(15) + 800(0) = 18\,000\ \text{kg m/s} \]

Step 2 — Common velocity

\[ v' = \frac{18\,000}{2000} = 9\ \text{m/s} \]

Step 3 — Energy

\[ KE: 135\,000 \to 81\,000\ \text{J},\ \text{so } 54\,000\ \text{J (40\%) is lost} \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Head-on collisions along one line; body 1 must be catching up with body 2 (v₁ > v₂).
  • External forces such as friction are ignored during the brief impact.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the sign of velocities in opposite directions.
  • Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in every collision.
