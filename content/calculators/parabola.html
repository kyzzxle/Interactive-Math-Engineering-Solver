What is a parabola?

A parabola is the set of all points that are the same distance from a fixed point, the focus, and a fixed line, the directrix. The point halfway between them is the vertex, and the line through the vertex and focus is the axis of symmetry. Every quadratic \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) graphs as a parabola.

Try changing the input

Make \(a\) smaller, for example \(y = 0.1x^2\). The parabola gets wider and the focus moves further from the vertex: \(p = \tfrac{1}{4a} = 2.5\).

What this calculator does

Enter a parabola in any of four forms:

  • \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) or \(x = ay^2 + by + c\);
  • \((x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)\) or \((y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)\).

It returns the vertex, focus, directrix, axis of symmetry, the value of \(p\), the direction it opens, the length of the latus rectum and the intercepts, and draws the curve with its focus and directrix.

The geometry behind it

Because every point is as far from the focus as from the directrix, the curve wraps around the focus. The number \(p\) is the distance from the vertex to the focus (and from the vertex to the directrix). Its sign gives the direction:

  • vertical axis: \(p > 0\) opens upward, \(p \lt 0\) downward;
  • horizontal axis: \(p > 0\) opens to the right, \(p \lt 0\) to the left.

The latus rectum is the chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis. Its length \(|4p|\) shows how wide the parabola is at the focus.

The reflective property

Any ray travelling parallel to the axis reflects off the parabola through the focus. That is why dish antennas, car headlights and solar cookers are parabolic.

Deriving the formulas

From the focus–directrix definition

Put the vertex at the origin, the focus at \((0, p)\) and the directrix at \(y = -p\). For a point \((x, y)\) on the parabola, the two distances are equal:

\[ \sqrt{x^2 + (y - p)^2} = |y + p| \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + y^2 - 2py + p^2 = y^2 + 2py + p^2 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = 4py. \]

Shifting the vertex to \((h, k)\) gives \((x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)\).

From the quadratic form

Complete the square in \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\):

\[ y = a\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + c - \frac{b^2}{4a} \;\Rightarrow\; \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{a}\left(y - c + \frac{b^2}{4a}\right). \]

Comparing with \((x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)\) gives the vertex and \(4p = \tfrac{1}{a}\), so \(p = \tfrac{1}{4a}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the vertex, focus and directrix of \(y = x^2 - 4x + 3\).

Step 1: Vertex

\[ x_0 = -\frac{-4}{2(1)} = 2, \qquad y_0 = 3 - \frac{16}{4} = -1 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad V = (2, -1) \]

Step 2: Focal distance

\[ p = \frac{1}{4a} = \frac{1}{4} \]

Step 3: Focus, directrix and axis

Since \(p > 0\), the parabola opens upward.

\[ F = \left(2,\ -1 + \tfrac{1}{4}\right) = \left(2, -\tfrac{3}{4}\right), \qquad \text{directrix } y = -\tfrac{5}{4}, \qquad \text{axis } x = 2 \]

Step 4: Intercepts

\(x^2 - 4x + 3 = (x - 1)(x - 3)\), so the x-intercepts are 1 and 3; the y-intercept is 3.

Check

The point \((3, 0)\) is \(\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{9}{16}} = \tfrac{5}{4}\) from the focus and \(0 - \left(-\tfrac{5}{4}\right) = \tfrac{5}{4}\) from the directrix. ✓

Second worked example: a horizontal parabola and a dish

Part 1: Reading the vertex form

For \((y - 1)^2 = 8(x + 2)\): the vertex is \((-2, 1)\) and \(4p = 8\), so \(p = 2\). The parabola opens to the right.

\[ F = (-2 + 2,\ 1) = (0, 1), \qquad \text{directrix } x = -4, \qquad \text{latus rectum } 8 \]

Part 2: Where to put the receiver

A satellite dish is 2 m across and 0.5 m deep. Put the vertex at the origin with the axis vertical, so \(x^2 = 4py\). The rim passes through \((1, 0.5)\):

\[ 1^2 = 4p(0.5) \;\Rightarrow\; p = 0.5\ \text{m} \]

The receiver goes at the focus, 0.5 m above the centre of the dish: level with the rim.

Practical applications

  • Antennas, telescopes and headlights: the reflective property sends parallel rays to or from the focus.
  • Projectiles: without air resistance, a thrown object follows a parabola (see projectile motion).
  • Suspension bridges: a cable carrying a uniform deck load hangs in a parabola.
  • Vertical road curves: crest and sag curves in highway design are parabolic.

Common mistakes

  1. Using \(4p\) as \(p\). In \((x - h)^2 = 12(y - k)\), \(p = 3\), not 12.
  2. Sign of the vertex. \((y + 1)^2\) means \(k = -1\).
  3. Putting the directrix on the wrong side. The focus is inside the curve; the directrix is outside, \(p\) units the other way.
  4. Mixing up the axis. If \(x\) is squared the axis is vertical; if \(y\) is squared it is horizontal.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The axis is parallel to the x- or y-axis. Tilted parabolas (with an \(xy\) term) are handled by the general conic calculator.
  • \(a\) and \(p\) cannot be 0; the equation would then be a straight line.
  • Vertex, focus, directrix and \(p\) are exact fractions; intercepts are rounded to 6 decimal places.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the focus of y = ax² + bx + c?

Find the vertex (−b/2a, c − b²/4a) and p = 1/(4a). The focus is p units above the vertex (below it if a is negative).

What is the directrix of a parabola?

The fixed line that, with the focus, defines the parabola. It is p units from the vertex on the opposite side to the focus.

What is the latus rectum?

The chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis. Its length is |4p|.

How do I tell which way a parabola opens?

If x is squared it opens up (p > 0) or down (p < 0). If y is squared it opens right (p > 0) or left (p < 0).
