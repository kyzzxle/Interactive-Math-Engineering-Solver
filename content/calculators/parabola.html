Parts of a parabola

A parabola is the set of points that are equally far from a fixed point, the focus, and a fixed line, the directrix. The point halfway between them is the vertex, and the line through the vertex and focus is the axis of symmetry. Enter a parabola in one of four common forms to find all of these, plus the focal distance \(p\), the latus rectum and the intercepts, with a graph.

Try changing the input

Make \(a\) larger in \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\): the parabola gets narrower and the focus moves closer to the vertex, because \(p = \tfrac{1}{4a}\).

Formulas and variables

FormVertexFocusDirectrix
\((x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)\)\((h, k)\)\((h, k + p)\)\(y = k - p\)
\((y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)\)\((h, k)\)\((h + p, k)\)\(x = h - p\)
\(y = ax^2 + bx + c\)\(\left(-\frac{b}{2a},\ c - \frac{b^2}{4a}\right)\)use \(p = \frac{1}{4a}\) in the first row

\(p\) is the signed distance from the vertex to the focus. For a vertical axis, \(p > 0\) opens upward and \(p < 0\) downward; for a horizontal axis, \(p > 0\) opens to the right. The latus rectum (the chord through the focus parallel to the directrix) has length \(|4p|\).

Worked example

\(y = x^2 - 4x + 3\)

Step 1 — Vertex

\[ x_0 = -\frac{-4}{2(1)} = 2, \qquad y_0 = 3 - \frac{(-4)^2}{4(1)} = -1 \]

Step 2 — Focal distance

\[ p = \frac{1}{4(1)} = \frac{1}{4} \]

Step 3 — Focus and directrix

\[ F = \left(2,\ -1 + \tfrac{1}{4}\right) = \left(2,\ -\tfrac{3}{4}\right), \qquad \text{directrix } y = -1 - \tfrac{1}{4} = -\tfrac{5}{4} \]

Step 4 — Intercepts

\(x^2 - 4x + 3 = (x - 1)(x - 3) = 0\) gives x-intercepts 1 and 3; the y-intercept is 3.

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Supported: parabolas with vertical or horizontal axes in the four forms listed. Rotated parabolas (with an \(xy\) term) are not supported.
  • \(a\) and \(p\) cannot be 0, since the equation would then describe a line.
  • Vertex, focus, directrix and \(p\) are exact fractions; intercepts are rounded to 6 decimal places.
