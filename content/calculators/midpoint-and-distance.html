Midpoint and distance between two points

Given two points \(P_1(x_1, y_1)\) and \(P_2(x_2, y_2)\) in the coordinate plane, the midpoint is the point exactly halfway between them and the distance is the length of the segment joining them. Both are among the first tools of analytic geometry: the midpoint finds centres and bisectors, the distance formula measures lengths, radii and perimeters.

Try changing the input

Move one point and watch the midpoint move half as far in the same direction. Swapping the two points changes nothing: both formulas are symmetric.

Formulas and variables

\[ M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right) \qquad d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2} \]
SymbolMeaning
\((x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2)\)The two given points (decimals and fractions are allowed).
\(\Delta x, \Delta y\)Horizontal and vertical changes, \(x_2 - x_1\) and \(y_2 - y_1\).
\(M\)Midpoint: the average of the coordinates.
\(d\)Distance: the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs \(|\Delta x|\) and \(|\Delta y|\).

The distance formula is the Pythagorean theorem in disguise: the horizontal and vertical changes are the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the segment \(P_1P_2\).

Worked example

Points \((-2, 1)\) and \((4, 9)\)

Step 1 — Midpoint

\[ M = \left(\frac{-2 + 4}{2},\ \frac{1 + 9}{2}\right) = (1,\ 5) \]

Step 2 — Changes in x and y

\[ \Delta x = 4 - (-2) = 6, \qquad \Delta y = 9 - 1 = 8 \]

Step 3 — Distance

\[ d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Works in the two-dimensional Cartesian plane with the same units on both axes.
  • Inputs may be integers, decimals or fractions such as 3/4; all arithmetic is exact.
  • Distances are given in simplest radical form (for example \(2\sqrt{5}\)) together with a decimal rounded to 6 places.
  • If both points are the same, the distance is 0 and the midpoint is that point.
