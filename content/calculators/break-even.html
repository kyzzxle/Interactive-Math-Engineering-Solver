What the break-even calculator does

A business has two kinds of cost. Fixed costs, such as rent, salaries and equipment payments, stay the same whatever it produces. Variable costs, such as materials and energy, grow with every unit made. The break-even point is the volume at which revenue exactly covers both: no profit and no loss.

The calculator finds the break-even volume and revenue, the contribution margin, the volume needed for a target profit, and the profit or loss at a planned volume. A break-even chart shows where the revenue and total cost lines cross.

Try it

In the explorer, raise the variable cost towards the price. The break-even point moves far to the right; when the price equals the variable cost, the lines never cross.

Formulas and variables

\[ \text{Total cost} = FC + v\,Q \qquad \text{Revenue} = p\,Q \]
\[ Q_{BE} = \frac{FC}{p - v} \qquad R_{BE} = p\,Q_{BE} \qquad Q_{\text{target}} = \frac{FC + \text{profit}}{p - v} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(FC\)Fixed cost per period
\(v\)Variable cost per unit
\(p\)Selling price per unit
\(p - v\)Contribution margin: what each unit contributes to fixed cost and profit
\(Q_{BE},\ R_{BE}\)Break-even volume and break-even revenue

Worked example

A bakery has fixed costs of 240,000 a month. Each cake costs 35 to make and sells for 75.

Step 1: Contribution margin

\[ p - v = 75 - 35 = 40 \]

Step 2: Break-even volume and revenue

\[ Q_{BE} = \frac{240{,}000}{40} = 6{,}000\ \text{cakes}, \qquad R_{BE} = 75 \times 6{,}000 = 450{,}000 \]

Step 3: Volume for a profit of 100,000

\[ Q = \frac{240{,}000 + 100{,}000}{40} = 8{,}500\ \text{cakes} \]

Assumptions and limitations

  • Price and variable cost per unit are constant, so cost and revenue are straight lines. Bulk discounts or overtime would bend them.
  • Fixed costs do not change within the range of output considered, and everything produced is sold.
  • The break-even volume is often fractional; in practice round up to the next whole unit, as the calculator also shows.
  • The break-even point is where two straight lines meet: the same algebra as the linear equation solver.
