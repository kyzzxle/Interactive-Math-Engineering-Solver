Where does a line meet a circle?

A straight line and a circle can meet in two points (the line is a secant), touch at one point (a tangent), or miss each other completely. Which case happens depends on one number: the perpendicular distance from the centre of the circle to the line, compared with the radius.

Try changing the input

Keep the circle fixed and slide the line away by changing \(C\). The two points move together, merge into one tangent point, and then disappear.

What this calculator does

  • Takes the line \(Ax + By + C = 0\) and the circle \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\).
  • Finds the distance \(d\) from the centre to the line and decides the case.
  • Gives the intersection points (or the point of tangency) and the chord length.
  • Draws the circle, the line and the perpendicular from the centre.

The geometry behind it

Drop a perpendicular from the centre to the line; it meets the line at the foot \(F\), the point of the line closest to the centre. Its length is \(d\).

  • \(d \lt r\): \(F\) is inside the circle, so the line passes through it and cuts it twice.
  • \(d = r\): \(F\) is on the circle, and the line just touches it there.
  • \(d > r\): even the closest point of the line is outside, so there is no intersection.

When there are two points, the perpendicular bisects the chord. The two points sit at equal distances \(t = \sqrt{r^2 - d^2}\) on each side of \(F\), by Pythagoras in the triangle centre–\(F\)–point.

The algebraic view

Substituting the line into the circle gives a quadratic. Its discriminant is positive, zero or negative exactly when \(d \lt r\), \(d = r\) or \(d > r\).

Deriving the method

By substitution

For a line \(y = mx + c\) and the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\), substitute for \(y\):

\[ x^2 + (mx + c)^2 = r^2 \;\Rightarrow\; (1 + m^2)x^2 + 2mc\,x + (c^2 - r^2) = 0. \]

The discriminant simplifies neatly:

\[ 4m^2c^2 - 4(1 + m^2)(c^2 - r^2) = 4\left[r^2(1 + m^2) - c^2\right]. \]

It is zero when \(c^2 = r^2(1 + m^2)\), that is when \(\tfrac{|c|}{\sqrt{1 + m^2}} = r\): the distance from the centre to the line equals the radius.

By geometry

The geometric method above avoids the quadratic altogether, works for vertical lines too, and gives the chord length directly. The calculator uses it.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find where the line \(x - y + 1 = 0\) meets the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\).

Step 1: Distance from the centre

\[ d = \frac{|1(0) - 1(0) + 1|}{\sqrt{1 + 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071 \lt 5 \]

So the line cuts the circle at two points.

Step 2: Substitute

From the line, \(y = x + 1\). Then \(x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25\), so \(2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0\), or \(x^2 + x - 12 = 0\).

Step 3: Solve

\[ (x + 4)(x - 3) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = -4 \text{ or } x = 3 \]

The points are \((-4, -3)\) and \((3, 4)\).

Check

The chord length is \(\sqrt{7^2 + 7^2} = 7\sqrt{2} \approx 9.899\), and \(2\sqrt{25 - \tfrac{1}{2}} = 2\sqrt{24.5} \approx 9.899\). ✓

Second worked example: tangent or miss?

Part 1: A tangent line

Does \(3x + 4y - 25 = 0\) touch \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)?

\[ d = \frac{|0 + 0 - 25|}{\sqrt{9 + 16}} = \frac{25}{5} = 5 = r \]

Yes, it is a tangent. The point of contact is the foot of the perpendicular: \(N = -25\), so \(F = \left(0 - \tfrac{3(-25)}{25},\ 0 - \tfrac{4(-25)}{25}\right) = (3, 4)\).

Part 2: A line that misses

Does \(x + y - 12 = 0\) meet the circle with centre \((1, 2)\) and radius 3?

\[ d = \frac{|1 + 2 - 12|}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{9}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 6.364 > 3 \]

No: the line passes more than twice the radius away from the centre.

Practical applications

  • Road design: where a straight approach meets a circular curve, and whether it joins tangentially.
  • Radar and coverage: whether a straight flight path enters a circular range, and for how long (the chord).
  • Machining: where a straight cut crosses a circular hole or boss.
  • Graphics and games: ray–circle collision tests.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the origin instead of the centre. The distance is measured from \((h, k)\), not from \((0, 0)\), unless the circle is centred at the origin.
  2. Comparing \(d\) with \(r^2\). Compare the distance with the radius itself.
  3. Forgetting the second point. A quadratic usually has two roots; each gives a point.
  4. Losing the y-coordinates. After solving for \(x\), substitute back into the line, not the circle (the circle gives two y-values for each x).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The line is entered in general form; \(A\) and \(B\) cannot both be 0.
  • The radius must be positive.
  • Points are calculated in floating point and shown to 6 decimal places; they are marked exact when they are whole numbers or short decimals.
  • A line that is tangent to within rounding error is treated as tangent.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a line is tangent to a circle?

Find the perpendicular distance from the centre to the line. If it equals the radius, the line is tangent.

How many points can a line and a circle share?

Two (a secant), one (a tangent) or none. A line can never cross a circle more than twice.

How do I find the length of the chord?

Chord length = 2√(r² − d²), where d is the distance from the centre to the line.

Can I solve it by substitution instead?

Yes. Solve the line for y (or x), substitute into the circle, and solve the quadratic. The geometric method gives the same points.
