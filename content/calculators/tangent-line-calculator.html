Tangent and normal lines

The tangent line to a curve at a point touches the curve there and has the same slope as the curve at that point — the value of the derivative. Close to the point it is the best straight-line approximation of the curve, which is the idea behind linear approximation and Newton's method. The normal line passes through the same point at right angles to the tangent.

Try changing the input

Move \(x_0\) along \(y = x^2 - 3x + 1\). At the vertex \(x_0 = 1.5\) the slope is 0, so the tangent is horizontal and the normal is vertical.

Formulas and variables

\[ y = f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0) \qquad \text{normal: } y = f(x_0) - \frac{1}{f'(x_0)}(x - x_0) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(x_0\)The x-coordinate of the point of tangency.
\(f(x_0)\)The y-coordinate of that point.
\(m = f'(x_0)\)Slope of the tangent line.

Worked example

Tangent to \(f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 1\) at \(x = 2\)

Step 1 — Point

\[ f(2) = 4 - 6 + 1 = -1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (2, -1) \]

Step 2 — Slope

\[ f'(x) = 2x - 3, \qquad m = f'(2) = 1 \]

Step 3 — Equation

\[ y - (-1) = 1(x - 2) \;\Rightarrow\; y = x - 3 \]

The normal line has slope \(-1\): \(y = -x + 1\).

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • \(f\) must be defined and differentiable at \(x_0\); otherwise no tangent is shown (vertical tangents are not handled).
  • For polynomials with a rational \(x_0\), every value is an exact fraction. Otherwise decimals are rounded to 6 places.
  • \(x_0\) may be an expression such as pi/3.
