What the angle between two lines calculator does

Two intersecting lines make two pairs of equal angles that add up to 180°. By convention the angle between the lines is the acute one, θ; the other is 180° − θ.

With slopes, the tangent formula gives θ directly. With direction vectors (in 2D or 3D) the dot product gives \(\cos\theta\). For lines given as \(Ax + By + C = 0\), the normals \((A, B)\) make the same angle as the lines.

Try it

Make m₁·m₂ = −1 in the explorer: the lines become perpendicular and the tan formula’s denominator is 0.

Formulas and variables

\[ \tan\theta = \left\lvert \frac{m_2 - m_1}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right\rvert \]\[ \cos\theta = \frac{\lvert u \cdot v \rvert}{\lVert u \rVert\,\lVert v \rVert} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(m_1,\ m_2\)Slopes of the two lines
\(u,\ v\)Direction vectors (2D or 3D)
\(\theta\)Acute angle between the lines

Equal slopes mean parallel lines (θ = 0°); m₁m₂ = −1 means perpendicular lines (θ = 90°).

Worked example

Lines with slopes 2 and −1/3

Step 1 — Substitute

\[ \tan\theta = \left| \frac{-\tfrac{1}{3} - 2}{1 + 2(-\tfrac{1}{3})} \right| = \left| \frac{-7/3}{1/3} \right| = 7 \]

Step 2 — Inverse tangent

\[ \theta = \tan^{-1} 7 \approx 81.87^\circ \]

Step 3 — The other angle

\[ 180^\circ - 81.87^\circ = 98.13^\circ \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Vertical lines have no slope: use the general-form or vector mode instead.
  • In 3D, skew lines (which never meet) still have an angle: that of their directions.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the absolute value and getting a negative angle.
  • Using the coefficients (A, B) as a direction in 2D: they are the normal; the direction is (B, −A). Both give the same angle.
