What is the angle between two lines?

Two lines that cross form two pairs of equal angles: an acute angle \(\theta\) and its supplement \(180^\circ - \theta\). By convention, "the angle between the lines" means the acute one, between 0° and 90°. It depends only on the directions of the lines, not on where they are, so it can be found from slopes, from general-form coefficients or from direction vectors in space.

What this calculator does

  • From slopes \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), with the tangent formula.
  • From general form \(A_1x + B_1y + C_1 = 0\) and \(A_2x + B_2y + C_2 = 0\), using the normal vectors.
  • From 3D direction vectors, with the dot product.

It gives the acute angle in degrees and radians, its supplement, and flags parallel and perpendicular lines.

The geometry behind it

A line with slope \(m\) makes an angle \(\alpha\) with the x-axis where \(\tan\alpha = m\). Two lines with inclinations \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_2\) meet at the angle \(\alpha_2 - \alpha_1\) (or its supplement), and the tangent subtraction formula turns that into slopes.

With vectors, the dot product gives the angle directly: \(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = |\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta\). Taking the absolute value of the dot product picks the acute angle, because a line has no preferred direction: \(\vec{u}\) and \(-\vec{u}\) describe the same line.

Special cases

Parallel: \(m_1 = m_2\), or \(\vec{u}\) a multiple of \(\vec{v}\). Perpendicular: \(m_1m_2 = -1\), or \(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = 0\).

Deriving the slope formula

Let \(\tan\alpha_1 = m_1\) and \(\tan\alpha_2 = m_2\). The angle between the lines is \(\alpha_2 - \alpha_1\), and by the tangent subtraction identity

\[ \tan(\alpha_2 - \alpha_1) = \frac{\tan\alpha_2 - \tan\alpha_1}{1 + \tan\alpha_1\tan\alpha_2} = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{1 + m_1m_2}. \]

The absolute value gives the acute angle. If \(1 + m_1m_2 = 0\) the tangent is undefined, which means \(\theta = 90^\circ\).

Why normals work too

The normal of each line is perpendicular to it, so rotating both lines by 90° turns them into their normals without changing the angle between them.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the angle between lines with slopes \(m_1 = 2\) and \(m_2 = -\tfrac{1}{3}\).

Step 1: Numerator and denominator

\[ m_2 - m_1 = -\tfrac{1}{3} - 2 = -\tfrac{7}{3}, \qquad 1 + m_1m_2 = 1 - \tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{1}{3} \]

Step 2: Tangent

\[ \tan\theta = \left\lvert \frac{-7/3}{1/3} \right\rvert = 7 \]

Step 3: Angle

\[ \theta = \tan^{-1} 7 \approx 81.87^\circ, \qquad 180^\circ - \theta \approx 98.13^\circ \]

Check

The inclinations are \(\tan^{-1} 2 \approx 63.43^\circ\) and \(180^\circ - \tan^{-1}\tfrac{1}{3} \approx 161.57^\circ\). Their difference is \(98.13^\circ\), whose supplement is \(81.87^\circ\). ✓

Second worked example: lines in space

Problem

Two lines have directions \(\vec{u} = (1, 2, 2)\) and \(\vec{v} = (2, -1, 2)\). Find the angle between them.

Step 1: Dot product

\[ \vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = (1)(2) + (2)(-1) + (2)(2) = 4 \]

Step 2: Lengths

\[ |\vec{u}| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3, \qquad |\vec{v}| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3 \]

Step 3: Angle

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{4}{9} \;\Rightarrow\; \theta = \cos^{-1}\tfrac{4}{9} \approx 63.61^\circ \]

The lines need not meet: for skew lines this is the angle between their directions.

Practical applications

  • Road junctions: the angle at which two straight roads meet.
  • Trusses and frames: the angles between members, needed to resolve forces.
  • Optics: angles of incidence and reflection measured from a normal line.
  • Crystallography: angles between directions in a crystal lattice.

Common mistakes

  1. Dropping the absolute value. Without it you may get the obtuse angle or a negative angle.
  2. Calculator in radian mode. \(\tan^{-1} 7\) is about 1.4289 rad, which is 81.87°.
  3. Using the coefficients as slopes. The slope of \(Ax + By + C = 0\) is \(-\tfrac{A}{B}\).
  4. Treating \(1 + m_1m_2 = 0\) as an error. It means the lines are perpendicular.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Slopes and coefficients may be fractions; the working is exact up to the inverse trig step.
  • Angles are shown to 4 decimal places.
  • Vertical lines have no slope: enter them in general form or as direction vectors.
  • Direction vectors cannot be the zero vector.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the angle between two lines?

tan θ = |(m₂ − m₁)/(1 + m₁m₂)| for slopes, or cos θ = |u·v|/(|u||v|) for direction vectors.

How do I know if two lines are perpendicular?

Their slopes multiply to −1, or their direction vectors have a dot product of 0.

Why is the acute angle used?

Crossing lines make two different angles that add to 180°. The acute one is the standard choice; the calculator also shows the other.

Does C in Ax + By + C = 0 change the angle?

No. C only shifts the line; the direction depends on A and B.
