What the optimization calculator does

Optimization means finding the best value — the largest volume, the smallest cost. With calculus, write the quantity to optimise as a function of one variable (using any constraint), then find where its derivative is zero.

A critical point is not automatically the answer: check it is in the allowed range and that it is a maximum or minimum, for example with the second-derivative test.

Try it

Drag the cut size in the explorer and watch the point on V(x): the peak is where the slope V′(x) is zero.

Formulas and variables

\[ f'(x^*) = 0,\quad f''(x^*) < 0 \Rightarrow \text{maximum},\quad f''(x^*) > 0 \Rightarrow \text{minimum} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(x\)The variable you choose (cut size, side length, radius)
\(x^*\)The optimal value

Supported problems: the open box cut from a sheet, the fenced rectangle (with or without a wall), and the closed cylindrical can.

Worked example

A rectangle with 100 m of fencing along a wall (three sides fenced)

Step 1 — Constraint and objective

\[ 2x + y = 100 \Rightarrow A(x) = x(100 - 2x) \]

Step 2 — Differentiate

\[ A'(x) = 100 - 4x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 25 \]

Step 3 — Check and answer

\[ A''(x) = -4 < 0:\ \text{maximum};\quad y = 50,\ A = 1250\ \text{m}^2 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • All given sizes must be positive. The box cut must be less than half of the shorter side.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the domain (a cut larger than half the sheet is impossible).
  • Stopping at f′(x) = 0 without checking whether it is a maximum or a minimum.
