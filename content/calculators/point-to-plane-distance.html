What is the distance from a point to a plane?

The distance from a point \(P(x_0, y_0, z_0)\) to a plane is the length of the shortest segment from \(P\) to any point of the plane. That shortest segment is always perpendicular to the plane, so it runs along the plane's normal vector and meets the plane at the foot of the perpendicular \(F\).

Try changing the input

Multiply every coefficient of the plane by 2, for example \(4x - 2y + 4z - 6 = 0\) instead of \(2x - y + 2z - 3 = 0\). The plane is the same, and so is the distance: the numerator and the denominator both double.

What this calculator does

  • Takes a plane \(Ax + By + Cz + D = 0\) and a point \(P(x_0, y_0, z_0)\).
  • Finds the perpendicular distance, exactly (in simplest radical form) and as a decimal.
  • Finds the foot of the perpendicular \(F\), the closest point of the plane to \(P\).
  • Tells you which side of the plane the point is on, and sketches the plane, point and perpendicular.

The geometry behind it

The vector \(\vec{n} = (A, B, C)\) is normal (perpendicular) to the plane. Take any point \(Q\) on the plane. The distance from \(P\) to the plane is the length of the projection of \(\vec{QP}\) onto \(\vec{n}\): the part of \(\vec{QP}\) that points straight out of the plane.

Substituting \(P\) into the left side of the plane equation measures exactly that, scaled by \(|\vec{n}|\). Its sign tells you the side: positive on the side \(\vec{n}\) points to, negative on the other side, zero on the plane.

Same idea in 2D

This is the three-dimensional version of the distance from a point to a line, \(d = \tfrac{|Ax_0 + By_0 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}\).

Formula breakdown

\[ d = \frac{\lvert A x_0 + B y_0 + C z_0 + D \rvert}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + C^2}} \]

Deriving the formula

Let \(Q(x_1, y_1, z_1)\) be any point on the plane, so \(Ax_1 + By_1 + Cz_1 = -D\). The distance is the length of the projection of \(\vec{QP}\) onto the unit normal:

\[ d = \frac{|\vec{QP}\cdot\vec{n}|}{|\vec{n}|} = \frac{|A(x_0 - x_1) + B(y_0 - y_1) + C(z_0 - z_1)|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + C^2}}. \]

Expand the numerator:

\[ Ax_0 + By_0 + Cz_0 - (Ax_1 + By_1 + Cz_1) = Ax_0 + By_0 + Cz_0 + D. \]

The point \(Q\) drops out, which gives the formula.

For the foot, move from \(P\) along the normal: \(F = P - t\vec{n}\). Requiring \(F\) to satisfy the plane equation gives \(N - t|\vec{n}|^2 = 0\), so \(t = \tfrac{N}{|\vec{n}|^2}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the distance from \(P(1, 4, 5)\) to the plane \(2x - y + 2z - 3 = 0\), and the foot of the perpendicular.

Step 1: Substitute the point

\[ N = 2(1) - 4 + 2(5) - 3 = 5 \]

Step 2: Length of the normal

\[ |\vec{n}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \]

Step 3: Distance

\[ d = \frac{|5|}{3} = \frac{5}{3} \approx 1.6667 \]

Step 4: Foot of the perpendicular

\[ t = \frac{5}{9}, \qquad F = \left(1 - \tfrac{10}{9},\ 4 + \tfrac{5}{9},\ 5 - \tfrac{10}{9}\right) = \left(-\tfrac{1}{9},\ \tfrac{41}{9},\ \tfrac{35}{9}\right) \]

Check

\[ 2\left(-\tfrac{1}{9}\right) - \tfrac{41}{9} + 2\left(\tfrac{35}{9}\right) - 3 = \tfrac{-2 - 41 + 70 - 27}{9} = 0 \]

So \(F\) is on the plane. ✓

Second worked example: a radical answer and parallel planes

Part 1: From the origin

How far is the origin from \(x + y + z = 1\)? First write the plane as \(x + y + z - 1 = 0\), so \(D = -1\).

\[ d = \frac{|0 + 0 + 0 - 1|}{\sqrt{1 + 1 + 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 0.5774 \]

Part 2: Two parallel planes

Find the distance between \(2x - y + 2z - 3 = 0\) and \(2x - y + 2z + 6 = 0\). The point \((0, -3, 0)\) lies on the first plane. Its distance to the second is

\[ d = \frac{|0 + 3 + 0 + 6|}{3} = 3. \]

The shortcut \(\tfrac{|D_2 - D_1|}{|\vec{n}|} = \tfrac{|6 - (-3)|}{3} = 3\) agrees. ✓

Practical applications

  • Clearances: how far a point on a pipe or beam sits from a wall, floor or sloping roof.
  • Geology and mining: the depth of a borehole point below a tilted rock layer.
  • Computer graphics: which side of a surface a point is on, and how far, for clipping and collision.
  • Machine learning: the margin of a point from a separating hyperplane.

Common mistakes

  1. Not moving everything to one side. For \(x + y + z = 1\), \(D = -1\), not 1.
  2. Forgetting the absolute value. Distance is never negative; the sign only tells you the side.
  3. Including D under the square root. The denominator is \(\sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + C^2}\) only.
  4. Parallel planes with different scaling. Make the normals identical before using \(|D_2 - D_1|\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The plane is entered in general form; \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) cannot all be 0.
  • Inputs may be fractions; all arithmetic is exact, with the distance in simplest radical form.
  • The sketch is a fixed-angle view to show the idea; it is not to scale along every axis.
  • For planes through three points, or angles between planes, use the lines and planes in space calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the distance from a point to a plane?

d = |Ax₀ + By₀ + Cz₀ + D| / √(A² + B² + C²), with the plane written as Ax + By + Cz + D = 0.

What does a distance of zero mean?

The point satisfies the plane equation, so it lies on the plane.

How do I find the closest point on the plane?

Move from P along the normal: F = P − t(A, B, C) with t = (Ax₀ + By₀ + Cz₀ + D)/(A² + B² + C²).

How do I find the distance between two parallel planes?

Pick any point on one plane and find its distance to the other, or use |D₂ − D₁|/√(A² + B² + C²) once both have the same A, B and C.
