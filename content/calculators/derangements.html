What the derangement calculator does

A derangement is an arrangement in which nothing ends up in its original place — every letter in the wrong envelope, nobody drawing their own name in a gift exchange. The number of derangements of n items is the subfactorial !n.

Surprisingly, the fraction of arrangements that are derangements settles almost immediately at 1/e ≈ 36.8%, whether there are 5 items or 5 million.

Try it

For n = 4 the calculator lists all 9 derangements of ABCD — check that no letter sits in its own position.

Formulas and variables

\[ !n = (n - 1)\left(!(n-1) + !(n-2)\right),\quad !0 = 1,\ !1 = 0 \]\[ !n = n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!} \qquad \frac{!n}{n!} \to \frac{1}{e} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(n\)Number of items
\(!n\)Number of derangements (subfactorial)
\(n!\)Number of all arrangements

The second formula comes from inclusion–exclusion: start with all n! arrangements, subtract those fixing at least one item, add back those fixing two, and so on.

Worked example

Four letters, four envelopes: in how many ways are all wrong?

Step 1 — Start values

\[ !0 = 1,\ !1 = 0,\ !2 = 1 \]

Step 2 — Recurrence

\[ !3 = 2(1 + 0) = 2,\qquad !4 = 3(2 + 1) = 9 \]

Step 3 — Probability

\[ \frac{9}{24} = 0.375 \approx \frac{1}{e} \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • n is a whole number from 0 to 1000; results are exact. Lists are shown for n ≤ 5.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing !n (subfactorial, written before n) with n! (factorial).
  • Counting arrangements where “at least one” is misplaced instead of “all”.
