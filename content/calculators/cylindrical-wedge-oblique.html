What the cylindrical wedge and oblique solids calculator does

A cylindrical wedge, or ungula ("hoof"), is cut from a right circular cylinder by a plane that passes through a diameter of the base and rises to a height \(h\) at the far side. It looks like a wedge of cheese cut from a round block.

An oblique cylinder or cone leans to one side: its axis is not perpendicular to its base. Cavalieri's principle says two solids with the same height and equal cross-sections at every level have the same volume, so leaning a cylinder or cone does not change its volume. Its slanted surface does get larger.

The calculator covers all three: the wedge's volume, curved face, base and slanted face; the oblique cylinder's volume and lateral area from its element length and angle; and the oblique cone's volume and lateral area.

Try it

In the explorer, drag the lean slider. The volume readout stays fixed while the lateral edge grows, which is Cavalieri's principle in action.

Formulas and variables

\[ \text{Wedge: } V = \tfrac{2}{3} r^2 h, \qquad A_{\text{curved}} = 2 r h, \qquad A_{\text{slanted}} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\,r\sqrt{r^2 + h^2} \]
\[ \text{Oblique cylinder: } h = L\sin\alpha, \qquad V = \pi r^2 h, \qquad A_{\text{lateral}} = P_{\text{right section}} \times L \]
\[ \text{Oblique cone: } V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Radius of the circular base
\(h\)Perpendicular height (for the wedge, the height at the far edge)
\(L\)Length of an element (lateral line) of an oblique cylinder
\(\alpha\)Angle between the elements and the base
\(P_{\text{right section}}\)Perimeter of the section perpendicular to the elements: an ellipse with semi-axes \(r\) and \(r\sin\alpha\)

The wedge's volume comes from slicing: sections perpendicular to the cutting diameter are right triangles of area \(\tfrac{h}{2r}(r^2 - x^2)\), and integrating from \(-r\) to \(r\) gives \(\tfrac{2}{3}r^2 h\). Remarkably, neither the volume nor the curved area contains π.

Worked example

A wedge cut from a cylinder of radius 6 cm, rising to 9 cm

Step 1: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{2}{3}(6)^2(9) = 216\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2: Curved surface

\[ A_{\text{curved}} = 2(6)(9) = 108\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Flat faces

\[ \text{base} = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi(6)^2 \approx 56.55, \qquad \text{slanted face} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}(6)\sqrt{36 + 81} \approx 101.94 \]

The total surface area is about \(108 + 56.55 + 101.94 = 266.49\ \text{cm}^2\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The wedge's cutting plane passes through a full diameter of the base. Other wedges (cut through a chord) need integration.
  • The oblique cylinder has parallel circular ends. Its lateral area uses the perimeter of an ellipse, which has no elementary formula, so it is computed numerically.
  • The oblique cone's lateral area is also computed numerically, by adding up thin triangles from the apex to the rim.
  • Upright versions: see the cylinder and cone calculators.
