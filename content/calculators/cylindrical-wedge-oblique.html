What are cylindrical wedges and oblique solids?

A cylindrical wedge, or ungula ("hoof"), is cut from a right circular cylinder by a plane that passes through a diameter of the base and rises to a height \(h\) at the far side. It looks like a wedge of cheese cut from a round block.

An oblique cylinder or cone leans to one side: its axis is not perpendicular to its base. A leaning stack of coins is an oblique cylinder.

What this calculator does

  • Wedge: from \(r\) and \(h\), the volume, the curved face, the semicircular base, the slanted half-ellipse and the total surface.
  • Oblique cylinder: from \(r\), the element length \(L\) and the angle \(\alpha\), the perpendicular height, the volume and the lateral area.
  • Oblique cone: from \(r\), \(h\) and the apex offset \(d\), the volume and the lateral area (computed numerically).

The geometry behind it

Cavalieri's principle: two solids with the same height whose horizontal cross-sections have equal areas at every level have the same volume. Leaning a cylinder or cone does not change its slices, so its volume does not change. Its slanted side does get larger.

No π in the wedge

The wedge's volume \(\tfrac{2}{3}r^2h\) and curved area \(2rh\) contain no π, even though it is cut from a cylinder. The slices that produce them are triangles, not circles.

Formula breakdown

\[ \text{Wedge: } V = \tfrac{2}{3} r^2 h, \qquad A_{\text{curved}} = 2 r h, \qquad A_{\text{slanted}} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\,r\sqrt{r^2 + h^2} \]
\[ \text{Oblique cylinder: } h = L\sin\alpha, \qquad V = \pi r^2 h, \qquad A_{\text{lateral}} = P_{\text{right section}} \times L \]
\[ \text{Oblique cone: } V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \]

Variables explained

SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Radius of the circular base
\(h\)Perpendicular height (for the wedge, the height at the far edge)
\(L\)Length of an element (lateral line) of an oblique cylinder
\(\alpha\)Angle between the elements and the base
\(d\)Horizontal offset of an oblique cone's apex from above the base centre
\(P_{\text{right section}}\)Perimeter of the section perpendicular to the elements: an ellipse with semi-axes \(r\) and \(r\sin\alpha\)

Deriving the formulas

Wedge volume

Put the cutting diameter on the \(x\)-axis. A slice perpendicular to it at \(x\) is a right triangle with base \(y = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2}\) and height \(\tfrac{h}{r}y\), so its area is \(\tfrac{h}{2r}(r^2 - x^2)\):

\[ V = \int_{-r}^{r} \frac{h}{2r}(r^2 - x^2)\,dx = \frac{h}{2r}\cdot\frac{4r^3}{3} = \frac{2}{3}r^2 h. \]

Wedge curved area

At angle \(t\) around the base, the curved wall has height \(h\sin t\). Unrolled over the arc length \(r\,dt\):

\[ A = \int_0^{\pi} h\sin t\cdot r\,dt = 2rh. \]

Oblique cylinder lateral area

Cut the cylinder perpendicular to its elements and unroll: the side becomes a parallelogram-like strip whose width is the perimeter of that right section and whose length is \(L\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A wedge is cut from a cylinder of radius 6 cm, rising to 9 cm. Find its volume and total surface.

Step 1: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{2}{3}(6)^2(9) = 216\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2: Curved surface

\[ A_{\text{curved}} = 2(6)(9) = 108\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Flat faces

\[ \text{base} = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi(6)^2 \approx 56.55, \qquad \text{slanted face} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}(6)\sqrt{36 + 81} \approx 101.94 \]

Step 4: Total

\(108 + 56.55 + 101.94 \approx 266.49\) cm².

Second worked example: an oblique cylinder

Problem

An oblique cylinder has base radius 4 cm, elements 10 cm long, inclined at 60° to the base. Find its volume and lateral area.

Step 1: Perpendicular height

\[ h = 10\sin 60^\circ = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.660\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \pi(4)^2(8.660) \approx 435.31\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 3: Right section and lateral area

The right section is an ellipse with semi-axes 4 and \(4\sin 60^\circ \approx 3.464\); its perimeter is about 23.479 cm.

\[ A_{\text{lateral}} \approx 23.479 \times 10 = 234.79\ \text{cm}^2 \]

An upright cylinder of the same height would have lateral area \(2\pi(4)(8.660) \approx 217.66\) cm², so the lean adds about 8%.

Practical applications

  • Tanks. A horizontal cylindrical tank tilted on its side contains a wedge-shaped volume of liquid.
  • Structures. Leaning columns and inclined pipes.
  • Manufacturing. Mitre cuts on round bars produce ungulas.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the element length for the volume. Volume uses the perpendicular height \(L\sin\alpha\).
  2. Using 2πr × L for the lateral area. That is only right when the cylinder is upright.
  3. Adding π to the wedge volume. It is \(\tfrac{2}{3}r^2h\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The wedge's cutting plane passes through a full diameter of the base.
  • The oblique cylinder has parallel circular ends; its lateral area uses a numerical ellipse perimeter.
  • The oblique cone's lateral area is computed numerically.
  • Upright versions: see the cylinder and cone calculators.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a cylindrical wedge?

V = (2/3)r²h, for a wedge cut by a plane through a diameter of the base rising to height h.

What is Cavalieri's principle?

If two solids have the same height and equal cross-sectional areas at every level, they have the same volume.

Does leaning a cylinder change its volume?

No, as long as the perpendicular height stays the same. It does increase the lateral area.

Why is there no simple formula for the lateral area of an oblique cylinder?

It needs the perimeter of an ellipse, which has no elementary closed form.
