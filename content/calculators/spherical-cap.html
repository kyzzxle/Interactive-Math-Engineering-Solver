What is a spherical cap?

A spherical cap is the piece of a sphere cut off by a single plane, like the top of a dome or the part of a ball above the waterline. Its flat face is a circle, the base, and its curved face is a zone of one base.

Cutting a sphere with two parallel planes gives a spherical segment (a slab of the sphere with two circular faces). Its curved surface is a zone. A hemisphere is a cap whose height equals the radius.

What this calculator does

  • Cap: from the sphere radius \(R\) and the cap height \(h\), finds the base radius, the volume, the curved area, the base area and the total surface area.
  • Segment: from \(R\) and the positions \(y_1\), \(y_2\) of the two cutting planes measured from the centre, finds the height, both base radii, the volume and the zone area.

Answers are given as exact multiples of π for whole-number or short-decimal inputs.

The geometry behind it

Draw the sphere from the side. The cutting plane is a horizontal line at distance \(R - h\) from the centre. From the centre to the rim of the base is \(R\) (a radius), so the right triangle with legs \(R - h\) and \(a\) gives the base radius:

\[ a^2 = R^2 - (R - h)^2 = h(2R - h). \]

Archimedes' surprise

The curved area of a cap is \(2\pi Rh\), which depends only on its height, not on where the cut is. Equal-height slices of a sphere always have equal curved areas, exactly like slices of the cylinder that wraps around it.

Deriving the formulas

Volume by slicing

Horizontal slices at height \(y\) are circles of radius \(\sqrt{R^2 - y^2}\). A cap of height \(h\) runs from \(y = R - h\) to \(y = R\):

\[ V = \int_{R-h}^{R} \pi(R^2 - y^2)\,dy = \frac{\pi h^2(3R - h)}{3}. \]

A segment between \(y_1\) and \(y_2\) is the same integral with those limits, which simplifies to \(\tfrac{\pi h}{6}(3a_1^2 + 3a_2^2 + h^2)\).

Curved area

For a surface of revolution, a thin band at height \(y\) has area \(2\pi x\,ds\). On a sphere, \(x\,ds = R\,dy\), so

\[ A = \int_{R-h}^{R} 2\pi R\,dy = 2\pi R h. \]

Complete worked example

Problem

A cap of height 2 cm is cut from a sphere of radius 5 cm. Find the base radius, the volume and the total surface area.

Step 1: Base radius

\[ a^2 = 2(2 \cdot 5 - 2) = 2(8) = 16, \qquad a = 4\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \frac{\pi(2)^2(3 \cdot 5 - 2)}{3} = \frac{\pi(4)(13)}{3} = \frac{52\pi}{3} \approx 54.45\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 3: Curved and total area

\[ A_{\text{curved}} = 2\pi(5)(2) = 20\pi, \qquad A_{\text{base}} = \pi(4)^2 = 16\pi, \qquad A = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Second worked example: a segment between two planes

Problem

A sphere of radius 5 m is cut by planes 1 m below and 3 m above the centre. Find the volume of the slab between them and its zone area.

Step 1: Height and base radii

\[ h = 3 - (-1) = 4, \qquad a_1^2 = 25 - 1 = 24, \qquad a_2^2 = 25 - 9 = 16 \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \frac{\pi(4)}{6}\left(3(24) + 3(16) + 16\right) = \frac{4\pi}{6}(136) = \frac{272\pi}{3} \approx 284.84\ \text{m}^3 \]

Step 3: Zone area

\[ A = 2\pi(5)(4) = 40\pi \approx 125.66\ \text{m}^2 \]

Practical applications

  • Domes and tank ends. Shallow domes and dished heads of tanks are caps.
  • Liquid in a spherical tank. The liquid at depth \(h\) fills a cap of height \(h\).
  • Optics. Lenses are bounded by spherical caps.
  • Geography. The area of the Earth above a given latitude is a zone.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the base radius a instead of the sphere radius R in \(\tfrac{\pi h^2(3R - h)}{3}\).
  2. Forgetting the base when the total surface is asked.
  3. Heights over 2R. A cap cannot be taller than the sphere's diameter.
  4. Signs of y₁ and y₂. Planes below the centre have negative positions.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The cutting planes are parallel and the solid is part of a perfect sphere.
  • For the cone from the centre plus a cap, see the spherical sector calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a spherical cap?

V = πh²(3R − h)/3, where R is the sphere's radius and h is the cap's height.

What is a zone of a sphere?

The curved surface between two parallel planes that cut the sphere. Its area is 2πRh, where h is the distance between the planes.

How much liquid is in a partly filled spherical tank?

If the liquid depth is h, the liquid is a cap of height h: V = πh²(3R − h)/3.

Is a hemisphere a spherical cap?

Yes, with h = R. Then V = (2/3)πR³ and the curved area is 2πR².
