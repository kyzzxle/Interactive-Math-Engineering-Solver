What the spherical cap and segment calculator does

A spherical cap is the piece of a ball cut off by one plane — a dome. A spherical segment (or zone, for its curved surface) is the slice between two parallel planes. Both depend only on the sphere’s radius \(R\) and where the cuts are.

A remarkable fact due to Archimedes: the curved area of a cap or zone is \(2\pi R h\), which depends only on its height \(h\), not on where on the sphere the slice is taken.

Try it

In the explorer, set h = R: the cap becomes a hemisphere holding exactly half of the sphere’s volume.

Formulas and variables

\[ a^2 = h(2R - h) \qquad V_{\text{cap}} = \frac{\pi h^2 (3R - h)}{3} \]\[ V_{\text{segment}} = \frac{\pi h}{6}\left(3a_1^2 + 3a_2^2 + h^2\right) \qquad A_{\text{curved}} = 2\pi R h \]
SymbolMeaning
\(R\)Radius of the sphere
\(h\)Height of the cap or segment
\(a,\ a_1,\ a_2\)Radii of the flat circular faces
\(y_1,\ y_2\)Signed distances of the cutting planes from the centre

Worked example

A cap of height 2 cm cut from a sphere of radius 5 cm

Step 1 — Base radius

\[ a^2 = 2(10 - 2) = 16,\quad a = 4\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2 — Volume

\[ V = \frac{\pi (2)^2 (15 - 2)}{3} = \frac{52\pi}{3} \approx 54.45\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 3 — Surface

\[ 2\pi(5)(2) + \pi(4)^2 = 20\pi + 16\pi = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • 0 < h ≤ 2R for a cap; −R ≤ y₁ < y₂ ≤ R for a segment.
  • h = R gives a hemisphere; h = 2R gives the whole sphere.
  • Exact π multiples are shown when R, h and the plane positions are exact numbers.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring h from the centre instead of from the cutting plane to the top of the cap.
  • Forgetting the flat base(s) when the total surface area is asked for.
