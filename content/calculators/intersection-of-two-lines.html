Where do two lines meet?

Two different lines in a plane either cross at exactly one point, never meet (they are parallel), or are actually the same line. Finding the point of intersection means solving the two equations simultaneously. This calculator uses Cramer's rule with exact fractions, checks the answer in both equations, reports the angle between the lines and graphs them.

Try changing the input

Make the second line's A and B proportional to the first line's (for example double them). The determinant becomes 0 and the lines are parallel — or identical if C is scaled the same way.

Formulas and variables

For \(A_1x + B_1y + C_1 = 0\) and \(A_2x + B_2y + C_2 = 0\):

\[ D = A_1B_2 - A_2B_1, \qquad x = \frac{B_1C_2 - B_2C_1}{D}, \qquad y = \frac{A_2C_1 - A_1C_2}{D} \]
CaseMeaning
\(D \ne 0\)The lines have different slopes and meet at exactly one point.
\(D = 0\), different linesParallel: no solution.
\(D = 0\), proportional equationsThe same line: infinitely many solutions.

The angle \(\theta\) between the lines satisfies \(\tan\theta = \left|\dfrac{A_1B_2 - A_2B_1}{A_1A_2 + B_1B_2}\right|\); the lines are perpendicular when \(A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 = 0\).

Worked example

\(2x + 3y - 12 = 0\) and \(x - y - 1 = 0\)

Step 1 — Determinant

\[ D = (2)(-1) - (1)(3) = -5 \]

Step 2 — Coordinates

\[ x = \frac{(3)(-1) - (-1)(-12)}{-5} = \frac{-15}{-5} = 3, \qquad y = \frac{(1)(-12) - (2)(-1)}{-5} = \frac{-10}{-5} = 2 \]

Step 3 — Check

\[ 2(3) + 3(2) - 12 = 0 \checkmark \qquad 3 - 2 - 1 = 0 \checkmark \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Each line must be written in general form; A and B cannot both be zero.
  • To convert \(y = mx + b\), use \(A = m\), \(B = -1\), \(C = b\).
  • Coordinates are exact fractions; decimals are rounded to 6 places and the angle to 4.
