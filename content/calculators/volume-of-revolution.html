What the volume of revolution calculator does

Spinning a flat region around an axis sweeps out a solid of revolution — a vase, a bowl, a cone. Its volume is found by slicing it into thin pieces and adding them up with an integral.

Spinning around the x-axis, each slice is a disk (or a washer if there is a hole). Spinning around the y-axis, it is easier to use thin cylindrical shells.

Formulas and variables

\[ V_{\text{disk}} = \pi\int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx \qquad V_{\text{washer}} = \pi\int_a^b \left[f(x)^2 - g(x)^2\right]dx \]\[ V_{\text{shell}} = 2\pi\int_a^b x\left[f(x) - g(x)\right]dx \]
SymbolMeaning
\(f(x)\)Outer curve
\(g(x)\)Inner curve (0 if there is no hole)
\([a, b]\)Interval of x

Worked example

Rotate y = x² for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2 about the y-axis

Step 1 — Shell method

\[ V = 2\pi\int_0^2 x \cdot x^2\,dx \]

Step 2 — Integrate

\[ = 2\pi\left[\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 2\pi(4) \]

Step 3 — Result

\[ V = 8\pi \approx 25.13 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • The region must satisfy f(x) ≥ g(x) ≥ 0 on [a, b] (it may not cross the axis of rotation).
  • For rotation about the y-axis, a ≥ 0.
  • Exact multiples of π for polynomials with rational limits; numerical otherwise.

Common mistakes

  • Using π∫(f − g)² instead of π∫(f² − g²) for washers.
  • Forgetting the factor x in the shell method.
