What are tangent and normal lines to a conic?

A tangent to a curve at a point is the line that just touches it there, meeting it at that point without crossing (for conics, it meets the curve at exactly one point). The normal is the line through the same point perpendicular to the tangent. For a circle the normal always passes through the centre, because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius.

What this calculator does

  • At a point on the curve: the tangent and normal, their slopes and equations.
  • With a given slope: every tangent with that slope and its point of contact.
  • From an outside point: the tangent lines through the point, their points of contact and the angle between them.

The geometry behind it

Substitute a line \(y = mx + c\) into the equation of a conic and you get a quadratic in \(x\). Two solutions mean the line cuts the conic twice; none means it misses. A tangent is the case in between: the quadratic has a double root, so its discriminant is zero. That one condition solves all three problem types.

The substitution rule

For a tangent at a point \((x_1, y_1)\) on the conic, replace \(x^2\) by \(x_1x\), \(y^2\) by \(y_1y\), \(x\) by \(\tfrac{x + x_1}{2}\) and \(y\) by \(\tfrac{y + y_1}{2}\). For example, the tangent to \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\) at \((x_1, y_1)\) is \(x_1x + y_1y = r^2\).

Deriving the formulas

Tangent with a given slope to an ellipse

Substitute \(y = mx + c\) into \(\tfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \tfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) and multiply by \(a^2b^2\):

\[ (b^2 + a^2m^2)x^2 + 2a^2mc\,x + a^2(c^2 - b^2) = 0. \]

For a double root, the discriminant is zero: \(4a^4m^2c^2 - 4(b^2 + a^2m^2)a^2(c^2 - b^2) = 0\), which simplifies to \(c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2\).

Tangent at a point, by implicit differentiation

Differentiating \(\tfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \tfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) gives \(\tfrac{2x}{a^2} + \tfrac{2y}{b^2}y' = 0\), so \(y' = -\tfrac{b^2x_1}{a^2y_1}\). The line through \((x_1, y_1)\) with this slope rearranges to \(\tfrac{x_1x}{a^2} + \tfrac{y_1y}{b^2} = 1\), the substitution rule.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the tangent and normal to \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) at \((3, 4)\).

Step 1: Tangent by the substitution rule

\[ 3x + 4y = 25 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\tfrac{3}{4}x + \tfrac{25}{4} \]

Step 2: Normal

The normal has slope \(\tfrac{4}{3}\) and passes through \((3, 4)\): \(y - 4 = \tfrac{4}{3}(x - 3)\), so \(y = \tfrac{4}{3}x\). It passes through the centre, as expected.

Second worked example: tangents from an outside point

Problem

Find the tangents to the parabola \(y^2 = 8x\) from the point \((-2, 3)\).

Step 1: Tangent condition

With \(4p = 8\), \(p = 2\), a line \(y = mx + c\) is tangent when \(c = \tfrac{2}{m}\).

Step 2: Through the point

\[ 3 = -2m + \frac{2}{m} \;\Rightarrow\; 2m^2 + 3m - 2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; m = \tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{or}\ m = -2 \]

Step 3: The tangents

\[ y = \tfrac{1}{2}x + 4 \quad\text{and}\quad y = -2x - 1 \]

Their slopes multiply to −1, so they are perpendicular: \((-2, 3)\) lies on the directrix \(x = -2\), and tangents from any point of the directrix are always perpendicular.

Practical applications

  • Optics: the reflection property of a parabola is proved with its tangent and normal.
  • Road and rail design: where a straight track joins a curve tangentially.
  • Mechanics: the velocity of a body moving along a conic orbit is along the tangent.

Common mistakes

  1. Using a point not on the curve with the substitution rule. That gives the polar line, not a tangent.
  2. Forgetting the second tangent. A given slope usually gives two tangents to an ellipse or hyperbola.
  3. Missing vertical tangents. They have no slope, so check \(x = x_1\) separately.
  4. Normal slope. It is \(-1/m_T\), not \(1/m_T\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The conic is in standard position (axes parallel to the coordinate axes). For tilted conics, rotate first with the general conic calculator.
  • A point inside an ellipse or circle has no tangents through it.
  • A hyperbola has no tangent with a slope less steep than its asymptotes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the equation of the tangent to a circle at a point?

For x² + y² = r² at (x₁, y₁) it is x₁x + y₁y = r². It is perpendicular to the radius to that point.

How do I find the normal line?

Find the slope of the tangent, take the negative reciprocal, and draw the line through the same point.

How many tangents can be drawn from an outside point?

Two for a circle, ellipse or parabola. For a hyperbola it can be two, one or none depending on the region.

What is the tangency condition?

Substitute the line into the conic: the resulting quadratic must have a zero discriminant (a double root).
