What the cone calculator does

A right circular cone has a circular base and a curved surface that narrows to a single point, the apex, directly above the centre of the base. Give the base radius and either the perpendicular height or the slant height: the calculator finds the other height with the Pythagorean theorem, then the lateral area, total surface area and volume.

Try changing the input

Increase the height while keeping the radius fixed: the volume grows in proportion to \(h\), but the curved area grows more slowly because the slant height \(\sqrt{r^2 + h^2}\) increases by less than \(h\).

Formulas and variables

\[ s = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} \qquad LSA = \pi r s \qquad TSA = \pi r^2 + \pi r s \qquad V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \]
SymbolMeaning
\(r\)Radius of the base.
\(h\)Perpendicular height from the base to the apex.
\(s\)Slant height: distance from the apex to the rim of the base along the surface.

The radius, height and slant height form a right triangle with \(s\) as the hypotenuse, which is why \(s\) must always be longer than \(r\). The volume is exactly one third of a cylinder with the same base and height.

Worked example

Radius 3 cm, height 4 cm

Step 1 — Slant height

\[ s = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \text{ cm} \]

Step 2 — Lateral and total area

\[ LSA = \pi(3)(5) = 15\pi, \qquad TSA = \pi(3)^2 + 15\pi = 24\pi \approx 75.40 \text{ cm}^2 \]

Step 3 — Volume

\[ V = \frac{1}{3}\pi(3)^2(4) = 12\pi \approx 37.70 \text{ cm}^3 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • The cone is a right circular cone (apex above the centre of the base).
  • All lengths must be positive, and a slant height must exceed the radius.
  • Exact forms with \(\pi\) and simplified square roots are shown for whole-number and short-decimal inputs; decimals are rounded to 6 places.
  • For a cone with its tip cut off, use the frustum of a cone calculator.
