What is a cone?

A right circular cone has a circular base and a curved surface that narrows to a single point, the apex, directly above the centre of the base. Ice-cream cones, traffic cones, funnels, conical tents and piles of sand are all cones.

Three lengths describe it: the base radius \(r\), the perpendicular height \(h\) from the base to the apex, and the slant height \(s\) measured along the sloping surface from the rim to the apex.

What this calculator does

Give the radius and either the height or the slant height. The calculator:

  • finds the missing height with the Pythagorean theorem, checking that the cone can exist,
  • computes the lateral (curved) area, the base area and the total surface area,
  • computes the volume, and compares it with the cylinder of the same base and height,
  • gives exact answers with π and simplified square roots when the inputs allow.

The geometry behind it

Cut a cone straight down through the apex. The cut face is an isosceles triangle, and half of it is a right triangle with legs \(r\) and \(h\) and hypotenuse \(s\). This one triangle links all three lengths:

\[ s^2 = r^2 + h^2. \]

Unroll the curved surface and it becomes a sector of a circle of radius \(s\). Its arc is the base circumference \(2\pi r\), which is what gives the curved area \(\pi r s\).

Two heights, two jobs

The height \(h\) controls the volume; the slant height \(s\) controls the curved surface. Using the wrong one is the most common error in cone problems.

Deriving the formulas

Volume by slicing

At a distance \(y\) below the apex, the horizontal slice is a circle of radius \(\tfrac{r}{h}y\) (similar triangles). Adding the slices:

\[ V = \int_0^h \pi\left(\frac{r y}{h}\right)^2 dy = \frac{\pi r^2}{h^2}\cdot\frac{h^3}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h. \]

Lateral area from the unrolled sector

A full circle of radius \(s\) has circumference \(2\pi s\) and area \(\pi s^2\). The sector keeps only the fraction \(\tfrac{2\pi r}{2\pi s} = \tfrac{r}{s}\) of it:

\[ LSA = \frac{r}{s}\cdot\pi s^2 = \pi r s. \]

Sector angle

To cut a cone from flat paper, the sector angle is \(\theta = \tfrac{r}{s}\times 360^\circ\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Find the slant height, the surface areas and the volume.

Step 1: Slant height

\[ s = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2: Lateral area

\[ LSA = \pi(3)(5) = 15\pi \approx 47.12\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Total surface area

\[ TSA = \pi(3)^2 + 15\pi = 9\pi + 15\pi = 24\pi \approx 75.40\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 4: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(3)^2(4) = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(36) = 12\pi \approx 37.70\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Answer

\(s = 5\) cm, \(LSA = 15\pi\), \(TSA = 24\pi\) cm², \(V = 12\pi \approx 37.70\) cm³.

Second worked example: slant height given

Problem

A conical tent has a base radius of 5 m and a slant height of 13 m. How much canvas covers the sloping side, and what volume of air is inside?

Step 1: Height

\[ h = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12\ \text{m} \]

Step 2: Canvas (lateral area)

\[ LSA = \pi(5)(13) = 65\pi \approx 204.20\ \text{m}^2 \]

Step 3: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(5)^2(12) = 100\pi \approx 314.16\ \text{m}^3 \]

Answer

About 204.2 m² of canvas and 314.2 m³ of air.

Practical applications

  • Stockpiles. Sand, gravel and grain form cones; the volume estimates the amount in a pile.
  • Hoppers and funnels. The conical part of a silo or hopper.
  • Sheet-metal work. The unrolled sector gives the pattern for a cone made of metal or paper.
  • Roofs and tents. The lateral area gives the roofing or canvas needed.

Common mistakes

  1. Using s in the volume formula. Volume needs the perpendicular height \(h\).
  2. Using h in the lateral area. The curved area needs the slant height \(s\).
  3. Forgetting the 1/3. Without it you get the cylinder's volume.
  4. A slant height shorter than the radius. Impossible: \(s\) is the hypotenuse. The calculator flags it.
  5. Diameter instead of radius. Halve the diameter first.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The cone is right: the apex is above the centre of the base. For a leaning cone, see oblique solids.
  • For a cone with its tip cut off, use the frustum of a cone calculator.
  • Exact radicals and multiples of π are shown for whole-number and short-decimal inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Why is a cone one third of a cylinder?

Slicing shows each cross-section's radius grows in proportion to the distance from the apex, so the volume is an integral of y², which gives one third. You can also see it by filling a cone with water three times to fill the matching cylinder.

What is the slant height of a cone?

The distance from the edge of the base to the apex along the surface: s = √(r² + h²).

How do I find the height from the slant height?

h = √(s² − r²). The slant height must be longer than the radius.

What is the surface area of an open cone, like an ice-cream cone?

Just the lateral area, πrs, because there is no base.

How do I make a paper cone of a given size?

Cut a sector of a circle of radius s with angle (r ÷ s) × 360°, then roll it so the two straight edges meet.
