What the double-angle and half-angle calculator does

The double-angle identities come from the sum identities with A = B. Cosine has three equivalent forms, each useful in a different situation (for example to rewrite cos²θ when integrating).

The half-angle identities come from solving the cosine double-angle formula backwards. They contain a square root whose sign depends on the quadrant of θ/2.

Try it

Try θ = 300° with cos(θ/2): θ/2 = 150° is in quadrant II, so the root must be negative.

Formulas and variables

\[ \sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta \qquad \cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta \]\[ \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos\theta}{2}} \qquad \cos\frac{\theta}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}} \qquad \tan\frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(\theta\)The given angle
\(\pm\)Sign chosen from the quadrant of θ/2

Worked example

Find sin 22.5° from θ = 45°

Step 1 — Identity

\[ \sin\frac{45^\circ}{2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos 45^\circ}{2}} \]

Step 2 — Sign

22.5° is in quadrant I, where sine is positive.

Step 3 — Evaluate

\[ \sin 22.5^\circ = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} \approx 0.3827 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Angles in degrees. Exact values for results at multiples of 15°; otherwise the exact substitution and a decimal.
  • tan(θ/2) needs sin θ ≠ 0; tan 2θ is undefined where cos 2θ = 0.

Common mistakes

  • Writing sin 2θ = 2 sin θ.
  • Dropping the ± in the half-angle formulas.
