Solving a right triangle

"Solving" a triangle means finding all of its sides and angles. In a right triangle one angle is already known to be 90°, so any two further measurements — at least one of them a length — determine everything else. Leave the unknown boxes empty and the calculator picks the right tool for your data: the Pythagorean theorem for two sides, or sine, cosine and tangent when an angle is involved.

Try changing the input

Keep the hypotenuse fixed and increase angle A: side a grows like \(\sin A\) and side b shrinks like \(\cos A\), while \(a^2 + b^2\) stays equal to \(c^2\).

Formulas and variables

\[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \qquad \sin A = \frac{a}{c} \qquad \cos A = \frac{b}{c} \qquad \tan A = \frac{a}{b} \qquad A + B = 90^\circ \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a, b\)Legs: the two sides that form the right angle.
\(c\)Hypotenuse: the side opposite the right angle (always the longest).
\(A, B\)Acute angles, opposite \(a\) and \(b\) respectively. \(C = 90^\circ\).

Memory aid: SOH–CAH–TOA — Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent.

Worked example

A ladder 10 m long makes a 30° angle with the wall

Take the ladder as the hypotenuse \(c = 10\) and the angle at the top as \(A = 30^\circ\).

Step 1 — Side opposite A (distance from the wall)

\[ a = c\sin A = 10 \sin 30^\circ = 5 \text{ m} \]

Step 2 — Side adjacent to A (height on the wall)

\[ b = c\cos A = 10\cos 30^\circ = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66 \text{ m} \]

Step 3 — Other angle

\[ B = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Enter exactly two values, at least one a side. Angles are in degrees and must be between 0° and 90°.
  • The hypotenuse must be longer than either leg; otherwise no right triangle exists.
  • The diagram is drawn to scale. Results are rounded to 6 decimal places.
  • For triangles without a right angle use the law of sines or law of cosines.
