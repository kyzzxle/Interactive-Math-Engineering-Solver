What the sum and difference identity calculator does

The sum and difference identities express the sine, cosine or tangent of A ± B using the sines and cosines of A and B. They turn awkward angles into combinations of familiar ones — for example 75° = 45° + 30°.

They are also the foundation of the double-angle and half-angle identities, and of formulas used for waves and rotations.

Try it

Try sin(30° + 60°): the identity gives exactly 1, whereas sin 30° + sin 60° ≈ 1.366.

Formulas and variables

\[ \sin(A \pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B \]\[ \cos(A \pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B \]\[ \tan(A \pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(A,\ B\)Two angles
\(\pm,\ \mp\)Use the top sign for a sum and the bottom sign for a difference

Worked example

Find sin 75° exactly

Step 1 — Split the angle

\[ \sin 75^\circ = \sin(45^\circ + 30^\circ) \]

Step 2 — Identity

\[ = \sin 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} \]

Step 3 — Simplify

\[ = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} \approx 0.9659 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Angles in degrees. Exact values appear when A and B are multiples of 15°.
  • tan is undefined at 90° + 180°n, so the tangent identity cannot use those angles.

Common mistakes

  • Writing sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B.
  • Using the same sign in the cosine identity: cos(A + B) has a minus.
