What the quartile calculator does

Quartiles split sorted data into four parts with equal numbers of values. Q₁ has a quarter of the data below it, the median Q₂ half, and Q₃ three quarters. The interquartile range Q₃ − Q₁ measures the spread of the middle half and is not affected by extreme values.

A percentile generalises the idea: the 90th percentile has 90% of the data below it.

Try it

Add a very large value to the data: the mean jumps, but the median and IQR hardly move.

Formulas and variables

\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 \]\[ \text{outlier if } x < Q_1 - 1.5\,\text{IQR} \ \text{ or } \ x > Q_3 + 1.5\,\text{IQR} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(Q_1,\ Q_2,\ Q_3\)First quartile, median, third quartile
\(P_k\)kth percentile (linear interpolation at position (n − 1)k/100)

Textbooks usually take Q₁ and Q₃ as the medians of the lower and upper halves; Excel, R and NumPy interpolate. For small data sets the two can differ slightly.

Worked example

Quartiles of 3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18, 21, 64

Step 1 — Median

\[ Q_2 = \frac{12 + 13}{2} = 12.5 \]

Step 2 — Halves

Lower half 3, 5, 7, 8, 12 gives \(Q_1 = 7\); upper half 13, 14, 18, 21, 64 gives \(Q_3 = 18\).

Step 3 — IQR and outliers

\[ \text{IQR} = 11,\quad \text{fences } [7 - 16.5,\ 18 + 16.5] = [-9.5,\ 34.5] \Rightarrow 64 \text{ is an outlier} \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • At least 4 values and at most 10,000.
  • Percentiles from 0 to 100, always by linear interpolation.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to sort the data first.
  • Comparing quartiles from software that uses a different method from your textbook.
