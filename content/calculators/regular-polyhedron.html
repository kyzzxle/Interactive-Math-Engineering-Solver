What is a regular polyhedron?

A regular polyhedron is a solid whose faces are all the same regular polygon, with the same number of faces meeting at every vertex. Only five exist, the Platonic solids: the tetrahedron (4 triangles), the cube (6 squares), the octahedron (8 triangles), the dodecahedron (12 pentagons) and the icosahedron (20 triangles). Dice used in board games are made in all five shapes.

What this calculator does

Give the edge, or any one of the surface area, volume, inradius or circumradius. The calculator finds the edge first, then all the others, plus the midradius, the area of one face and the dihedral angle between faces. It draws the solid in 3D, and for the tetrahedron, cube and octahedron with a rational edge it gives exact radical forms.

The geometry behind it

Why only five? At each vertex at least three faces must meet, and their angles must add to less than 360° or the corner would lie flat. Triangles (60°) allow 3, 4 or 5 per vertex: tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron. Squares (90°) allow only 3: the cube. Pentagons (108°) allow only 3: the dodecahedron. Hexagons (120°) already add to 360° with three, so nothing else is possible.

Every regular polyhedron has a centre that is the same distance from every face (the inradius \(r\)), every edge midpoint (the midradius) and every vertex (the circumradius \(R\)).

Euler's formula

For every one of them, vertices − edges + faces = 2. For the cube: 8 − 12 + 6 = 2.

Deriving the formulas

Surface area

An equilateral triangle of side \(a\) has area \(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\). The tetrahedron has 4 of them: \(\sqrt{3}a^2\); the octahedron 8: \(2\sqrt{3}a^2\); the icosahedron 20: \(5\sqrt{3}a^2\).

Volume of the tetrahedron

A regular tetrahedron is a triangular pyramid. Its base area is \(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\); the apex lies above the centroid of the base, which is \(\tfrac{a}{\sqrt{3}}\) from each corner, so the height is \(h = \sqrt{a^2 - \tfrac{a^2}{3}} = a\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\). Then

\[ V = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\cdot a\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{12}a^3. \]

A check that works for all five

Join the centre to every face: the solid splits into pyramids, one per face, each with height \(r\). So \(V = \tfrac{1}{3}rA\). The calculator uses this to confirm every result.

Complete worked example

Problem

A regular tetrahedron has edges of 6 cm. Find its surface area, volume and radii.

Step 1: Surface area

\[ A = \sqrt{3}(6)^2 = 36\sqrt{3} \approx 62.354\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{12}(6)^3 = \frac{216\sqrt{2}}{12} = 18\sqrt{2} \approx 25.456\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 3: Radii

\[ r = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{12}(6) = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} \approx 1.2247, \qquad R = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{4}(6) = \frac{3\sqrt{6}}{2} \approx 3.6742 \]

Step 4: Check

\(\tfrac{1}{3}rA = \tfrac{1}{3}(1.2247)(62.354) \approx 25.456\) ✓.

Second worked example: edge from the volume

Problem

A dodecahedral paperweight has a volume of 1000 cm³. How long are its edges?

Step 1: Solve for the edge

\[ a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{V}{(15 + 7\sqrt{5})/4}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{1000}{7.6631}} = \sqrt[3]{130.495} \approx 5.0722\ \text{cm} \]

Step 2: Surface area

\[ A = 3\sqrt{25 + 10\sqrt{5}}\,(5.0722)^2 \approx 20.6457 \times 25.727 \approx 531.16\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Practical applications

  • Gaming dice: d4, d6, d8, d12 and d20 are the five Platonic solids.
  • Chemistry: methane is tetrahedral; many crystals and molecules are octahedral or icosahedral.
  • Architecture: geodesic domes are based on the icosahedron.
  • Biology: many virus shells are icosahedral.

Common mistakes

  1. Confusing the inradius and circumradius. The inradius touches the faces; the circumradius reaches the corners.
  2. Using a square pyramid for an octahedron. An octahedron is two square pyramids base to base, so its volume is twice one pyramid's.
  3. Rounding √5 too early in dodecahedron and icosahedron formulas.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The solids are regular: all edges equal and all faces identical regular polygons. A box with unequal sides is a prism, and a pyramid on a square base is a pyramid.
  • Exact radical forms are shown for the tetrahedron, cube and octahedron when the edge is a whole number or fraction.

Frequently asked questions

How many regular polyhedrons are there?

Exactly five: the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron.

What is the volume of a regular tetrahedron?

V = a³√2/12, where a is the edge.

What is the difference between inradius and circumradius?

The inradius is the radius of the largest sphere inside the solid, touching every face. The circumradius is the radius of the sphere through every vertex.

Why are there no regular polyhedrons with hexagon faces?

Three hexagons meeting at a point already make 360°, which lies flat, so they tile a plane instead of closing into a solid.
