Work, mixture and clock problems

These three word-problem types share one idea: find the quantity that is conserved or that changes at a constant rate, and write an equation for it. In work problems it is the fraction of the job done per hour; in mixtures, the amount of pure substance; on a clock, the angle each hand turns per minute.

Try changing the input

Give B a longer time than A and set B to "works against A", like a drain. If the drain empties faster than the tap fills, the tank is never full.

What this calculator does

  • Work: the time for two workers, pipes or machines together, or with one working against the other, with an optional head start for A.
  • Mixture: the concentration of a mixture, or how much of a second solution to add to reach a target.
  • Clock angle: the angle between the hands at any time.
  • Clock times: when, between h and h + 1 o'clock, the hands are together, opposite or at a given angle.

The ideas behind it

Work

If A finishes a job in \(t_A\) hours, A does \(\tfrac{1}{t_A}\) of it each hour. Rates add (times do not), so working together the rate is \(\tfrac{1}{t_A} + \tfrac{1}{t_B}\).

Mixtures

The amount of pure substance in a solution is volume × concentration. Mixing conserves it: pure before = pure after.

Clocks

The minute hand turns 6° per minute; the hour hand turns 30° per hour, which is 0.5° per minute. So the minute hand gains \(5.5^\circ\) per minute on the hour hand.

Why the hour hand moves

At 3:30 the hour hand is halfway between 3 and 4, at 105°, not at 90°. Forgetting this is the most common clock mistake.

Deriving the clock formulas

Measure both hands clockwise from 12. At \(h\):\(m\), the hour hand is at \(30h + 0.5m\) degrees and the minute hand at \(6m\) degrees. Their difference is

\[ \theta = |30h + 0.5m - 6m| = |30h - 5.5m|. \]

To find when the angle is \(\theta\), solve \(30h - 5.5m = \pm\theta\) for \(m\): \(m = \tfrac{30h \mp \theta}{5.5} = \tfrac{2}{11}(30h \mp \theta)\). Keep the values from 0 to 60 minutes.

Mixture equation

Pure substance: \(x_1c_1 + x_2c_2 = (x_1 + x_2)c\). Solving for \(c\) or for \(x_2\) gives the two mixture formulas.

Complete worked example

Problem

A can do a job in 10 hours and B in 15 hours. A works alone for 2 hours, then B joins. How long do they work together, and what is the total time?

Step 1: Rates

\[ r_A = \tfrac{1}{10}, \qquad r_B = \tfrac{1}{15}, \qquad r_A + r_B = \tfrac{3}{30} + \tfrac{2}{30} = \tfrac{1}{6} \]

Step 2: Work done by A alone

\[ 2 \times \tfrac{1}{10} = \tfrac{1}{5}, \quad \text{leaving } \tfrac{4}{5} \]

Step 3: Time together

\[ t = \frac{4/5}{1/6} = \frac{24}{5} = 4.8 \text{ hours}, \qquad \text{total } 2 + 4.8 = 6.8 \text{ hours} \]

Check

\(\tfrac{1}{5} + 4.8 \times \tfrac{1}{6} = 0.2 + 0.8 = 1\) whole job. ✓

Second worked example: the clock

Part 1: The angle at 3:40

\[ \text{hour hand } 30(3) + 0.5(40) = 110^\circ, \qquad \text{minute hand } 6(40) = 240^\circ \]

The angle is \(|110 - 240| = 130^\circ\). The formula agrees: \(|90 - 220| = 130^\circ\).

Part 2: When are the hands at right angles after 4:00?

\[ m = \tfrac{2}{11}(120 - 90) = 5\tfrac{5}{11}, \qquad m = \tfrac{2}{11}(120 + 90) = 38\tfrac{2}{11} \]

At about 4:05:27 and 4:38:11.

Practical applications

  • Pumps and reservoirs: filling a tank with an inflow and an outflow.
  • Production planning: how long two machines or crews take together.
  • Chemistry and medicine: diluting a solution or mixing to a target concentration.
  • Concrete and fuels: blending materials of different grades.
  • Board exams: all three are standard problem types in engineering licensure reviews.

Common mistakes

  1. Adding times instead of rates. Two pipes taking 6 h and 4 h do not take 10 h, or 5 h, together: they take 2.4 h.
  2. Averaging concentrations. Weight each concentration by its amount.
  3. Leaving the hour hand at the hour mark. It moves 0.5° per minute.
  4. Reporting a reflex angle. If the difference is over 180°, the angle between the hands is 360° minus it.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Workers keep a constant rate and do not get in each other's way.
  • Mixed volumes simply add (no shrinkage on mixing).
  • The clock is an ordinary 12-hour analog clock with hands that move smoothly.
  • All results are exact fractions, with decimals alongside.

Frequently asked questions

How do I solve a work problem?

Turn each time into a rate (1/time), add the rates, and take the reciprocal of the total rate to get the time together.

How do I find the angle between clock hands?

Use θ = |30h − 5.5m|. If the result is more than 180°, subtract it from 360°.

How often do the hands of a clock overlap?

11 times in 12 hours, every 12/11 hours (about 65.45 minutes), because the minute hand gains 360° on the hour hand in that time.

How do I solve a mixture problem?

Write an equation for the pure substance: amount × concentration before mixing equals amount × concentration after.
