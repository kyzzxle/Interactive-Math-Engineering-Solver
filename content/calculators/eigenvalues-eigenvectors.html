What the eigenvalue calculator does

An eigenvector of a matrix A is a non-zero vector that A only stretches or shrinks, without turning: \(Av = \lambda v\). The stretch factor \(\lambda\) is its eigenvalue.

Eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial det(A − λI) = 0; each eigenvector is then found by solving (A − λI)v = 0. They describe vibration modes, stability of systems and long-term behaviour of repeated processes.

Try it

For a 2 × 2 matrix the diagram shows the unit circle turning into an ellipse — along the eigenvector lines there is no rotation.

Formulas and variables

\[ A\,v = \lambda v \iff \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 \]\[ 2 \times 2:\ \lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr}A)\lambda + \det A = 0 \qquad \sum \lambda = \operatorname{tr}A,\ \prod \lambda = \det A \]
SymbolMeaning
\(\lambda\)Eigenvalue
\(v\)Eigenvector
\(\operatorname{tr}A\)Trace: sum of the diagonal entries

Worked example

Eigenvalues of A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]]

Step 1 — Characteristic equation

\[ \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = 2,\ 5 \]

Step 2 — Eigenvector for λ = 2

\[ (A - 2I)v = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}v = 0 \Rightarrow v = (1,\ -2) \]

Step 3 — Eigenvector for λ = 5

\[ \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}v = 0 \Rightarrow v = (1,\ 1) \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Real 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 matrices with entries of at most 9 digits.
  • Eigenvectors are exact for rational eigenvalues and unit-length decimals otherwise; complex eigenvalues are shown without eigenvectors.

Common mistakes

  • Accepting the zero vector as an eigenvector.
  • Forgetting that any non-zero multiple of an eigenvector is also an eigenvector.
