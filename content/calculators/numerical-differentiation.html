Numerical differentiation

When a function is known only through measurements or a computer simulation, its derivative must be estimated from nearby values. Finite-difference formulas approximate the slope of the curve by the slope of a short secant line. This calculator applies the forward, backward or central difference, compares the estimate with the exact derivative, and shows how the error shrinks as the step size \(h\) decreases.

Try changing the input

Look at the error table: for each tenfold reduction in \(h\), the forward and backward errors drop by about 10 but the central error by about 100.

Supported finite-difference formulas

MethodFormulaTruncation error
Forward\(f'(x) \approx \dfrac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}\)\(O(h)\)
Backward\(f'(x) \approx \dfrac{f(x) - f(x - h)}{h}\)\(O(h)\)
Central\(f'(x) \approx \dfrac{f(x + h) - f(x - h)}{2h}\)\(O(h^2)\)

The orders come from Taylor's theorem: \(f(x \pm h) = f(x) \pm hf'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x) \pm \cdots\). In the central difference the \(h^2\) terms cancel, leaving an error proportional to \(h^2\).

Worked example

Central difference for \(\sin x\) at \(x = 1\), \(h = 0.1\)

Step 1 — Evaluate

\[ \sin 1.1 \approx 0.8912073601, \qquad \sin 0.9 \approx 0.7833269096 \]

Step 2 — Difference quotient

\[ f'(1) \approx \frac{0.8912073601 - 0.7833269096}{0.2} \approx 0.5394022522 \]

Step 3 — Compare

The exact value is \(\cos 1 \approx 0.5403023059\), so the error is about \(9.0 \times 10^{-4}\).

Assumptions and limits

  • \(f\) must be defined at every point the formula uses and smooth near \(x\).
  • \(h\) must be between \(10^{-12}\) and 1000. Extremely small \(h\) is counter-productive: subtracting nearly equal numbers loses digits (round-off error), so the total error rises again below about \(h = 10^{-8}\).
  • The exact derivative used for comparison is computed symbolically.
