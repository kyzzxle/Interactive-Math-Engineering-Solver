What the Fourier series calculator does

A Fourier series writes a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines whose frequencies are whole multiples of the basic frequency. Even a square wave, with its sudden jumps, can be built from smooth waves.

The coefficients measure how much of each harmonic the signal contains — the basis of audio processing, vibration analysis and the theory of heat flow.

Try it

Raise N in the explorer for the square wave: the fit improves, but an overshoot of about 9% near each jump never goes away (the Gibbs phenomenon).

Formulas and variables

\[ f(t) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n\cos n\omega_0 t + b_n\sin n\omega_0 t\right),\quad \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} \]\[ a_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t)\cos(n\omega_0 t)\,dt \qquad b_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t)\sin(n\omega_0 t)\,dt \]
SymbolMeaning
\(T,\ \omega_0\)Period and fundamental angular frequency
\(a_n,\ b_n\)Cosine and sine coefficients
\(N\)Number of harmonics in a partial sum

Odd functions (square, sawtooth) have only sine terms; even functions (triangle) only cosine terms.

Worked example

Square wave of amplitude A

Step 1 — Symmetry

The square wave is odd, so every aₙ is 0.

Step 2 — Sine coefficients

\[ b_n = \frac{2}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} f(t)\sin(n\omega_0 t)\,dt = \begin{cases} \frac{4A}{n\pi}, & n \text{ odd} \\ 0, & n \text{ even} \end{cases} \]

Step 3 — Series

\[ f(t) = \frac{4A}{\pi}\left(\sin\omega_0 t + \frac{\sin 3\omega_0 t}{3} + \frac{\sin 5\omega_0 t}{5} + \cdots\right) \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Three standard waves: square, sawtooth and triangle, each with a chosen amplitude and period.
  • Up to 100 harmonics in the partial sum.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the even harmonics vanish for the square and triangle waves.
  • Expecting the partial sums to converge uniformly at a jump (they overshoot).
