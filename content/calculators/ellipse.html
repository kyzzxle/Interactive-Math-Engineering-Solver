What is an ellipse?

An ellipse is the set of all points whose distances to two fixed points, the foci, add up to the same constant. Tie a loop of string around two pins and pull it tight with a pencil: the curve you trace is an ellipse. It looks like a stretched circle, with a long major axis and a short minor axis crossing at its centre.

Try changing the input

Make \(a\) and \(b\) equal. The foci move to the centre, the eccentricity becomes 0 and the ellipse is a circle.

The geometry behind it

Call the longer semi-axis \(p\) and the shorter one \(q\). At the end of the minor axis, a point is the same distance from each focus, and the two distances add to \(2p\), so each is \(p\). That makes a right triangle with legs \(q\) and \(c\) and hypotenuse \(p\):

\[ c^2 = p^2 - q^2. \]

The eccentricity \(e = c/p\) measures how stretched the ellipse is: 0 for a circle, approaching 1 for a very flat ellipse.

Orbits

Planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus (Kepler's first law). Earth's orbit has \(e \approx 0.017\), so it is almost a circle.

Deriving the equation

Place the foci at \((\pm c, 0)\) and let the distances add to \(2a\):

\[ \sqrt{(x + c)^2 + y^2} + \sqrt{(x - c)^2 + y^2} = 2a. \]

Move one root across, square, simplify, and square again. The result is \((a^2 - c^2)x^2 + a^2y^2 = a^2(a^2 - c^2)\). Writing \(b^2 = a^2 - c^2\) and dividing by \(a^2b^2\):

\[ \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1. \]

Shifting the centre to \((h, k)\) replaces \(x\) by \(x - h\) and \(y\) by \(y - k\).

Complete worked example

Problem

An ellipse has centre \((2, -1)\), horizontal semi-axis 5 and vertical semi-axis 3. Find its equation, foci and eccentricity.

Step 1: Standard form

\[ \frac{(x - 2)^2}{25} + \frac{(y + 1)^2}{9} = 1 \]

Step 2: Focal distance

\(a > b\), so the major axis is horizontal: \(c^2 = 25 - 9 = 16\), \(c = 4\).

Step 3: Key points

\[ \text{vertices } (-3, -1),\ (7, -1) \qquad \text{co-vertices } (2, 2),\ (2, -4) \qquad \text{foci } (-2, -1),\ (6, -1) \]

Step 4: Shape

\[ e = \tfrac{4}{5} = 0.8, \qquad \text{latus rectum } \tfrac{2(9)}{5} = \tfrac{18}{5}, \qquad \text{directrices } x = 2 \pm \tfrac{25}{4} \]

Step 5: General form

Multiply by 225 and expand: \(9x^2 + 25y^2 - 36x + 50y - 164 = 0\). The area is \(15\pi \approx 47.12\).

Check

The co-vertex \((2, 2)\) is \(\sqrt{16 + 9} = 5\) from each focus, and \(5 + 5 = 10 = 2a\). ✓

Second worked example: from general form

Problem

Identify the ellipse \(4x^2 + 9y^2 - 16x + 18y - 11 = 0\).

Step 1: Group and factor

\[ 4(x^2 - 4x) + 9(y^2 + 2y) = 11 \]

Step 2: Complete the squares

\[ 4(x - 2)^2 - 16 + 9(y + 1)^2 - 9 = 11 \;\Rightarrow\; 4(x - 2)^2 + 9(y + 1)^2 = 36 \]

Step 3: Divide by 36

\[ \frac{(x - 2)^2}{9} + \frac{(y + 1)^2}{4} = 1 \]

So \(h = 2\), \(k = -1\), \(a = 3\), \(b = 2\): enter these in the calculator.

Step 4: Foci

\[ c = \sqrt{9 - 4} = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \text{foci } \left(2 \pm \sqrt{5},\ -1\right), \qquad e = \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \approx 0.745 \]

Practical applications

  • Orbits: planets, moons and satellites follow ellipses.
  • Whispering galleries and lithotripsy: sound or shock waves from one focus reflect to the other.
  • Arches and bridges: semi-elliptical arches give wide, low openings.
  • Gears and cams: elliptical profiles give a varying speed ratio.

Common mistakes

  1. Using \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\). That is the hyperbola. For an ellipse, subtract the smaller square from the larger.
  2. Reading \(a^2\) as \(a\). In \(\tfrac{x^2}{25}\), the semi-axis is 5.
  3. Putting the foci on the minor axis. The foci always lie on the longer axis.
  4. Expecting an exact perimeter. There is no simple exact formula; Ramanujan's approximation is used.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The axes of the ellipse are parallel to the coordinate axes. For tilted ellipses, use the general conic calculator.
  • Both semi-axes must be positive. Fractions are allowed and the results are exact.
  • The perimeter is an approximation (error below 0.001% unless the ellipse is very flat, and never more than about 0.4%).

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the foci of an ellipse?

Compute c² = (larger semi-axis)² − (smaller semi-axis)². The foci are c units from the centre along the major axis.

What is the eccentricity of an ellipse?

e = c divided by the major semi-axis. It is 0 for a circle and approaches 1 as the ellipse gets flatter.

What is the area of an ellipse?

πab, where a and b are the two semi-axes.

Is a circle an ellipse?

Yes: a circle is an ellipse with equal semi-axes, so both foci are at the centre and e = 0.
