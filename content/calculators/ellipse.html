What the ellipse calculator does

An ellipse is the set of points whose distances to two fixed points, the foci, add up to a constant — the length of the major axis. Pin a loop of string at the foci and trace around with a pencil, and you draw an ellipse.

In standard form the centre is \((h, k)\), the horizontal semi-axis is \(a\) and the vertical one \(b\). The longer of the two is the major semi-axis; the foci lie on that axis at distance \(c\) from the centre.

Try it

In the explorer, make a = b: the foci merge at the centre and the ellipse becomes a circle (e = 0).

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1 \]\[ c^2 = |a^2 - b^2| \qquad e = \frac{c}{\max(a, b)} \qquad \text{latus rectum} = \frac{2\min(a,b)^2}{\max(a,b)} \]
SymbolMeaning
\((h, k)\)Centre
\(a,\ b\)Horizontal and vertical semi-axes
\(c\)Centre-to-focus distance
\(e\)Eccentricity, 0 ≤ e < 1

The area is πab. The perimeter has no elementary formula; Ramanujan’s approximation is very accurate.

Worked example

Centre (2, −1), a = 5, b = 3

Step 1 — Equation

\[ \frac{(x - 2)^2}{25} + \frac{(y + 1)^2}{9} = 1 \]

Step 2 — Focal distance

\[ c^2 = 25 - 9 = 16,\quad c = 4 \]

Step 3 — Foci and eccentricity

\[ (2 \pm 4,\ -1) = (-2, -1),\ (6, -1),\qquad e = \tfrac{4}{5} \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • a and b must be positive. If a = b the ellipse is a circle.
  • Only axis-aligned ellipses (no rotation, no xy term) are supported.

Common mistakes

  • Using c² = a² + b² (that is the hyperbola).
  • Assuming a is always the major semi-axis: here a is horizontal, so check which is larger.
