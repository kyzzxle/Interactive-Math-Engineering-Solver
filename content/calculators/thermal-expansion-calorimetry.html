What the thermal physics calculator does

Most materials expand when heated: the change in length is proportional to the original length and to the temperature change. That is why bridges have expansion joints and rails have gaps.

Calorimetry tracks heat: warming a mass m by ΔT needs Q = mcΔT, where c is the specific heat capacity. When hot and cold objects are mixed in an insulated container, the heat lost by one equals the heat gained by the other.

Try it

Water’s specific heat is about 4.6 times aluminium’s — which is why the sea warms so slowly.

Formulas and variables

\[ \Delta L = \alpha L_0 \Delta T \qquad \Delta V = \beta V_0 \Delta T\ \ (\beta \approx 3\alpha \text{ for solids}) \]\[ Q = m c \Delta T \qquad T_f = \frac{m_1 c_1 T_1 + m_2 c_2 T_2}{m_1 c_1 + m_2 c_2} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(\alpha,\ \beta\)Linear and volume expansion coefficients (1/K)
\(c\)Specific heat capacity (J/(kg K))
\(\Delta T\)Temperature change (1 °C = 1 K)
\(Q\)Heat (J)

Worked example

0.2 kg of copper at 90 °C dropped into 0.5 kg of water at 20 °C

Step 1 — Heat capacities

\[ m_1c_1 = 0.2(385) = 77,\quad m_2c_2 = 0.5(4186) = 2093\ \text{J/K} \]

Step 2 — Balance

\[ T_f = \frac{77(90) + 2093(20)}{77 + 2093} \approx 22.48\ ^\circ\text{C} \]

Step 3 — Check

Copper gives out \(77(90 - 22.48) \approx 5199\) J; water takes in \(2093(22.48 - 20) \approx 5199\) J.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • No phase changes (melting, boiling). Material constants are typical room-temperature values.
  • Mixing assumes an insulated container that absorbs no heat.

Common mistakes

  • Using °C temperatures in the expansion formula instead of the change ΔT.
  • Forgetting the latent heat when ice melts or water boils.
