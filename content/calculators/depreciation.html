What the depreciation calculator does

Machines, vehicles and buildings lose value as they are used. Depreciation spreads that loss, the first cost minus the salvage value, over the asset's useful life. The value left on the records at any time is the book value.

The calculator finds the depreciation charge for a chosen year, the accumulated depreciation, the book value and a full schedule by five methods:

  • Straight line: the same amount every year.
  • Sinking fund: equal yearly deposits that, with interest, add up to \(C - S\). Smaller charges early, larger later.
  • Declining balance (Matheson): a fixed percentage of the book value each year, chosen so the book value ends exactly at \(S\).
  • Double declining balance: twice the straight-line rate, applied to the book value.
  • Sum of the years' digits (SYD): fractions \(n/\text{SYD}, (n-1)/\text{SYD}, \dots\) of \(C - S\), largest first.

Try it

In the explorer, compare the curves. The declining-balance and SYD methods fall fastest early on, which is why they are called accelerated methods. All except double declining balance end exactly at the salvage value.

Formulas and variables

\[ \text{Straight line:}\quad d = \frac{C - S}{n}, \qquad BV_m = C - m\,d \]
\[ \text{Sinking fund:}\quad d = (C - S)\,\frac{i}{(1 + i)^{n} - 1}, \qquad D_m = d\,\frac{(1 + i)^{m} - 1}{i} \]
\[ \text{Declining balance:}\quad k = 1 - \sqrt[n]{\frac{S}{C}}, \qquad BV_m = C\,(1 - k)^{m} \]
\[ \text{SYD:}\quad d_m = (C - S)\,\frac{n - m + 1}{n(n + 1)/2} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(C\)First cost (purchase price plus installation)
\(S\)Salvage value at the end of the life
\(n\)Useful life in years
\(m\)The year being considered
\(d_m,\ D_m\)Depreciation in year \(m\), and accumulated depreciation after \(m\) years
\(BV_m\)Book value at the end of year \(m\): \(C - D_m\)
\(k,\ i\)Declining-balance rate; interest rate for the sinking fund method

Worked example

A machine costs 800,000, lasts 10 years and has a salvage value of 80,000. Find its book value after 4 years.

Straight line

\[ d = \frac{800{,}000 - 80{,}000}{10} = 72{,}000, \qquad BV_4 = 800{,}000 - 4(72{,}000) = 512{,}000 \]

Declining balance (Matheson)

\[ k = 1 - \sqrt[10]{\frac{80{,}000}{800{,}000}} \approx 0.205672, \qquad BV_4 = 800{,}000\,(0.794328)^4 \approx 318{,}485.74 \]

Sum of the years' digits

\[ \text{SYD} = \frac{10 \times 11}{2} = 55, \qquad D_4 = 720{,}000 \times \frac{10 + 9 + 8 + 7}{55} \approx 445{,}090.91, \qquad BV_4 \approx 354{,}909.09 \]

The same machine can show very different book values depending on the method, which is why the method must be stated.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The life is a whole number of years and depreciation is charged at the end of each year.
  • The Matheson formula needs a salvage value above 0. Double declining balance ignores the salvage value, so its final book value is usually not exactly \(S\).
  • Tax rules (such as MACRS in the United States or local tax codes) may prescribe their own methods and lives; these are not modelled here.
  • The sinking fund deposit is an annuity whose future worth is \(C - S\).
