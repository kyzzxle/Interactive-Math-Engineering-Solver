Variance and standard deviation

The variance measures how far data values typically fall from the mean, using squared distances; the standard deviation is its square root, in the same units as the data. There are two versions, and choosing the right one matters: use the population formula when you have every member of the group, and the sample formula when your data are a sample used to estimate a larger population.

Try changing the input

Switch between sample and population for the same data: the sample standard deviation is always a little larger, because it divides by \(n - 1\) instead of \(n\).

Formulas: population versus sample

PopulationSample
Mean\(\mu = \frac{\sum x_i}{N}\)\(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)
Variance\(\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \mu)^2}{N}\)\(s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}\)
Standard deviation\(\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}\)\(s = \sqrt{s^2}\)

Dividing by \(n - 1\) (Bessel's correction) compensates for the fact that a sample's values sit, on average, slightly closer to their own mean than to the true population mean.

Worked example

Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9

Step 1 — Mean

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{40}{8} = 5 \]

Step 2 — Squared deviations

\[ 9 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 32 \]

Step 3 — Variance and standard deviation

\[ \sigma^2 = \frac{32}{8} = 4,\ \sigma = 2 \qquad s^2 = \frac{32}{7} \approx 4.5714,\ s \approx 2.1381 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • At least 2 values are needed (a sample variance with one value would divide by zero).
  • Squared deviations are summed with exact fractions, avoiding the round-off problems of the "shortcut" formula \(\sum x^2 - n\bar{x}^2\).
  • The deviation table shows up to the first 50 values; results are rounded to 6 decimal places.
