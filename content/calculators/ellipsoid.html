What is an ellipsoid?

An ellipsoid is a sphere stretched or squashed by different amounts in three perpendicular directions. Its size is given by three semi-axes \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\): half its length, width and height, measured from the centre. Every cross-section through the centre is an ellipse.

Special cases have their own names. With all three semi-axes equal it is a sphere. With two equal it is a spheroid: prolate (rugby-ball shaped) when the different axis is longer, oblate (like the Earth or a lentil) when it is shorter.

What this calculator does

  • Computes the volume exactly, \(\tfrac{4}{3}\pi abc\), with an exact multiple of π for whole-number inputs.
  • Computes the surface area to full precision with elliptic integrals (there is no elementary formula for a general ellipsoid).
  • Compares it with Thomsen's approximation, accurate to about 1%, which is easy to use by hand.

The geometry behind it

Take a sphere of radius 1 and stretch it by a factor \(a\) in one direction, \(b\) in the second and \(c\) in the third. Every small cube inside it becomes a box \(a\times b\times c\) times larger, so the volume is multiplied by \(abc\):

\[ V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(1)^3\cdot abc = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi abc. \]

Surface area does not stretch so simply, because different patches of the surface are stretched by different amounts depending on their direction. That is why its formula needs integrals.

Formula breakdown

QuantityFormula
Volume\(V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi abc\)
Exact surface area\(S = 2\pi C^2 + \dfrac{2\pi AB}{\sin\varphi}\left(E(\varphi,k)\sin^2\varphi + F(\varphi,k)\cos^2\varphi\right)\)
with\(A \ge B \ge C,\ \ \cos\varphi = \dfrac{C}{A},\ \ k^2 = \dfrac{A^2(B^2 - C^2)}{B^2(A^2 - C^2)}\)
Thomsen's approximation\(S \approx 4\pi\left(\dfrac{(ab)^p + (ac)^p + (bc)^p}{3}\right)^{1/p},\ p = 1.6075\)

\(F\) and \(E\) are the incomplete elliptic integrals of the first and second kind; the calculator evaluates them numerically.

Deriving the formulas

Volume by slicing

Slice perpendicular to the \(z\)-axis. At height \(z\), the cross-section is an ellipse with semi-axes \(a\sqrt{1 - z^2/c^2}\) and \(b\sqrt{1 - z^2/c^2}\), whose area is \(\pi ab\left(1 - \tfrac{z^2}{c^2}\right)\). Integrating:

\[ V = \int_{-c}^{c} \pi ab\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{c^2}\right)dz = \pi ab\left(2c - \frac{2c}{3}\right) = \frac{4}{3}\pi abc. \]

Spheroids

When two semi-axes are equal, the surface area has a closed form. For a prolate spheroid (\(a = b \lt c\)) with eccentricity \(e = \sqrt{1 - a^2/c^2}\):

\[ S = 2\pi a^2\left(1 + \frac{c}{a e}\arcsin e\right). \]

Complete worked example

Problem

An ellipsoid has semi-axes 5 cm, 3 cm and 2 cm. Find its volume and surface area.

Step 1: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(5)(3)(2) = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(30) = 40\pi \approx 125.66\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2: Surface area by Thomsen's approximation

\[ (ab)^p = 15^{1.6075} \approx 77.73,\quad (ac)^p = 10^{1.6075} \approx 40.50,\quad (bc)^p = 6^{1.6075} \approx 17.82 \] \[ S \approx 4\pi\left(\frac{77.73 + 40.50 + 17.82}{3}\right)^{1/1.6075} \approx 4\pi(10.728) \approx 134.81\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Exact surface area

Evaluating the elliptic integrals gives \(S \approx 134.78\) cm², so the approximation was off by only 0.03%.

Second worked example: an egg-shaped tank

Problem

A water tank is an ellipsoid 4 m long, 2 m wide and 2 m high. How many litres does it hold?

Step 1: Semi-axes

\[ a = 2,\quad b = 1,\quad c = 1\ \text{m} \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(2)(1)(1) = \tfrac{8}{3}\pi \approx 8.378\ \text{m}^3 \approx 8378\ \text{L} \]

Answer

About 8380 litres. Notice the full dimensions had to be halved first.

Practical applications

  • Tank heads and domes. Ellipsoidal ends of pressure vessels.
  • Earth science. The Earth is an oblate spheroid; geodesy uses ellipsoid models.
  • Biology and medicine. Estimating the volume of organs, eggs and tumours from three measurements.
  • Sports. Rugby and American footballs are close to prolate spheroids.

Common mistakes

  1. Using full lengths instead of semi-axes. Halve each full dimension first, or the volume is 8 times too large.
  2. Guessing the area as 4π times something. There is no simple exact area formula; use the calculator or Thomsen's approximation.
  3. Assuming an ellipsoid is an elliptical cylinder. The ends are rounded, not flat.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The ellipsoid is solid and complete. For half of one (an ellipsoidal dome), halve the volume.
  • The surface area is computed numerically to about 10 significant figures.
  • With all semi-axes equal, the results agree with the sphere calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of an ellipsoid?

V = (4/3)πabc, where a, b and c are the three semi-axes.

Is there a simple formula for the surface area?

Not in general: it needs elliptic integrals. Thomsen's approximation, 4π(((ab)^p + (ac)^p + (bc)^p)/3)^(1/p) with p = 1.6075, is within about 1%.

What is the difference between a spheroid and an ellipsoid?

A spheroid is an ellipsoid with two equal semi-axes, so it is symmetric about one axis.

What happens when a = b = c?

The ellipsoid is a sphere of radius a: V = (4/3)πa³ and S = 4πa².
