What the ellipsoid calculator does

An ellipsoid is a sphere stretched by different amounts along three perpendicular directions. Its half-widths along those directions are the semi-axes \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\). When two semi-axes are equal it is a spheroid: oblate (flattened, like the Earth) or prolate (elongated, like a rugby ball).

The volume has a simple exact formula. The surface area does not: for a general ellipsoid it needs elliptic integrals, which this calculator evaluates numerically and compares with Thomsen’s well-known approximation.

Try it

Set all three semi-axes equal in the explorer: the formulas reduce to the sphere’s \(V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\) and \(A = 4\pi r^2\).

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1 \]\[ V = \frac{4}{3}\pi abc \]\[ S = 2\pi C^2 + \frac{2\pi AB}{\sin\varphi}\left(E(\varphi,k)\sin^2\varphi + F(\varphi,k)\cos^2\varphi\right) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a,\ b,\ c\)Semi-axes (half-widths) along three perpendicular directions
\(A \ge B \ge C\)The same semi-axes sorted from largest to smallest
\(\cos\varphi = C/A,\ k^2 = \frac{A^2(B^2 - C^2)}{B^2(A^2 - C^2)}\)Angle and modulus of the elliptic integrals
\(F,\ E\)Incomplete elliptic integrals of the first and second kind

Thomsen’s approximation \(S \approx 4\pi\left(\tfrac{(ab)^p + (ac)^p + (bc)^p}{3}\right)^{1/p}\) with \(p = 1.6075\) is within about 1.06% for every ellipsoid.

Worked example

An ellipsoid with semi-axes 5, 3 and 2 cm

Step 1 — Volume

\[ V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (5)(3)(2) = 40\pi \approx 125.66\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2 — Elliptic-integral parameters

\[ \cos\varphi = \frac{2}{5},\quad k^2 = \frac{25(9 - 4)}{9(25 - 4)} \approx 0.6614 \]

Step 3 — Surface area

Evaluating \(F\) and \(E\) numerically gives \(S \approx 134.78\ \text{cm}^2\). Thomsen’s formula gives about 134.6 cm², within 0.2%.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • All three semi-axes must be positive. Units must match; the volume is in cubic units and the area in square units.
  • The volume is exact (a multiple of π when the inputs are exact). The surface area is numerical, accurate to about 10 significant digits.
  • Semi-axes that differ by more than a factor of a million are rejected because the integral becomes unreliable.

Common mistakes

  • Using full widths (diameters) instead of semi-axes: halve them first.
  • Assuming the surface area is “4π × the average radius squared” — that is only true for a sphere.
