What the polynomial long division calculator does

Polynomial long division works like long division of numbers: divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, and repeat until the remainder’s degree is less than the divisor’s.

It is used to simplify improper rational functions, find slant asymptotes, factor polynomials once a root is known, and prepare for partial fractions.

Try it

Compare the graph of P(x)/D(x) with its quotient: far from the origin they almost coincide.

Formulas and variables

\[ P(x) = D(x)\,Q(x) + R(x),\quad \deg R < \deg D \]\[ \text{Remainder theorem: } P(x) \div (x - c) \text{ leaves } R = P(c) \]
SymbolMeaning
\(P\)Dividend
\(D\)Divisor
\(Q\)Quotient
\(R\)Remainder

Worked example

Divide 2x³ − 3x² + 4x − 5 by x − 2

Step 1

\[ 2x^3 \div x = 2x^2;\quad 2x^2(x - 2) = 2x^3 - 4x^2;\quad \text{remainder } x^2 + 4x - 5 \]

Step 2

\[ x^2 \div x = x;\quad x(x - 2) = x^2 - 2x;\quad \text{remainder } 6x - 5 \]

Step 3

\[ 6x \div x = 6;\quad 6(x - 2) = 6x - 12;\quad \text{remainder } 7 \]

Result

\[ 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 5 = (x - 2)(2x^2 + x + 6) + 7,\qquad P(2) = 7\ \checkmark \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Polynomials up to degree 8; coefficients may be fractions.
  • The divisor cannot be the zero polynomial.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out zero terms (write x⁴ − 1 as x⁴ + 0x³ + 0x² + 0x − 1 when working by hand).
  • Subtracting only the first term of the product instead of the whole product.
