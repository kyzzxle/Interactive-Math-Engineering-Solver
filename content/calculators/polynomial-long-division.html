What is polynomial long division?

Polynomial long division divides one polynomial by another, just as long division divides numbers. The result is a quotient \(Q(x)\) and a remainder \(R(x)\) whose degree is lower than the divisor's, so that \(P(x) = D(x)\,Q(x) + R(x)\). When the remainder is 0, the divisor is a factor.

Try changing the input

Divide \(x^4 - 1\) by \(x^2 + 1\). The remainder is 0, so \(x^4 - 1 = (x^2 + 1)(x^2 - 1)\).

The idea behind it

Each cycle removes the highest power of what is left:

  1. Divide the leading term of the current remainder by the leading term of the divisor: that is the next term of the quotient.
  2. Multiply the whole divisor by that term.
  3. Subtract the product. The leading terms cancel, so the degree drops.

Stop when the remainder's degree is less than the divisor's.

The remainder theorem

Dividing by \(x - c\) leaves the remainder \(P(c)\). So \(x - c\) is a factor exactly when \(P(c) = 0\).

Formula breakdown

\[ P(x) = D(x)\,Q(x) + R(x), \qquad \deg R \lt \deg D \]
IdeaStatement
Fraction form\(\tfrac{P(x)}{D(x)} = Q(x) + \tfrac{R(x)}{D(x)}\)
Remainder theorem\(P(x) \div (x - c)\) leaves \(P(c)\)

Synthetic division and the remainder theorem

For a divisor \(x - c\), long division can be shortened to synthetic division: bring down the leading coefficient, multiply by \(c\), add to the next coefficient, and repeat. The last number is the remainder.

Why does it equal \(P(c)\)? Substitute \(x = c\) into \(P(x) = (x - c)Q(x) + R\): the first term vanishes, leaving \(P(c) = R\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Divide \(2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 5\) by \(x - 2\).

Step 1

\(\tfrac{2x^3}{x} = 2x^2\). Multiply: \(2x^3 - 4x^2\). Subtract: \(x^2 + 4x - 5\).

Step 2

\(\tfrac{x^2}{x} = x\). Multiply: \(x^2 - 2x\). Subtract: \(6x - 5\).

Step 3

\(\tfrac{6x}{x} = 6\). Multiply: \(6x - 12\). Subtract: \(7\).

Result

\[ Q(x) = 2x^2 + x + 6, \qquad R = 7 \]

Check

Remainder theorem: \(P(2) = 16 - 12 + 8 - 5 = 7\). ✓

Practical applications

  • Factoring: once one root is known, division gives the remaining factor.
  • Improper fractions: splitting off the polynomial part before partial fractions or integration.
  • Slant asymptotes: the quotient of a rational function shows its long-run behaviour.
  • Error-detecting codes: cyclic redundancy checks use polynomial division.

Common mistakes

  1. Missing zero terms. Write \(x^4 - 1\) as \(x^4 + 0x^3 + 0x^2 + 0x - 1\).
  2. Subtracting only the first term. Subtract the whole product; change every sign.
  3. Wrong sign in synthetic division. For \(x + 3\), use \(c = -3\).
  4. Stopping too early or too late. Stop exactly when the remainder's degree is lower than the divisor's.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Polynomials in \(x\) up to degree 8 with rational coefficients; the working is exact.
  • The divisor cannot be 0.

Frequently asked questions

How do I divide polynomials?

Repeat: divide the leading terms, multiply the divisor by the result, subtract. Stop when the remainder has a lower degree than the divisor.

What is the remainder theorem?

The remainder when P(x) is divided by x − c equals P(c).

When can I use synthetic division?

When the divisor is linear with leading coefficient 1, of the form x − c.

What does a remainder of 0 mean?

The divisor is a factor of the dividend: P(x) = D(x)·Q(x) exactly.
