How do you find the area of a polygon from its coordinates?

If you know the coordinates of every corner of a polygon, you can find its area without measuring any heights or splitting it into triangles by hand. The shoelace formula (also called the surveyor's formula or Gauss's area formula) cross-multiplies the coordinates of neighbouring vertices, adds the results and halves them.

Try changing the input

Enter the same vertices in the reverse order. The area stays the same, but the sum changes sign: positive means counter-clockwise, negative means clockwise.

What this calculator does

  • Takes 3 to 30 vertices, listed in order around the polygon.
  • Shows every shoelace term in a table and adds them.
  • Gives the exact area, the perimeter, the centroid and the direction the vertices go round.
  • Warns if two sides cross, because the formula then does not give the enclosed area.

The geometry behind it

Each side \(P_iP_{i+1}\) forms a triangle with the origin. The quantity \(\tfrac{1}{2}(x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i)\) is that triangle's signed area: positive when the side runs counter-clockwise around the origin, negative when it runs clockwise.

Going all the way round the polygon, the triangles outside the polygon are added once and subtracted once, so they cancel. What is left is exactly the area of the polygon, with a sign that shows the direction of travel.

Why the name "shoelace"

Write the coordinates in two columns and draw the diagonal products: they criss-cross like the laces of a shoe.

Deriving the formula

The area of the triangle with vertices \(O(0, 0)\), \(P_i\) and \(P_{i+1}\) is half the cross product of the vectors \(\vec{OP_i}\) and \(\vec{OP_{i+1}}\):

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\left(x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i\right). \]

Fan out from the origin to every side and add the signed areas. For a simple polygon the result is \(\pm A\) whether the origin is inside or outside, because every region outside the polygon is covered once forwards and once backwards. The triangle formula in the table is the case \(n = 3\), expanded.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the area of the quadrilateral with vertices \((0, 0)\), \((4, 0)\), \((4, 3)\), \((0, 5)\).

Step 1: Cross-multiply neighbouring vertices

Side\(x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i\)
1 → 2\(0 \cdot 0 - 4 \cdot 0 = 0\)
2 → 3\(4 \cdot 3 - 4 \cdot 0 = 12\)
3 → 4\(4 \cdot 5 - 0 \cdot 3 = 20\)
4 → 1\(0 \cdot 0 - 0 \cdot 5 = 0\)

Step 2: Add and halve

\[ A = \tfrac{1}{2}\,|0 + 12 + 20 + 0| = 16 \]

Check

The shape is a trapezoid with parallel vertical sides 3 and 5, a distance 4 apart: \(\tfrac{3 + 5}{2} \times 4 = 16\). ✓ The sum was positive, so the vertices go counter-clockwise.

Second worked example: a triangle and its centroid

Problem

Find the area and centroid of the triangle \((1, 1)\), \((6, 2)\), \((3, 7)\).

Step 1: Shoelace terms

\[ 1 \cdot 2 - 6 \cdot 1 = -4, \qquad 6 \cdot 7 - 3 \cdot 2 = 36, \qquad 3 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 7 = -4 \]

Step 2: Area

\[ A = \tfrac{1}{2}\,|-4 + 36 - 4| = \tfrac{1}{2}(28) = 14 \]

Step 3: Centroid

For a triangle the centroid is the average of the vertices:

\[ \left(\frac{1 + 6 + 3}{3},\ \frac{1 + 2 + 7}{3}\right) = \left(\tfrac{10}{3},\ \tfrac{10}{3}\right) \]

Check

The triangle formula gives the same area:

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}|1(2 - 7) + 6(7 - 1) + 3(1 - 2)| = \tfrac{1}{2}|{-5} + 36 - 3| = 14\ \checkmark \]

Practical applications

  • Land surveying: the area of a lot from the coordinates of its corners in a traverse.
  • Civil works: cross-section areas for earthwork volumes.
  • GIS and mapping: areas of parcels, lakes and districts stored as polygons.
  • Structural design: areas and centroids of plate shapes and sections.

Common mistakes

  1. Vertices out of order. They must go around the boundary. Jumping across makes the sides cross.
  2. Forgetting to wrap round. The last term pairs the last vertex with the first.
  3. Forgetting to halve. The sum is twice the area.
  4. Keeping a negative sign. A negative sum just means clockwise order; the area is its absolute value.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The polygon is simple: its sides do not cross. The calculator warns if they do.
  • Vertices can be listed clockwise or counter-clockwise, starting anywhere.
  • Coordinates may be fractions; the area and centroid are exact. The perimeter is rounded.
  • Between 3 and 30 vertices.

Frequently asked questions

What is the shoelace formula?

A = ½|Σ(xᵢyᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁yᵢ)|, where the vertices are listed in order and the last one wraps round to the first.

Does it matter which vertex I start with?

No. Any starting vertex and either direction give the same area, as long as you go around the boundary in order.

Why did I get a negative answer?

The vertices were listed clockwise. Take the absolute value; the size of the area is unchanged.

Does it work for concave polygons?

Yes, for any polygon whose sides do not cross, convex or concave.
