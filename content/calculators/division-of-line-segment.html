What is the division of a line segment?

Given two points \(A\) and \(B\), a point \(P\) on the line through them divides the segment \(AB\) in the ratio \(r_1 : r_2\) when \(AP : PB = r_1 : r_2\). If \(P\) lies between \(A\) and \(B\) the division is internal; if \(P\) lies on the line beyond \(A\) or \(B\), it is external. The midpoint is the special case of internal division in the ratio 1 : 1.

What this calculator does

  • Point from a ratio: finds the point dividing \(AB\) internally or externally in \(r_1 : r_2\).
  • Ratio from a point: checks that \(P\) is on line \(AB\) and finds \(AP : PB\), saying whether the division is internal or external.
  • Centroid: finds the centroid of a triangle and confirms it divides each median 2 : 1.

All coordinates can be whole numbers, decimals or fractions, and the answers are exact fractions.

The geometry behind it

Moving from \(A\) to \(B\), the \(x\)- and \(y\)-coordinates change steadily. A point that is a fraction \(t\) of the way along has coordinates \(A + t(B - A)\). If \(AP : PB = r_1 : r_2\), then \(t = \tfrac{r_1}{r_1 + r_2}\), which gives the section formula.

Cross the weights

In the section formula, \(A\) is multiplied by \(r_2\) and \(B\) by \(r_1\). The point is pulled towards the endpoint with the larger weight, so the smaller part of the ratio is next to it.

Deriving the formulas

Drop perpendiculars from \(A\), \(P\) and \(B\) to the \(x\)-axis. By similar triangles, the horizontal distances are in the same ratio as \(AP : PB\):

\[ \frac{x - x_1}{x_2 - x} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} \;\Longrightarrow\; r_2(x - x_1) = r_1(x_2 - x) \;\Longrightarrow\; x = \frac{r_2 x_1 + r_1 x_2}{r_1 + r_2}. \]

The \(y\)-coordinate follows the same way. For external division, \(P\) is outside the segment, so one of the distances changes sign; replacing \(r_2\) by \(-r_2\) gives the external formula.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the point dividing \(A(-2, 3)\) and \(B(8, -2)\) internally in the ratio 2 : 3.

Step 1: x-coordinate

\[ x = \frac{3(-2) + 2(8)}{2 + 3} = \frac{-6 + 16}{5} = 2 \]

Step 2: y-coordinate

\[ y = \frac{3(3) + 2(-2)}{5} = \frac{9 - 4}{5} = 1 \]

Step 3: Check

\(t = \tfrac{2}{5}\): \(x = -2 + \tfrac{2}{5}(10) = 2\) and \(y = 3 + \tfrac{2}{5}(-5) = 1\) ✓. So \(P = (2, 1)\).

Second worked example: external division and the ratio

External division

Divide \(A(1, 2)\), \(B(4, 5)\) externally in the ratio 3 : 1.

\[ x = \frac{3(4) - 1(1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{11}{2}, \qquad y = \frac{3(5) - 1(2)}{2} = \frac{13}{2} \]

\(P = \left(\tfrac{11}{2}, \tfrac{13}{2}\right)\), beyond \(B\), with \(AP\) three times \(PB\).

Finding the ratio

In what ratio does \(P(2, 1)\) divide \(A(-2, 3)\), \(B(8, -2)\)?

\[ r = \frac{x - x_1}{x_2 - x} = \frac{2 - (-2)}{8 - 2} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3} \]

So \(AP : PB = 2 : 3\), internal (the ratio is positive).

Practical applications

  • Surveying and mapping: locating points a given fraction along a boundary line.
  • Computer graphics: linear interpolation between two points.
  • Mechanics: the centre of mass of two masses divides the line between them in the inverse ratio of the masses.
  • Geometry proofs: centroids, medians and the points that trisect a segment.

Common mistakes

  1. Pairing the weights wrongly. \(r_2\) multiplies \(A\) and \(r_1\) multiplies \(B\).
  2. Using the internal formula for an external division.
  3. Assuming any point divides AB. Check that \(P\) is on the line first.
  4. Mixing up the order of the ratio. \(AP : PB\) starts at \(A\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • Ratios are entered as two positive numbers; choose internal or external separately.
  • An external division in the ratio 1 : 1 does not exist (the point would be at infinity).
  • For the distance and midpoint only, use the midpoint and distance calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the section formula?

The point dividing A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio r₁ : r₂ is ((r₂x₁ + r₁x₂)/(r₁ + r₂), (r₂y₁ + r₁y₂)/(r₁ + r₂)).

What is the difference between internal and external division?

Internal division puts the point between A and B. External division puts it on the line AB extended beyond one end.

How do I find the points that trisect a segment?

Divide internally in the ratios 1 : 2 and 2 : 1.

How is the centroid related to the section formula?

The centroid divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1 from the vertex. Applying the section formula to a vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side gives the average of the three vertices.
