What the Poisson distribution calculator does

The Poisson distribution models the number of events in a fixed interval — calls per hour, typos per page, accidents per month — when events happen independently at a steady average rate λ.

Its mean and variance are both λ. For small λ it is lopsided; for large λ it looks like a normal curve.

Try it

In the explorer, push λ up past 15 and the bars take on a bell shape.

Formulas and variables

\[ P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k}{k!},\quad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \]\[ E[X] = \operatorname{Var}(X) = \lambda \]
SymbolMeaning
\(\lambda\)Average number of events in the interval
\(k\)Number of events

“At least k” is computed as 1 − P(X ≤ k − 1), since the list of possible values never ends.

Worked example

A help desk averages 3 calls per hour: probability of exactly 2 calls

Step 1 — Substitute

\[ P(X = 2) = \frac{e^{-3}\,3^2}{2!} \]

Step 2 — Evaluate

\[ = \frac{9}{2}e^{-3} \approx 0.2240 \]

Step 3 — Interpret

About 22% of hours have exactly two calls.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • λ > 0 (up to 100,000); k a whole number.
  • Events must be independent, one at a time, at a constant average rate.

Common mistakes

  • Using a λ for the wrong interval (2 per minute is 120 per hour).
  • Summing to “infinity” instead of using the complement for “at least”.
