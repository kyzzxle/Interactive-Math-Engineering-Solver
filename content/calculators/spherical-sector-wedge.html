What are spherical sectors, lunes and wedges?

Besides caps and segments, solid mensuration studies three more parts of a sphere:

  • A spherical sector is a cone with its apex at the centre, together with the cap above its rim, like an ice-cream cone filled to a rounded top. Its curved surface is a zone.
  • A spherical wedge is cut by two half-planes through a diameter, like a segment of an orange. Its curved surface is a lune.
  • A spherical triangle is bounded by three great-circle arcs. Joined to the centre it forms a spherical pyramid.

What this calculator does

  • Sector: from \(R\) and the cap height \(h\), finds the volume, the zone area, the cone's surface, the total surface, the rim radius and the cone's half-angle.
  • Wedge: from \(R\) and the angle \(\theta\), finds the lune area, the wedge volume and the wedge's total surface.
  • Triangle: from \(R\) and the three angles, finds the spherical excess, the area and the volume of the spherical pyramid.

The geometry behind it

Each of these is a simple fraction of the whole sphere:

  • a wedge of angle \(\theta\) is \(\tfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\) of the sphere;
  • a sector with zone height \(h\) is \(\tfrac{h}{2R}\) of the sphere, because zones of equal height have equal area;
  • a spherical triangle is \(\tfrac{E}{720^\circ}\) of the surface, where \(E\) is its spherical excess (Girard's theorem).

Pyramids from the centre

Both the sector and the spherical pyramid are made of thin cones from the centre, each of height \(R\), so their volumes are \(\tfrac{1}{3}R\) times their curved area.

Formula breakdown

\[ \text{Sector: } V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^2 h, \qquad A = 2\pi R h + \pi R a, \quad a = \sqrt{h(2R - h)} \]
\[ \text{Wedge: } A_{\text{lune}} = \frac{\pi R^2 \theta}{90^\circ}, \qquad V = \frac{\pi R^3 \theta}{270^\circ}, \qquad A_{\text{total}} = A_{\text{lune}} + \pi R^2 \]
\[ \text{Triangle: } E = A + B + C - 180^\circ, \qquad A_{\triangle} = \frac{\pi R^2 E}{180^\circ}, \qquad V_{\text{pyramid}} = \tfrac{1}{3}A_{\triangle} R \]

Variables explained

SymbolMeaning
\(R\)Radius of the sphere
\(h\)Height of the zone (cap) that forms the sector's curved surface
\(a\)Radius of the sector's rim
\(\theta\)Angle between the two planes of a wedge, in degrees
\(A, B, C\)Angles of a spherical triangle, in degrees
\(E\)Spherical excess: how much the triangle's angles exceed 180°

Deriving the formulas

Sector

The zone has area \(2\pi R h\). Splitting the sector into thin cones of height \(R\) on the zone gives

\[ V = \tfrac{1}{3}R\,(2\pi R h) = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^2 h. \]

Its flat-ish inner surface is a cone with slant height \(R\) and base radius \(a\), so its area is \(\pi R a\).

Wedge and lune

Rotating the wedge by \(\theta\) repeatedly, \(\tfrac{360^\circ}{\theta}\) copies fill the sphere. So the lune is \(\tfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot 4\pi R^2 = \tfrac{\pi R^2 \theta}{90^\circ}\) and the wedge is \(\tfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \tfrac{\pi R^3\theta}{270^\circ}\).

Girard's theorem

Each pair of great circles through a vertex of the triangle cuts two opposite lunes. The three pairs of lunes cover the sphere once, except the triangle and its opposite copy, which are covered three times. Comparing areas gives \(A_{\triangle} = \pi R^2\,\tfrac{E}{180^\circ}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A sphere of radius 9 cm is cut into a wedge of 40°. Find the lune and the volume.

Step 1: Fraction of the sphere

\[ \frac{40}{360} = \frac{1}{9} \]

Step 2: Lune area

\[ A = \frac{1}{9} \times 4\pi(9)^2 = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Wedge volume

\[ V = \frac{1}{9} \times \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(9)^3 = 108\pi \approx 339.29\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 4: Total surface

\[ A_{\text{total}} = 36\pi + \pi(9)^2 = 117\pi \approx 367.57\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Second worked example: a sector and a spherical triangle

Sector

A sector of a sphere of radius 6 cm has a cap height of 2 cm.

\[ V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(6)^2(2) = 48\pi \approx 150.80\ \text{cm}^3, \qquad a = \sqrt{2(12 - 2)} = \sqrt{20} \approx 4.472 \] \[ A = 2\pi(6)(2) + \pi(6)(4.472) = 24\pi + 26.833\pi \approx 159.70\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Triangle

A spherical triangle on a sphere of radius 10 m has angles 80°, 75° and 65°.

\[ E = 80 + 75 + 65 - 180 = 40^\circ, \qquad A_{\triangle} = \frac{\pi(10)^2(40)}{180} = \frac{200\pi}{9} \approx 69.81\ \text{m}^2 \]

Practical applications

  • Geography and navigation. Areas of regions bounded by great circles; the region between two meridians is a lune.
  • Astronomy. Areas on the celestial sphere.
  • Engineering. Segments of spherical tanks and domes cut by radial planes.

Common mistakes

  1. Confusing a sector with a cap. A sector includes the cone down to the centre.
  2. Forgetting the two semicircles when the total surface of a wedge is asked.
  3. Using plane-triangle rules. A spherical triangle's angles add to more than 180°.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Angles are in degrees. The wedge angle is the dihedral angle between the two cutting planes.
  • A spherical triangle's angles must each be below 180° and add to more than 180° (and less than 540°).
  • For a cap or a slab between two parallel planes, use the spherical cap and segment calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a spherical sector?

V = (2/3)πR²h, where h is the height of its zone (cap).

What is a lune?

The part of a sphere's surface between two great semicircles with the same ends. Its area is πR²θ/90° for an angle θ in degrees.

What is spherical excess?

The amount by which a spherical triangle's angles add to more than 180°. The triangle's area is proportional to it.

What is the difference between a spherical wedge and a sector?

A wedge is cut by two planes through a diameter, like an orange segment. A sector is a cone from the centre plus the cap on top of it.
