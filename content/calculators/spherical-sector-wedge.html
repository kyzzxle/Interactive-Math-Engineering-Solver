What the spherical sector and wedge calculator does

Besides caps and segments, solid mensuration studies three more parts of a sphere:

  • A spherical sector is a cone with its apex at the centre, together with the cap above its rim, like an ice-cream cone filled to a rounded top. Its curved surface is a zone.
  • A spherical wedge is cut by two half-planes through a diameter, like a segment of an orange. Its curved surface is a lune.
  • A spherical triangle is bounded by three great-circle arcs. Joined to the centre it forms a spherical pyramid.

Each one is a simple fraction of the whole sphere, which is why the answers come out as exact multiples of π.

Try it

In the explorer, set the wedge angle to 90°: the wedge is a quarter of the sphere, so its volume is \(\tfrac{1}{4}\cdot\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi R^3\).

Formulas and variables

\[ \text{Sector: } V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^2 h, \qquad A = 2\pi R h + \pi R a, \quad a = \sqrt{h(2R - h)} \]
\[ \text{Wedge: } A_{\text{lune}} = \frac{\pi R^2 \theta}{90^\circ}, \qquad V = \frac{\pi R^3 \theta}{270^\circ} \]
\[ \text{Triangle: } E = A + B + C - 180^\circ, \qquad A_{\triangle} = \frac{\pi R^2 E}{180^\circ}, \qquad V_{\text{pyramid}} = \tfrac{1}{3}A_{\triangle} R \]
SymbolMeaning
\(R\)Radius of the sphere
\(h\)Height of the zone (cap) that forms the sector's curved surface
\(a\)Radius of the sector's rim
\(\theta\)Angle between the two planes of a wedge, in degrees
\(E\)Spherical excess: how much the triangle's angles exceed 180°

The sector's volume is \(\tfrac{1}{3}R\) times its zone area, just as a pyramid's volume is \(\tfrac{1}{3}\) height times base. The wedge is the fraction \(\theta/360^\circ\) of the sphere, and a spherical triangle is the fraction \(E/720^\circ\) of the sphere's surface (Girard's theorem).

Worked example

A sphere of radius 9 cm is cut into a wedge of 40°. Find the lune and the volume.

Step 1: Fraction of the sphere

\[ \frac{40}{360} = \frac{1}{9} \]

Step 2: Lune area

\[ A = \frac{1}{9} \times 4\pi(9)^2 = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Wedge volume

\[ V = \frac{1}{9} \times \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(9)^3 = 108\pi \approx 339.29\ \text{cm}^3 \]

A sector of the same sphere with a cap 2 cm high would have \(V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(9)^2(2) = 108\pi\): the same volume, by coincidence.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Angles are in degrees. The wedge angle is the dihedral angle between the two cutting planes.
  • A spherical triangle's angles must each be below 180° and add to more than 180° (and less than 540°).
  • For a cap or a slab between two parallel planes, and the area of its zone, use the spherical cap and segment calculator.
