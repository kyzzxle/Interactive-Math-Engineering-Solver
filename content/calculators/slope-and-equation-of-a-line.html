What are the slope and the equation of a line?

The slope of a line measures its steepness: how much \(y\) changes for every one-unit increase in \(x\). Once you know the slope and one point, the whole line is fixed, and it can be written as an equation that every point on the line satisfies.

Try changing the input

Make the two x-values equal and the slope becomes undefined: the line is vertical and its equation is simply \(x = \text{constant}\).

The geometry behind it

Between any two points of a line, the ratio of the vertical change (the rise) to the horizontal change (the run) is the same. That constant ratio is the slope. It is also the tangent of the angle \(\theta\) the line makes with the positive x-axis.

  • \(m > 0\): the line rises to the right.
  • \(m \lt 0\): it falls to the right.
  • \(m = 0\): it is horizontal.
  • No slope: it is vertical (the run is zero).

Parallel and perpendicular lines

Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\), so each slope is the negative reciprocal of the other: \(m_2 = -\tfrac{1}{m_1}\).

Deriving the forms

Point–slope form

Let \((x, y)\) be any other point on the line. Its slope from \((x_1, y_1)\) must equal \(m\):

\[ \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = m \;\Rightarrow\; y - y_1 = m(x - x_1). \]

Slope–intercept form

Expand and solve for \(y\): \(y = mx + (y_1 - m x_1)\). The constant \(b = y_1 - m x_1\) is the value of \(y\) when \(x = 0\), the y-intercept.

General form

Move every term to one side and multiply by the common denominator so that \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are whole numbers. A slope of \(-\tfrac{A}{B}\) can be read straight back from it.

Complete worked example

Problem

Find the equation of the line through \((1, 3)\) and \((4, 9)\).

Step 1: Slope

\[ m = \frac{9 - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2 \]

Step 2: Point–slope form

\[ y - 3 = 2(x - 1) \]

Step 3: Slope–intercept form

\[ b = 3 - 2(1) = 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 2x + 1 \]

Step 4: General form and intercepts

\[ 2x - y + 1 = 0, \qquad \text{y-intercept } 1, \qquad \text{x-intercept } -\tfrac{1}{2} \]

Check

Substitute the second point: \(2(4) + 1 = 9\). ✓ The angle of inclination is \(\tan^{-1} 2 \approx 63.43^\circ\).

Second worked example: a point and a fractional slope

Problem

Find the line through \((-2, 5)\) with slope \(-\tfrac{3}{4}\), and the line through the same point perpendicular to it.

Step 1: Point–slope form

\[ y - 5 = -\tfrac{3}{4}(x + 2) \]

Step 2: The y-intercept

\[ b = 5 - \left(-\tfrac{3}{4}\right)(-2) = 5 - \tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{7}{2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = -\tfrac{3}{4}x + \tfrac{7}{2} \]

Step 3: General form

Multiply by 4 and move everything to the left: \(3x + 4y - 14 = 0\). The x-intercept is \(\tfrac{14}{3}\).

Step 4: The perpendicular line

Its slope is the negative reciprocal, \(\tfrac{4}{3}\):

\[ y - 5 = \tfrac{4}{3}(x + 2) \;\Rightarrow\; y = \tfrac{4}{3}x + \tfrac{23}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; 4x - 3y + 23 = 0 \]

Check

\(\left(-\tfrac{3}{4}\right)\left(\tfrac{4}{3}\right) = -1\), so the lines are perpendicular. ✓

Practical applications

  • Road and ramp design: grade is slope written as a percentage; a 1 : 12 ramp has slope \(\tfrac{1}{12} \approx 8.3\%\).
  • Roof pitch: a 6/12 pitch rises 6 units for every 12 across.
  • Linear models: cost, depreciation and calibration lines have a rate (the slope) and a starting value (the intercept).
  • Drainage and pipes: the fall per metre of a sewer line.

Common mistakes

  1. Run over rise. Slope is the change in \(y\) divided by the change in \(x\), not the other way round.
  2. Mixed order. Subtract in the same order on top and bottom: \(\tfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\), not \(\tfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_1 - x_2}\).
  3. Zero versus undefined. A horizontal line has slope 0; a vertical line has no slope at all.
  4. Reading the slope from the general form. For \(Ax + By + C = 0\) the slope is \(-\tfrac{A}{B}\), not \(A\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • Two different points are required; one point alone does not determine a line.
  • Slopes and intercepts are computed exactly as fractions; decimals are shown alongside.
  • Vertical lines have an undefined slope and no slope–intercept form; horizontal lines have slope 0 and no x-intercept (unless they are the x-axis).
  • The angle of inclination is rounded to 4 decimal places.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the slope from two points?

Divide the change in y by the change in x: m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Keep the subtraction in the same order on top and bottom.

How do I convert y = mx + b to general form?

Move every term to one side, for example mx − y + b = 0, then multiply by a common denominator so the coefficients are whole numbers.

What is the slope of a perpendicular line?

The negative reciprocal. If one line has slope 2, a perpendicular line has slope −1/2, because the product of the slopes is −1.

Why is the slope of a vertical line undefined?

Every point on a vertical line has the same x-coordinate, so the run is 0 and the slope would require dividing by zero.
