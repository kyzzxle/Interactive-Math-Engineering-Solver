Slope and the equation of a line

The slope of a line measures its steepness: how much \(y\) changes for every one-unit increase in \(x\). Once you know the slope and one point, the whole line is determined. This calculator accepts two points or a point and a slope and returns the slope, the point–slope form, the slope–intercept form, the general form with whole-number coefficients, both intercepts and the angle of inclination.

Try changing the input

Make the two x-values equal and the slope becomes undefined: the line is vertical and its equation is simply \(x = \text{constant}\).

Formulas and variables

\[ m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \qquad y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \qquad y = mx + b \qquad Ax + By + C = 0 \]
SymbolMeaning
\(m\)Slope: rise over run. Positive lines rise to the right, negative lines fall.
\(b\)y-intercept: where the line crosses the y-axis, \(b = y_1 - m x_1\).
\(A, B, C\)Whole-number coefficients of the general form.
\(\theta\)Angle of inclination, \(\tan\theta = m\), measured from the positive x-axis (0° to 180°).

Worked example

The line through \((1, 3)\) and \((4, 9)\)

Step 1 — Slope

\[ m = \frac{9 - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2 \]

Step 2 — Point–slope form

\[ y - 3 = 2(x - 1) \]

Step 3 — Slope–intercept form

\[ b = 3 - 2(1) = 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 2x + 1 \]

Step 4 — General form

\[ 2x - y + 1 = 0 \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Two different points are required; one point alone does not determine a line.
  • Slopes and intercepts are computed exactly as fractions; decimals are shown alongside.
  • Vertical lines have an undefined slope and no slope–intercept form; horizontal lines have slope 0 and no x-intercept (unless they are the x-axis).
  • The angle of inclination is rounded to 4 decimal places.
