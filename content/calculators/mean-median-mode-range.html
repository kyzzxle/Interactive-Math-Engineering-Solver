Mean, median, mode and range

These four numbers summarise a data set. The mean, median and mode describe its centre in three different ways; the range describes its spread. Paste your values and the calculator sorts them, computes all four exactly and draws the distribution with the mean and median marked.

Try changing the input

Add one very large value to the test scores. The mean jumps, but the median barely moves — the median is resistant to outliers, the mean is not.

Definitions

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \qquad \text{range} = x_{\max} - x_{\min} \]
MeasureHow to find it
Mean \(\bar{x}\)Add all values and divide by how many there are.
MedianSort the values. With an odd count it is the middle one; with an even count, the average of the two middle values.
ModeThe value(s) that occur most often. If every value occurs equally often there is no mode.
RangeLargest value minus smallest value.

Worked example

Data: 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42

Step 1 — Mean

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{4 + 8 + 15 + 16 + 23 + 42}{6} = \frac{108}{6} = 18 \]

Step 2 — Median (6 values, so average the 3rd and 4th)

\[ \text{median} = \frac{15 + 16}{2} = 15.5 \]

Step 3 — Mode and range

Every value appears once, so there is no mode. Range \(= 42 - 4 = 38\).

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • Enter between 1 and 10,000 numeric values; text, units or symbols are rejected with the offending entry named.
  • The mean is computed exactly as a fraction, so long data sets do not accumulate rounding error. Non-terminating means are shown rounded to 6 decimal places.
  • Several values can share the mode (bimodal or multimodal data).
