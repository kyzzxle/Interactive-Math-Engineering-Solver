What the wave calculator does

A periodic wave repeats itself in space and in time. The wavelength \(\lambda\) is the distance between neighbouring crests, the frequency \(f\) is how many crests pass a point each second, and the wave speed \(v\) is how fast the pattern travels. In one period \(T\) the wave moves forward exactly one wavelength, which gives the wave equation.

Choose which quantity to find and enter the other two. The calculator also gives the period, angular frequency and wave number. The explorer animates a travelling wave you can pause, step through and reset.

Try it

In the explorer, watch the orange buoy: it only moves up and down, while the wave pattern travels along. Waves carry energy, not the medium itself.

Formulas and variables

\[ v = f\lambda \qquad T = \frac{1}{f} \qquad \omega = 2\pi f \qquad k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]
SymbolMeaningSI unit
\(v\)Wave speedm/s
\(f\)Frequencyhertz, Hz (per second)
\(\lambda\)Wavelengthm
\(T\)Periods

Useful speeds: sound in air about 343 m/s at 20 °C, sound in water about 1480 m/s, light in a vacuum \(3.00 \times 10^8\) m/s (type 3e8).

Worked example

The wavelength of the note A (440 Hz) in air

Step 1 — Known values

\(v = 343\) m/s, \(f = 440\) Hz.

Step 2 — Rearrange the wave equation

\[ \lambda = \frac{v}{f} \]

Step 3 — Substitute and simplify

\[ \lambda = \frac{343}{440} \approx 0.780\ \text{m} \]

Step 4 — Period

\[ T = \frac{1}{440} \approx 0.00227\ \text{s} = 2.27\ \text{ms} \]

Assumptions and limitations

  • The wave is periodic with a single frequency, travelling through a uniform medium at constant speed.
  • Wave speed depends on the medium, not on the frequency, for sound in air and light in a vacuum. In dispersive media (deep-water waves, light in glass) the speed varies with frequency.
  • All values must be positive. Very large or small numbers can be typed in scientific notation, such as 532e-9.
