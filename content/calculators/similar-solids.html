What the similar solids calculator does

Two solids are similar when one is an exact scaled copy of the other: every length is multiplied by the same scale factor \(k\). Areas are then multiplied by \(k^2\) and volumes by \(k^3\).

This calculator starts from whatever you know — two matching lengths, or a ratio of lengths, areas or volumes — and finds the other ratios, exactly when possible.

Try it

A model at scale 1 : 10 needs only 1/1000 of the material of the real object — that is k³ at work.

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{L_2}{L_1} = k \qquad \frac{A_2}{A_1} = k^2 \qquad \frac{V_2}{V_1} = k^3 \]
SymbolMeaning
\(k\)Length ratio (scale factor), solid 2 ÷ solid 1
\(A_1,\ A_2\)Surface areas
\(V_1,\ V_2\)Volumes

From an area ratio, take a square root to get k; from a volume ratio, take a cube root.

Worked example

Two similar jugs are 4 cm and 6 cm tall; the small one holds 64 cm³

Step 1 — Scale factor

\[ k = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} \]

Step 2 — Volume ratio

\[ k^3 = \frac{27}{8} \]

Step 3 — Larger volume

\[ V_2 = 64 \times \frac{27}{8} = 216\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Ratios must be positive. The solids must be similar: same shape, every length scaled equally.
  • Exact fractions are shown when the roots of the ratios are rational.

Common mistakes

  • Scaling a volume by k instead of k³ (or an area by k instead of k²).
  • Mixing up the order of the ratio: here it is always solid 2 divided by solid 1.
