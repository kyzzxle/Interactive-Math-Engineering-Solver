What are similar solids?

Two solids are similar when one is an exact scaled copy of the other: the same shape, with every length multiplied by the same scale factor \(k\). Two spheres are always similar; two cubes are always similar; a model car and the real car are similar if the model is accurate.

What this calculator does

Give two matching lengths, the scale factor itself, the ratio of the surface areas or the ratio of the volumes. The calculator finds the scale factor \(k\), the area ratio \(k^2\) and the volume ratio \(k^3\), exactly where possible, and scales a surface area or volume of the first solid to the second.

The geometry behind it

Every length is multiplied by \(k\). An area is a length times a length, so it is multiplied by \(k \cdot k = k^2\). A volume is length × length × length, so it is multiplied by \(k^3\).

\[ \frac{L_2}{L_1} = k, \qquad \frac{A_2}{A_1} = k^2, \qquad \frac{V_2}{V_1} = k^3. \]

Why big animals have thick legs

Double an animal's size and its weight (volume) grows 8 times, but the strength of its bones (cross-section area) only 4 times. That is why elephants have proportionally thicker legs than mice.

Deriving the formulas

Take a cube of edge \(a\). Its similar copy has edge \(ka\). Then

\[ \frac{A_2}{A_1} = \frac{6(ka)^2}{6a^2} = k^2, \qquad \frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{(ka)^3}{a^3} = k^3. \]

Any solid can be filled with tiny cubes; scaling the solid scales every cube the same way, so the same ratios hold for every shape. Check with spheres: \(\tfrac{4}{3}\pi(kr)^3 = k^3\cdot\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Two similar cones have heights 4 cm and 6 cm. The smaller holds 64 cm³. How much does the larger hold?

Step 1: Scale factor

\[ k = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} \]

Step 2: Volume ratio

\[ k^3 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{27}{8} \]

Step 3: Scale the volume

\[ V_2 = 64 \times \frac{27}{8} = 216\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Second worked example: from a volume ratio

Problem

Two similar tanks have volumes in the ratio 27 : 8. The smaller needs 40 m² of paint. How much does the larger need?

Step 1: Undo the cube

\[ k = \sqrt[3]{\frac{27}{8}} = \frac{3}{2} \]

Step 2: Area ratio and paint

\[ k^2 = \frac{9}{4}, \qquad A_2 = 40 \times \frac{9}{4} = 90\ \text{m}^2 \]

Practical applications

  • Scale models. Predicting the real object's material and capacity from a model.
  • Packaging. How much more a larger, similar container holds.
  • Engineering. Scaling laws in structures and fluid flow.
  • Biology. Why heat loss, strength and weight change differently with size.

Common mistakes

  1. Scaling volume by k. Volume scales by \(k^3\), area by \(k^2\).
  2. Taking the ratio upside down. Keep solid 2 ÷ solid 1 consistently.
  3. Assuming solids are similar. Two cylinders are similar only if both radius and height scale by the same factor.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The two solids are exactly similar.
  • Masses scale like volumes only for the same material.

Frequently asked questions

If one solid is twice as tall, how much bigger is its volume?

If the solids are similar, the volume is 2³ = 8 times larger and the surface area 2² = 4 times larger.

How do I find the scale factor from two volumes?

Divide the volumes and take the cube root: k = ∛(V₂/V₁).

Are all cylinders similar?

No. Two cylinders are similar only if their heights and radii are in the same ratio. All spheres and all cubes are similar.

Does the rule work for any shape?

Yes, for any pair of exactly similar solids, however complicated.
