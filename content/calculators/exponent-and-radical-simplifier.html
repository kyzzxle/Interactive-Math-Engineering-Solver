What are exponents and radicals?

An exponent says how many times a base is multiplied by itself: \(x^3 = x \cdot x \cdot x\). A radical undoes it: \(\sqrt[3]{x^3} = x\). The two are the same idea written two ways, because a root is a fractional power, \(\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}\). A handful of rules let you simplify any product, quotient or power of them.

Try changing the input

Evaluate \(16^{-3/4}\). The 4 is a fourth root (\(\sqrt[4]{16} = 2\)), the 3 is a cube (\(2^3 = 8\)) and the minus sign is a reciprocal: \(\tfrac{1}{8}\).

What this calculator does

  • Laws of exponents: simplifies products, quotients and powers of monomials such as \(\tfrac{(2x^3y^{-2})^2}{4x^{-1}y}\), writing the result with positive exponents.
  • Radicals: simplifies \(\sqrt[n]{k\,x^a y^b}\) by pulling out every perfect \(n\)th power.
  • Rational powers: evaluates \(b^{p/q}\) exactly or in simplest radical form, with a decimal.

Each result ends with a check: substituting sample values, or raising the answer back to the power of the root.

The ideas behind it

Exponents count factors, so the rules are about counting:

  • Multiplying \(x^3 \cdot x^2\) puts 3 + 2 factors together: \(x^5\).
  • Dividing cancels factors: \(\tfrac{x^5}{x^2} = x^3\). Carrying on past zero gives negative exponents: \(x^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{x^2}\).
  • A power of a power multiplies the counts: \((x^3)^2 = x^6\).

A fractional exponent fits the same rules: \(\left(x^{1/2}\right)^2 = x^1\), so \(x^{1/2}\) must be \(\sqrt{x}\).

Simplest radical form

A radical is simplified when nothing inside is a perfect \(n\)th power: \(\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2} = 6\sqrt{2}\).

Simplifying a radical, step by step

For \(\sqrt[n]{k\,x^a}\):

  1. Split the coefficient \(k\) into the largest perfect \(n\)th power and what is left.
  2. Divide each exponent \(a\) by \(n\): the quotient comes out of the root, the remainder stays in. For example \(x^5\) under a square root is \(x^{2\cdot 2 + 1}\), so \(x^2\) comes out and \(x\) stays in.
  3. Multiply the outside parts and the inside parts.

To check, raise the outside part to the power \(n\) and multiply by what is inside: it must rebuild the original radicand.

Complete worked example

Problem

Simplify \(\dfrac{(2x^3y^{-2})^2}{4x^{-1}y}\).

Step 1: Power of a product

\[ (2x^3y^{-2})^2 = 4x^6y^{-4} \]

Step 2: Divide, subtracting exponents

\[ \frac{4x^6y^{-4}}{4x^{-1}y} = x^{6 - (-1)}\,y^{-4 - 1} = x^7y^{-5} \]

Step 3: Positive exponents

\[ x^7y^{-5} = \frac{x^7}{y^5} \]

Check

With \(x = 1.3\) and \(y = 0.7\), both the original and \(\tfrac{x^7}{y^5}\) give about 37.33. ✓

Second worked example: a radical and a rational power

Part 1: Simplify \(\sqrt{72x^5y^2}\)

\[ 72 = 36 \cdot 2, \qquad x^5 = x^4 \cdot x, \qquad y^2 = y^2 \] \[ \sqrt{72x^5y^2} = 6x^2y\sqrt{2x} \]

Check: \((6x^2y)^2 \cdot 2x = 36x^4y^2 \cdot 2x = 72x^5y^2\). ✓

Part 2: Evaluate \(16^{-3/4}\)

\[ 16^{-3/4} = \frac{1}{\left(\sqrt[4]{16}\right)^3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8} = 0.125 \]

Practical applications

  • Scientific notation and units: \(10^3 \times 10^{-6} = 10^{-3}\), converting kilo- and micro- units.
  • Scaling laws: volume grows as length³ and area as length², so a model at scale \(\tfrac{1}{10}\) has \(\tfrac{1}{1000}\) of the volume.
  • Physics formulas: the pendulum period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}\) and Kepler's law \(T^2 \propto a^3\).
  • Growth and decay: compound interest and half-lives use powers.

Common mistakes

  1. Multiplying exponents in a product. \(x^3 \cdot x^2 = x^5\), not \(x^6\).
  2. Treating a negative exponent as a negative number. \(2^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{8}\), not \(-8\).
  3. Applying a power to only part of a product. \((2x)^3 = 8x^3\), not \(2x^3\).
  4. Splitting a root over a sum. \(\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5\), not \(3 + 4\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • Variables are assumed positive, so even roots need no absolute values and division is allowed.
  • The laws mode handles products, quotients and powers of monomials (no sums).
  • Radicands are a single term with an integer coefficient.

Frequently asked questions

What does a fractional exponent mean?

a^(p/q) is the q-th root of a raised to the power p. For example 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4.

How do I simplify a square root?

Write the number as a perfect square times what is left and take the square root of the perfect square: √50 = √(25·2) = 5√2.

Why is any number to the power 0 equal to 1?

Because aⁿ/aⁿ = 1, and by the quotient rule it is also a⁰.

How do I get rid of negative exponents?

Move the factor to the other side of the fraction bar and make the exponent positive: x⁻²y = y/x².
