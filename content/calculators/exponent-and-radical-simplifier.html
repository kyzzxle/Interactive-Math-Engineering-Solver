Simplifying exponents and radicals

Exponent and radical expressions can usually be written in a shorter, standard form: no negative exponents, each variable written once, and no perfect powers left under a root. This simplifier supports three clearly defined types of problem, each with its own step-by-step explanation:

  • Laws of exponents — products, quotients and powers of monomials such as \(\dfrac{(2x^3y^{-2})^2}{4x^{-1}y}\).
  • Radicals — a root of a single term, such as \(\sqrt{72x^5y^2}\) or \(\sqrt[3]{-54a^7}\).
  • Rational powers of numbers — such as \(16^{-3/4}\) or \(12^{3/2}\).

Try changing the input

Change the index in \(\sqrt{72x^5y^2}\) from 2 to 3: different factors are perfect cubes, so a different amount comes out of the root.

Rules used

\[ a^m a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \]
\[ a^{p/q} = \sqrt[q]{a^p} \qquad \sqrt[n]{a^n b} = a\sqrt[n]{b} \]

To simplify a root of index \(n\), divide each exponent by \(n\): the quotient comes out of the root and the remainder stays inside. For a number, split off the largest perfect \(n\)th power.

Worked example

Simplify \(\dfrac{(2x^3y^{-2})^2}{4x^{-1}y}\)

Step 1 — Power of a product

\[ (2x^3y^{-2})^2 = 4x^6y^{-4} \]

Step 2 — Divide like bases (subtract exponents)

\[ \frac{4x^6y^{-4}}{4x^{-1}y} = x^{6 - (-1)}\, y^{-4 - 1} = x^7 y^{-5} \]

Step 3 — Positive exponents

\[ x^7 y^{-5} = \frac{x^7}{y^5} \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • All variables are assumed to be positive. This is the usual textbook convention and means even roots need no absolute-value signs.
  • Sums and differences (such as \(\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\)) are outside the supported forms and are rejected with an explanation.
  • Even roots of negative numbers are not real and are rejected. Exponent denominators are limited to 12.
  • Exact results use fractions and simplified radicals; decimals are given to 10 significant figures.
