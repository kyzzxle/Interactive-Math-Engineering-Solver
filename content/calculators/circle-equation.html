What is the equation of a circle?

A circle is the set of all points at the same distance, the radius \(r\), from a fixed point, the centre \((h, k)\). Writing that condition with the distance formula gives the equation of the circle. It comes in two forms: the standard form, which shows the centre and radius at a glance, and the expanded general form.

Try changing the input

In the general-form mode, make \(F\) large and positive, for example \(x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y + 20 = 0\). Completing the square gives a negative \(r^2\), so no real circle exists.

The geometry behind it

A point \((x, y)\) is on the circle exactly when its distance from the centre is \(r\):

\[ \sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} = r. \]

Squaring both sides gives the standard form. Expanding it gives an equation whose \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) terms have the same coefficient and which has no \(xy\) term. That is how you recognise a circle in general form.

Three points fix a circle

Any three points that are not on one line lie on exactly one circle. Its centre is where the perpendicular bisectors of the sides of the triangle meet.

Formula breakdown

\[ (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 \qquad x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 \]

From general form to standard form

Completing the square reverses the expansion. Take \(x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 8y + 9 = 0\):

  1. Group the terms and move \(F\): \((x^2 + 6x) + (y^2 - 8y) = -9\).
  2. Add half of each linear coefficient, squared, to both sides: \(\left(\tfrac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 9\) and \(\left(\tfrac{-8}{2}\right)^2 = 16\).
  3. Both brackets are now perfect squares: \[ (x^2 + 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 8y + 16) = -9 + 9 + 16 \;\Rightarrow\; (x + 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 16. \]

The centre is \((-3, 4)\) and the radius is 4. If the right-hand side had come out as 0, the "circle" would be a single point; if negative, there would be no real points at all.

Complete worked example

Problem

Write the equation of the circle with centre \((2, -3)\) and radius 5 in standard and general form.

Step 1: Standard form

\[ (x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25 \]

Step 2: Expand

\[ x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 + 6y + 9 = 25 \]

Step 3: General form

\[ x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 12 = 0 \]

Check

\(D = -2(2) = -4\), \(E = -2(-3) = 6\) and \(F = 4 + 9 - 25 = -12\). ✓ The circumference is \(10\pi \approx 31.42\) and the area is \(25\pi \approx 78.54\).

Second worked example: the circle through three points

Problem

Find the circle through \((0, 0)\), \((4, 0)\) and \((0, 2)\).

Step 1: Substitute each point into \(x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\)

\[ (0, 0):\ F = 0 \qquad (4, 0):\ 16 + 4D + F = 0 \qquad (0, 2):\ 4 + 2E + F = 0 \]

Step 2: Solve

\(F = 0\), so \(D = -4\) and \(E = -2\). The equation is \(x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 2y = 0\).

Step 3: Centre and radius

\[ (h, k) = (2, 1), \qquad r^2 = \frac{16 + 4}{4} - 0 = 5, \qquad r = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236 \]

Check

The angle at \((0, 0)\) is a right angle, so the side from \((4, 0)\) to \((0, 2)\) must be a diameter. Its midpoint is \((2, 1)\) and its length is \(\sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}\). ✓

Practical applications

  • Surveying and road design: laying out circular curves from their centre and radius.
  • Machining and CNC: circular interpolation and bolt-hole circles.
  • Locating a source: three equal-time measurements around an event lie on a circle whose centre is the source.
  • Coverage areas: the region within a set range of a tower or sensor.

Common mistakes

  1. Sign of the centre. \((x + 3)^2\) means \(h = -3\), not \(+3\).
  2. Radius versus radius squared. The right side of the standard form is \(r^2\); for \(= 16\), the radius is 4.
  3. Completing the square on one side only. Whatever you add on the left must also be added on the right.
  4. Unequal \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) coefficients. Divide through first; if they still differ, the curve is an ellipse, not a circle.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The general form is entered with the \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) coefficients equal to 1. Divide through first if they are not.
  • All arithmetic is exact; the radius is given in simplest radical form when it is not a whole number.
  • The three points must be different and not on one line.
  • For equations with an \(xy\) term or unequal squared terms, use the general conic calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the centre and radius from the general form?

The centre is (−D/2, −E/2) and r² = (D² + E²)/4 − F. Completing the square gives the same result step by step.

How do I find a circle through three points?

Substitute each point into x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 and solve the three linear equations for D, E and F.

When does the general form not describe a circle?

When (D² + E²)/4 − F is 0 (a single point) or negative (no real points).

What is the equation of a circle centred at the origin?

x² + y² = r². The centre (0, 0) makes h and k zero.
