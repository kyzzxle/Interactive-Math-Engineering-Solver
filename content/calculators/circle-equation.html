Equation of a circle

A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance \(r\) (the radius) from a fixed point \((h, k)\) (the centre). Its equation can be written in standard form, which shows the centre and radius directly, or in general form, which is what you get after expanding. This calculator converts between them, finds a circle from its centre and one point, and finds the unique circle through three points.

Try changing the input

In general form, make F larger: the right-hand side after completing the square shrinks, the circle gets smaller, and eventually there is no real circle at all.

Formulas and variables

\[ (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 \qquad x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 \]
\[ h = -\frac{D}{2}, \qquad k = -\frac{E}{2}, \qquad r^2 = \frac{D^2 + E^2}{4} - F \]
SymbolMeaning
\((h, k)\)Centre of the circle.
\(r\)Radius (must be positive).
\(D, E, F\)Coefficients of the general form (coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) are both 1).

Worked example

Convert \(x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 12 = 0\) to standard form

Step 1 — Group and move the constant

\[ (x^2 - 4x) + (y^2 + 6y) = 12 \]

Step 2 — Complete each square

\[ (x^2 - 4x + 4) + (y^2 + 6y + 9) = 12 + 4 + 9 \]

Step 3 — Standard form

\[ (x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25 \]

The centre is \((2, -3)\) and the radius is \(\sqrt{25} = 5\).

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • General-form input assumes the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) are 1. Divide the whole equation first if they are another equal number.
  • If \(r^2 \le 0\) after completing the square there is no real circle (or only a single point), and no result is shown.
  • Three points must not lie on one straight line.
  • All work uses exact fractions; radii are given in simplest radical form, and circumference and area are rounded.
