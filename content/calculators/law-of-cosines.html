The law of cosines

The law of cosines extends the Pythagorean theorem to every triangle. It solves the two cases the law of sines cannot start: two sides with the angle between them (SAS), and three sides (SSS). Unlike the law of sines it is never ambiguous, because the inverse cosine gives a unique angle between 0° and 180°.

Try changing the input

Set the included angle to 90°: the \(-2ab\cos C\) term vanishes and the formula becomes \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\). Make it larger than 90° and the third side grows beyond the Pythagorean value.

Formulas and variables

\[ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C \qquad \cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(a, b, c\)Side lengths.
\(A, B, C\)Angles opposite those sides; \(C\) is the angle between \(a\) and \(b\).

For three sides to form a triangle, the two shorter sides must add up to more than the longest — the triangle inequality.

Worked example

Sides 5, 7, 8

Step 1 — Angle B (opposite the side 7)

\[ \cos B = \frac{5^2 + 8^2 - 7^2}{2(5)(8)} = \frac{40}{80} = \frac{1}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; B = 60^\circ \]

Step 2 — Angle A

\[ \cos A = \frac{7^2 + 8^2 - 5^2}{2(7)(8)} = \frac{88}{112} \approx 0.7857 \;\Rightarrow\; A \approx 38.21^\circ \]

Step 3 — Angle C

\[ C = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 38.21^\circ \approx 81.79^\circ \]

Assumptions, domain and rounding

  • SAS: the angle must be the one between the two given sides, strictly between 0° and 180°.
  • SSS: the sides must satisfy the triangle inequality; impossible triangles are rejected with an explanation.
  • Angles are in degrees; results are rounded to 6 decimal places.
