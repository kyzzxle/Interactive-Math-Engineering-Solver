What the rational expression simplifier does

A rational expression is a fraction of two polynomials, \(P(x)/Q(x)\). It is simplified by cancelling their common factors, just as \(\tfrac{6}{8} = \tfrac{3}{4}\) cancels a common factor 2.

The calculator finds the greatest common divisor of the polynomials exactly (Euclid’s algorithm), shows both in factored form, and keeps track of the values of x where the original expression is not defined — cancelling a factor does not make those values allowed.

Try it

Look at the graph: a cancelled factor leaves a hole, while a factor that stays in the denominator gives a vertical asymptote.

Formulas and variables

\[ \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = \frac{P(x)/G(x)}{Q(x)/G(x)},\quad G = \gcd(P, Q),\quad Q(x) \ne 0 \]
SymbolMeaning
\(P,\ Q\)Numerator and denominator polynomials
\(G\)Greatest common divisor (the common factor)

Worked example

Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + x − 6)

Step 1 — Factor

\[ \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x + 3)(x - 2)} \]

Step 2 — Cancel (x + 3)

\[ = \frac{x - 3}{x - 2} \]

Step 3 — Excluded values

The original denominator is zero at \(x = -3\) and \(x = 2\), so \(x \ne -3, 2\). At \(x = -3\) the graph has a hole; at \(x = 2\) a vertical asymptote.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Polynomials in x up to degree 6 with rational coefficients.
  • Factors are over the rationals: quadratics with irrational roots are left as they are.

Common mistakes

  • Cancelling terms instead of factors: (x + 3)/(x + 5) cannot be “simplified” to 3/5.
  • Dropping the excluded values after cancelling.
