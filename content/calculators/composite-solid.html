What is a composite solid?

A composite solid is made by joining simple solids together, or by removing one from another. A grain silo is a cylinder with a hemispherical roof; a medicine capsule is a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end; a pencil is a cylinder with a cone; an ice-cream cone is a cone topped with a hemisphere; a pipe is a cylinder with a smaller cylinder removed.

What this calculator does

Choose one of five common shapes and enter its dimensions. The calculator:

  • finds the volume of each part separately, then adds them (or subtracts the hole),
  • lists only the exposed surfaces and adds their areas,
  • draws the solid to scale in a side view, and in 3D in the explorer.

The geometry behind it

Volumes simply add: the space inside a silo is the space in the cylinder plus the space in the dome. Surface areas need more care. Where two parts are joined, the circle where they meet is inside the solid and is not part of its surface, so it must not be counted.

The method for any composite solid

1. Sketch it and split it into standard solids. 2. Find each volume and add or subtract. 3. List the faces you could touch from outside, and add only those areas.

Deriving the formulas

Each formula is assembled from standard parts:

  • cylinder: \(V = \pi r^2 h\), curved side \(2\pi r h\), each end \(\pi r^2\);
  • hemisphere: \(V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3\) (half of \(\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)), curved surface \(2\pi r^2\);
  • cone: \(V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 H\), curved surface \(\pi r s\) with \(s = \sqrt{r^2 + H^2}\).

For a silo, the cylinder's top circle is covered by the dome, so the surface is the cylinder side, the dome and the floor only: \(2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2\). A capsule has no flat faces at all.

Complete worked example

Problem

A silo is a cylinder of radius 3 m and height 10 m with a hemispherical roof. Find its capacity and its outside surface.

Step 1: Volume of each part

\[ V_{\text{cyl}} = \pi(3)^2(10) = 90\pi, \qquad V_{\text{hemi}} = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(3)^3 = 18\pi \]

Step 2: Total volume

\[ V = 90\pi + 18\pi = 108\pi \approx 339.29\ \text{m}^3 \]

Step 3: Exposed surfaces

\[ 2\pi(3)(10) = 60\pi\ (\text{side}), \quad 2\pi(3)^2 = 18\pi\ (\text{dome}), \quad \pi(3)^2 = 9\pi\ (\text{floor}) \] \[ A = 60\pi + 18\pi + 9\pi = 87\pi \approx 273.32\ \text{m}^2 \]

Second worked example: a pipe

Problem

A steel pipe 100 cm long has an outer radius of 5 cm and an inner radius of 4 cm. Find the volume of steel.

Step 1: Outer cylinder minus the hole

\[ V = \pi(5)^2(100) - \pi(4)^2(100) = 2500\pi - 1600\pi = 900\pi \approx 2827.43\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2: Mass

With steel at about 7.85 g/cm³, the pipe weighs about \(2827.43 \times 7.85 \approx 22{,}200\) g, or 22.2 kg.

Practical applications

  • Storage. Silos, water towers and tanks with domed ends.
  • Manufacturing. Capsules, pencils, bullets and bolts.
  • Plumbing and structures. The weight of pipes and hollow columns.
  • Exam problems. Composite solids are a favourite way to test several formulas at once.

Common mistakes

  1. Counting hidden faces. The joining circle between parts is inside the solid.
  2. Using a full sphere for a dome. A hemisphere is half: \(\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3\).
  3. Forgetting the inside of a pipe when finding the surface to paint or coat.
  4. Mismatched radii. The parts must share the same radius where they join.

Assumptions and limitations

  • All parts share the radius \(r\), and the hemispheres and cones sit flush on the cylinder.
  • Wall thickness is ignored except for the pipe.
  • For other combinations, add the results of the cylinder, cone and sphere calculators.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the volume of a composite solid?

Split it into standard solids, find each volume, and add them (subtract any holes).

Why is the surface area not just the sum of the parts' surfaces?

Where two parts join, the surfaces that touch are hidden inside the solid. Only the outside faces count.

What is the volume of a capsule?

πr²h + (4/3)πr³: a cylinder plus two hemispheres, which together make one sphere.

What is the volume of a hollow pipe?

π(R² − r²)h, where R and r are the outer and inner radii.
