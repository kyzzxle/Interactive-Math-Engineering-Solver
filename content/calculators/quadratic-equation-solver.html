What is a quadratic equation?

A quadratic equation has the form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with \(a \ne 0\). Its graph \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) is a parabola, and the solutions, or roots, are where the parabola crosses the x-axis. There can be two real roots, one repeated root, or two complex roots, and the discriminant \(b^2 - 4ac\) tells you which before you solve.

Try changing the input

Increase \(c\) until the parabola lifts off the x-axis. The discriminant turns negative and the roots become complex, written \(p \pm qi\).

What this calculator does

  • Computes the discriminant and says what kind of roots to expect.
  • Solves with the quadratic formula, giving exact roots in simplest radical form (with \(i\) when complex) and decimals.
  • Gives the factored form when the roots are rational.
  • Reports the vertex, the sum and product of the roots, and checks them with Vieta's formulas.

The idea behind it

Every quadratic can be rewritten by completing the square as \(a(x - h)^2 + k\), where \((h, k)\) is the vertex. Setting it to 0 and taking a square root gives the quadratic formula. The square root of the discriminant decides everything:

  • \(b^2 - 4ac \gt 0\): two real roots, the parabola crosses the axis twice.
  • \(b^2 - 4ac = 0\): one repeated root, the vertex touches the axis.
  • \(b^2 - 4ac \lt 0\): no real roots; two complex conjugate roots.

Vieta's formulas

The roots always satisfy \(x_1 + x_2 = -\tfrac{b}{a}\) and \(x_1x_2 = \tfrac{c}{a}\): a quick check on any answer.

Deriving the quadratic formula

Divide \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) by \(a\) and move the constant: \(x^2 + \tfrac{b}{a}x = -\tfrac{c}{a}\). Add \(\left(\tfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2\) to both sides to complete the square:

\[ \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}. \]

Take the square root of both sides, \(x + \tfrac{b}{2a} = \pm\tfrac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), and subtract \(\tfrac{b}{2a}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

Solve \(x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0\).

Step 1: Discriminant

\[ \Delta = (-3)^2 - 4(1)(-10) = 9 + 40 = 49 \gt 0 \]

Two real roots, and since 49 is a perfect square they are rational.

Step 2: Quadratic formula

\[ x = \frac{3 \pm 7}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x_1 = -2, \quad x_2 = 5 \]

Step 3: Factored form

\[ (x + 2)(x - 5) = 0 \]

Check

Sum \(-2 + 5 = 3 = -\tfrac{b}{a}\); product \(-10 = \tfrac{c}{a}\). ✓

Second worked example: complex roots

Problem

Solve \(x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0\).

Step 1: Discriminant

\[ \Delta = 4 - 20 = -16 \lt 0 \]

Step 2: Square root of a negative number

\[ \sqrt{-16} = 4i \]

Step 3: Roots

\[ x = \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2i \]

Check

Sum \(-2\) and product \((-1)^2 + 2^2 = 5\), matching \(-\tfrac{b}{a}\) and \(\tfrac{c}{a}\). ✓ The parabola has its vertex at \((-1, 4)\), above the axis.

Practical applications

  • Projectiles: when a thrown object reaches a given height (see projectile motion).
  • Area problems: the dimensions of a plot with a given area and perimeter.
  • Electrical circuits: the characteristic equation of an RLC circuit; complex roots mean oscillation.
  • Optimisation: the vertex gives the maximum or minimum of a quadratic cost or profit.

Common mistakes

  1. Not setting the equation to 0 first. Read \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) only from \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\).
  2. Sign of \(-b\). If \(b = -3\), then \(-b = 3\).
  3. Squaring a negative \(b\). \((-3)^2 = 9\), not \(-9\).
  4. Dividing only part of the numerator by \(2a\). The whole \(-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}\) is divided.

Assumptions and limitations

  • \(a\) cannot be 0. Coefficients may be fractions or decimals; the working is exact.
  • Exact roots are in simplest radical form; decimals are rounded to 6 places.
  • For higher-degree polynomials, use the polynomial factoring calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the quadratic formula?

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a). It solves every quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0.

What does the discriminant tell me?

If b² − 4ac is positive there are two real roots, if 0 one repeated root, and if negative two complex roots.

When can I factor instead?

When the discriminant is a perfect square, the roots are rational and the quadratic factors over the integers or fractions.

What are complex roots?

Roots of the form p ± qi, which appear when the discriminant is negative. They always come in conjugate pairs for real coefficients.
