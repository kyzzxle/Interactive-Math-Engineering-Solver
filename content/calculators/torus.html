What the torus calculator does

A torus is the doughnut shape made by sweeping a circle of radius \(r\) (the tube) around a circle of radius \(R\) (from the centre of the hole to the centre of the tube).

Pappus’s centroid theorems give its size neatly: the volume is the tube’s cross-section area times the distance travelled by its centre, \(2\pi R\), and the surface area is the tube’s circumference times that same distance.

Try it

Double R in the explorer: the volume doubles. Double r: the volume becomes four times larger.

Formulas and variables

\[ V = (\pi r^2)(2\pi R) = 2\pi^2 R r^2 \]\[ A = (2\pi r)(2\pi R) = 4\pi^2 R r \]
SymbolMeaning
\(R\)Major radius: centre of the hole to the centre of the tube
\(r\)Minor radius: radius of the tube
\(R - r,\ R + r\)Inner and outer radii of the ring

Worked example

A doughnut with R = 6 cm and r = 2 cm

Step 1 — Volume

\[ V = 2\pi^2 (6)(2)^2 = 48\pi^2 \approx 473.74\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2 — Surface area

\[ A = 4\pi^2 (6)(2) = 48\pi^2 \approx 473.74\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Note

The numbers match only because r = 2 here; volume and area are different kinds of quantity.

Domain, special cases and common mistakes

  • Requires R ≥ r > 0 (a ring torus; R = r is a horn torus with no hole).
  • For r > R the tube crosses the axis (spindle torus) and the formulas count the overlap twice, so this case is rejected.

Common mistakes

  • Using the outer radius R + r as R.
  • Writing π instead of π²: the torus formulas contain π twice.
