What is a torus?

A torus is the doughnut shape made by sweeping a circle around an axis in its own plane that does not cross it. Inner tubes, O-rings, bagels and lifebuoys are tori.

Two radii describe it: the major radius \(R\), from the centre of the hole to the centre of the tube, and the minor radius \(r\), the radius of the tube itself. The hole has radius \(R - r\) and the outer edge has radius \(R + r\).

The geometry behind it

The key idea is the Theorem of Pappus: when a flat shape is swept around an axis, the volume is its area times the distance travelled by its centroid, and the surface is its perimeter times that same distance.

For a torus, the swept shape is a circle of radius \(r\) whose centre travels around a circle of radius \(R\), a distance of \(2\pi R\).

Straighten the doughnut

Cut the torus once and bend it straight: it becomes a cylinder of radius \(r\) and length \(2\pi R\). Its volume \(\pi r^2 \cdot 2\pi R\) and curved area \(2\pi r \cdot 2\pi R\) are exactly the torus formulas.

Deriving the formulas

Volume by washers

Slice horizontally. At height \(y\) the slice is a ring between radii \(R - \sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\) and \(R + \sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\), with area \(4\pi R\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\). Integrating,

\[ V = \int_{-r}^{r} 4\pi R\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\,dy = 4\pi R\cdot\frac{\pi r^2}{2} = 2\pi^2 R r^2, \]

because the integral of \(\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\) is the area of a half-circle.

From R and the hole

If you know the inner radius \(R_i\) and outer radius \(R_o\) instead, then \(R = \tfrac{R_o + R_i}{2}\) and \(r = \tfrac{R_o - R_i}{2}\).

Complete worked example

Problem

A torus has major radius 6 cm and tube radius 2 cm. Find its volume and surface area.

Step 1: Volume

\[ V = 2\pi^2(6)(2)^2 = 2\pi^2(24) = 48\pi^2 \approx 473.74\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 2: Surface area

\[ A = 4\pi^2(6)(2) = 48\pi^2 \approx 473.74\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Hole and outer size

Hole radius \(6 - 2 = 4\) cm; outer radius \(6 + 2 = 8\) cm, so the torus is 16 cm across.

Second worked example: from inner and outer diameters

Problem

A rubber O-ring has an inner diameter of 40 mm and an outer diameter of 50 mm. What volume of rubber does it contain?

Step 1: Radii

\[ R_i = 20,\ R_o = 25 \;\Rightarrow\; R = \frac{25 + 20}{2} = 22.5\ \text{mm},\quad r = \frac{25 - 20}{2} = 2.5\ \text{mm} \]

Step 2: Volume

\[ V = 2\pi^2(22.5)(2.5)^2 = 2\pi^2(140.625) \approx 2775.8\ \text{mm}^3 \approx 2.78\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Practical applications

  • Seals and O-rings. The material needed and how much an O-ring fills its groove.
  • Inner tubes and lifebuoys. Air volume and surface material.
  • Electrical engineering. Toroidal transformer cores and inductors.
  • Architecture and plumbing. Curved pipe bends are parts of tori.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the hole radius as R. \(R\) goes to the centre of the tube, not to its inner edge.
  2. Using diameters. Convert inner/outer diameters to \(R\) and \(r\) first.
  3. π instead of π². The torus formulas contain \(\pi^2\).

Assumptions and limitations

  • This is a ring torus with \(R \ge r\). At \(R = r\) it becomes a horn torus with no hole.
  • The tube's cross-section is a circle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the volume of a torus?

V = 2π²Rr², where R is the distance from the centre to the middle of the tube and r is the tube radius.

What is the surface area of a torus?

A = 4π²Rr.

What is the Theorem of Pappus?

Sweeping a flat shape around an axis, the volume equals its area times the distance its centroid travels, and the surface equals its perimeter times that distance.

How do I find R and r from the inner and outer radii?

R is their average and r is half their difference.
