What is a sphere?

A sphere is the set of all points in space that are the same distance from a fixed point, the centre. That distance is the radius \(r\), and twice it is the diameter \(d = 2r\). A ball bearing, a soap bubble, a planet (approximately) and the tank of an LPG storage sphere are all spheres.

Any plane through the centre cuts the sphere in a great circle of radius \(r\), the largest circle that can be drawn on its surface. Half a sphere, cut by such a plane, is a hemisphere.

What this calculator does

Enter any one of the radius, the diameter, the surface area or the volume. The calculator works back to the radius, then finds every other measurement:

  • the radius and the diameter,
  • the surface area \(A\) and the volume \(V\),
  • the circumference of a great circle.

When the radius or diameter is a whole number or a short decimal, the answers are also given in exact form as multiples of π, such as \(36\pi\), which is how most textbooks and board exams expect them.

The geometry behind it

A sphere has only one measurement that matters: its radius. Every other quantity is a power of \(r\) multiplied by a constant:

  • lengths (diameter, circumference) grow in proportion to \(r\),
  • the surface area grows with \(r^2\),
  • the volume grows with \(r^3\).

Archimedes, who considered it his greatest discovery, showed that a sphere fits exactly inside a cylinder of radius \(r\) and height \(2r\), and that the sphere has two thirds of the cylinder's volume and the same curved area as the cylinder's side.

Why the volume is one third of r times the area

Imagine the surface split into many tiny patches, each the base of a thin cone whose apex is the centre. Each cone has height \(r\), so its volume is \(\tfrac{1}{3}\times\text{base}\times r\). Adding them all gives \(V = \tfrac{1}{3}rA\). The calculator uses this as a check on every answer.

Deriving the formulas

Volume by slicing

Slice the sphere into thin horizontal discs. At height \(y\) above the centre the disc has radius \(\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\) (Pythagoras), so its area is \(\pi(r^2 - y^2)\). Adding the discs from \(y = -r\) to \(y = r\):

\[ V = \int_{-r}^{r} \pi\left(r^2 - y^2\right) dy = \pi\left[r^2 y - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{-r}^{r} = \pi\left(\frac{2r^3}{3} + \frac{2r^3}{3}\right) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3. \]

Surface area from the volume

Add a thin shell of thickness \(\Delta r\) to the sphere. The extra volume is about the surface area times the thickness, so the surface area is the rate of change of the volume:

\[ A = \frac{dV}{dr} = \frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right) = 4\pi r^2. \]

Working backwards

If the surface area is known, divide by \(4\pi\) and take the square root. If the volume is known, multiply by 3, divide by \(4\pi\) and take the cube root. These are the rearrangements in the table above.

Complete worked example

Problem

A ball has a radius of 3 cm. Find its surface area and volume, exactly and to two decimal places.

Step 1: List the given value

\(r = 3\) cm.

Step 2: Surface area

\[ A = 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi(3)^2 = 4\pi(9) = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^2 \]

Step 3: Volume

\[ V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi(27) = 36\pi \approx 113.10\ \text{cm}^3 \]

Step 4: Check

\[ \tfrac{1}{3}rA = \tfrac{1}{3}(3)(36\pi) = 36\pi \checkmark \]

Answer

\(A = 36\pi \approx 113.10\) cm² and \(V = 36\pi \approx 113.10\) cm³. At \(r = 3\) the two numbers happen to be equal, but their units are different.

Second worked example: radius from the volume

Problem

A spherical water tank must hold 50 m³. What diameter must it have, and how much steel plate covers its surface?

Step 1: Solve the volume formula for r

\[ r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3V}{4\pi}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3(50)}{4\pi}} = \sqrt[3]{11.9366} \approx 2.2854\ \text{m} \]

Step 2: Diameter

\[ d = 2r \approx 4.5708\ \text{m} \]

Step 3: Surface area

\[ A = 4\pi(2.2854)^2 \approx 4\pi(5.2231) \approx 65.64\ \text{m}^2 \]

Answer

The tank needs a diameter of about 4.57 m and roughly 65.6 m² of plate, before allowing for seams and waste.

Practical applications

  • Storage tanks. Spheres hold the most volume for the least surface, so pressurised gas tanks are spherical: less steel and fewer weak points.
  • Sports and manufacturing. The volume of a ball gives the material needed; the surface area gives the leather, rubber or paint.
  • Science. Drops, bubbles, cells and planets are modelled as spheres to estimate mass (volume × density) and heat loss (through the surface).
  • Domes. A hemispherical dome has curved area \(2\pi r^2\), which estimates roofing material.

Common mistakes

  1. Using the diameter as the radius. Halve the diameter first. Using \(d\) instead of \(r\) makes the area 4 times and the volume 8 times too large.
  2. Mixing up r² and r³. Area uses \(r^2\) (square units); volume uses \(r^3\) (cubic units).
  3. Forgetting the 4/3. \(V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\), not \(\pi r^3\) or \(\tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^3\).
  4. Hemisphere surface. A solid hemisphere has a curved part \(2\pi r^2\) and a flat circle \(\pi r^2\): \(3\pi r^2\) in total.
  5. Rounding π too early. Keep π until the last step, or use the calculator's exact form.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The solid is a perfect sphere. For a stretched or flattened sphere, use the ellipsoid calculator.
  • The inputs must be positive. All lengths should be in the same unit.
  • For part of a sphere, use the spherical cap and segment or sector and wedge calculators.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the volume of a sphere?

V = (4/3)πr³, where r is the radius. With the diameter d it is V = πd³/6.

How do I find the radius if I know the volume?

Multiply the volume by 3, divide by 4π, and take the cube root: r = ∛(3V/4π). Choose "Volume" in the calculator and it does this for you.

Why is the surface area 4πr²?

Archimedes showed the sphere has the same area as the curved side of the cylinder that just contains it: 2πr wide and 2r tall, which is 4πr². It is also exactly four great circles.

What happens to the volume if I double the radius?

The volume becomes 2³ = 8 times larger and the surface area 2² = 4 times larger.

How many litres does a sphere hold?

Work in centimetres and divide the volume in cm³ by 1000, or work in metres and multiply the volume in m³ by 1000.

What is the volume of a hemisphere?

Half the sphere: (2/3)πr³. Its total surface (curved part plus flat base) is 3πr².
