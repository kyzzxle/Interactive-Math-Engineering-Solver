Confidence intervals

A sample gives only an estimate of a population value. A confidence interval wraps that estimate in a margin of error: a range of plausible values for the true population mean or proportion, at a chosen confidence level such as 95%. This calculator builds the three most common intervals from summary statistics and shows the critical value, standard error and margin of error.

Try changing the input

Raise the confidence level from 90% to 99%: the interval widens. Quadruple the sample size: the margin of error halves, because it shrinks like \(1/\sqrt{n}\).

Supported intervals and assumptions

IntervalFormulaUse when
Mean, \(\sigma\) known\(\bar{x} \pm z^{*}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)The population standard deviation is known; random sample; population normal or \(n\) large.
Mean, \(\sigma\) unknown\(\bar{x} \pm t^{*}_{n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\)The usual case: use the sample standard deviation \(s\) and the t-distribution.
Proportion\(\hat{p} \pm z^{*}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}\)At least 10 successes and 10 failures in a random sample.

The critical value cuts off \(\frac{\alpha}{2}\) in each tail, where \(\alpha = 1 - \text{confidence level}\). For 95%, \(z^{*} \approx 1.960\).

Worked example

\(\bar{x} = 52.3\), \(s = 6.1\), \(n = 15\), 95% confidence

Step 1 — Standard error

\[ SE = \frac{6.1}{\sqrt{15}} \approx 1.5750 \]

Step 2 — Critical value (14 degrees of freedom)

\[ t^{*}_{14} \approx 2.1448 \]

Step 3 — Interval

\[ 52.3 \pm 2.1448 \times 1.5750 = 52.3 \pm 3.378 \;\Rightarrow\; (48.92,\ 55.68) \]

Interpretation, domain and rounding

  • "95% confidence" describes the method: about 95% of intervals built this way from repeated samples contain the true value. It is not a 95% probability that this particular interval does.
  • The data must come from a random sample. A t-interval needs \(n \ge 2\); proportion intervals are refused when there are fewer than 10 successes or failures, because the normal approximation then fails.
  • Critical values are computed directly (not read from a table); results are rounded to 6 decimal places.
